# Battle for Taiz: the siege and the fight for the roads, 17–30 October 2026

Last updated Friday, 30 October 2026, morning, Israel time (UTC+2), from the Yemen Desk editions of 16–30 October plus fresh checks of Critical Threats, AP and Asharq Al-Awsat. The first version covered 17–24 October; the new section below covers 25–30 October. Ask Scout to refresh it.

**How to read the labels:** *confirmed* means a named, independent source (a UN agency, a wire service, or an analyst with geolocated footage) reports it as fact. *Claim* means one party to the war says it and no independent source has checked it. *Unconfirmed* means only a local or partisan outlet reports it. *Inference* is my own reading, not a reported fact.

---

## What changed since 24 October (25–30 October)

**Short version:** The siege has not been broken. Government forces made their first independently confirmed gain, in hills west of the city, but the Houthis are still on the main southwestern road, the governor now says the city is in famine, and the Houthi leader denies there is a siege at all.

- **First confirmed government gain, west of the city (27 October).** Government military sources told [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/arab-world/5334405-yemeni-govt-forces-press-advance-houthis-near-bab-al-mandeb-taiz) that troops took Mount al-Midan (also spelled al-Maydan) above the al-Dhabab valley with air support and can now fire on stretches of the al-Dhabab road. AFP photographed government fighters around Mount al-Midan on 26 October (same article). Critical Threats said on 26 October that it could not confirm the hill, then reported on 27 October that geolocated government footage does show government forces in previously Houthi-held mountains west of the city, in Sabr al-Mawadim district. *Confirmed* that they gained some ground; *claim* that they control the road by fire. (I could not reopen the 27 October Critical Threats page; this rests on the Yemen Desk's reading of it.)
- **The Houthis are still at the Hangar Checkpoint (28 October).** Houthi footage that [Critical Threats geolocated](https://www.criticalthreats.org/analysis/iran-update-october-28-2026) shows Houthi fighters operating at the Hangar Checkpoint, on the main road into the city from the southwest, and firing an anti-tank missile at a government tank from mountains southwest of the city. That contradicts anti-Houthi claims on 23–25 October that the checkpoint had been retaken. *Confirmed.* The road to Aden is still cut.
- **Al-Misrakh: fighting, no confirmed change.** The government reported fighting at al-Aqroud and Kahlan (al-Misrakh district) and Hamma and al-Shaqab (Sabr al-Mawadim) on 25–29 October and says it repelled Houthi attacks. Critical Threats (29 October) says the Houthis "have repeatedly failed to consolidate control" of these areas, but no source names new ground taken there. The 23 October report of government forces at **Jabal Manara** has still not been confirmed by any independent source, so it stays *unconfirmed*. On 25 October Critical Threats showed government drones bombing Houthi forces in al-Misrakh, which means the Houthis still hold ground there.
- **Toxic-shell allegation (28 October).** The government's Taiz media office said Houthi shells fired near Kahlan and al-Aqroud may have held "internationally banned toxic substances" and asked the UN for testing. [Critical Threats](https://www.criticalthreats.org/analysis/iran-update-october-29-2026) notes the government gave no evidence and no local source showed proof. *Claim, unconfirmed.*
- **Shelling and the city's own figures.** Minister Eshraq al-Maqtari said more than 2,000 shells and rockets hit the city in recent days and that it is surrounded on all four sides; the governor said Houthis seized the wells in Wadi al-Dhubab that supply about 3 million liters of water. AP could not verify either ([AP via Newser, 28 October](https://www.newser.com/article/c032db4b09725fea0f6320eec49de5d4/houthis-say-saudi-led-airstrikes-killed-14-at-a-market-in-northern-yemen-and-other-mideast-news.html)). Taiz authorities said Houthi snipers killed five civilians in Hamma and al-Damna on 27 October, and an ambulance was hit near al-Awat on 28 October ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/arab-world/5334405-yemeni-govt-forces-press-advance-houthis-near-bab-al-mandeb-taiz)). *Claims by the government.*
- **Famine dispute (29 October).** Governor Nabil Shamsan said: "The city is in famine, not on the brink of it." He said the last outside water source is lost, cooking gas is cut off entirely, and 271 people have been killed and 1,129 wounded since the offensive began. The World Food Program said it cannot reach more than 600,000 people in Taiz who depend on aid and that wheat flour is nearly gone. Human Rights Watch said the siege could amount to the war crime of starving civilians. Houthi leader Abdul-Malik al-Houthi called the siege "Saudi-fabricated" and said "the way is open" for food ([AP via The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/taiz-houthi-yemen-cairo-world-food-program-b3075955.html)). **No formal famine declaration has been made.** Famine is a technical finding by the IPC, the UN-backed food-crisis scale, and none has been issued for Taiz. The governor's casualty count is his own and much higher than the UN's verified count below.
- **Coalition strikes.** A Saudi strike on homes of the al-Qouz family in al-Barah, Maqbana district (Houthi-held, western Taiz), killed at least 13, according to the Houthi-run health ministry as reported by AP; AFP had 12 dead ([New Straits Times/AFP](https://www.nst.com.my/world/world/2026/10/1566467/houthis-report-12-killed-saudi-strike-yemen-homes); [The Independent/AP](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/taiz-houthi-yemen-cairo-world-food-program-b3075955.html)). Saudi Arabia has not commented. Earlier, strikes hit Houthi barracks in al-Turbah (25–26 October, Al Arabiya, via the Yemen Desk) and the abandoned Sofitel hotel hill the Houthis use for artillery ([Critical Threats, 24 October](https://www.criticalthreats.org/analysis/iran-update-october-24-2026-6add17463a4cf)).
- **Mokha and Dhubab: a sea-supply war.** Coalition aircraft struck Houthi positions and the port at Mokha on 28–29 October; government-aligned media say two Houthi boats carrying weapons from Hudaydah were destroyed on 28 October. Critical Threats reads this as an effort to cut the Houthis' sea route to the Bab al-Mandab front and notes the government holds no port of its own on that coast ([Critical Threats, 29 October](https://www.criticalthreats.org/analysis/iran-update-october-29-2026)). This settles the Mokha question from last week: Mokha is Houthi-held. Pro-government forces say they still hold Dhubab airport and the Kahbub mountains; that is their own *claim*.
- **No road has opened.** The relief road officials said on 24 October might open "in the coming days" has not opened by 30 October, as far as any source I checked shows.
- **Displacement.** The UN migration agency counts about 124,000 people displaced into Taiz since August (AP); the Yemen Desk also cites a UN aid office figure of 281,000 displaced across the wider area, which I could not reopen.

---

## Bottom line

Taiz is under a Houthi siege: the UN says more than 800,000 people are cut off from aid, and by most accounts the Houthis have held the main road south to Aden since early October. The government still holds the city center. As of 30 October its only independently confirmed gain is ground in the hills west of the city (27 October); the Houthis still hold the Hangar Checkpoint and the road to Aden, and no road has reopened. The governor now says the city is in famine. The army spokesman's daily strike and casualty figures are unverified and contradict each other, while government commanders and the foreign minister concede that the siege is tightening and that a counteroffensive is still being prepared.

## The shape of the battle

**A siege, not a front line.** The fight for Taiz is not one line that moves back and forth. It is a contest over the roads and high ground around a city of more than a million people. Reuters reported on 16 October that the Houthis had encircled Taiz by cutting the main road to Aden ([Reuters via Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/10/yemens-besieged-taiz-families-take-shelter-houthis-close)). Sources disagree on when this happened. The Yemen Desk and Critical Threats (23 October) say 12 October, Critical Threats (19 October) says 6 October, and the UN's "20 days and counting" on 23 October implies about 3 October. I would write "early October" until that is settled. A former prime minister, Maeen Abdelmalik Saeed, told [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/10/17/battle-for-taiz-is-for-whole-of-western-yemen-warns-ex-pm-maeen-abdelmalik-saeed/) on 17 October that the Houthis were taking the countryside and roads first and had not yet formed a full blockade. He expected one within days or weeks. In practice the week confirmed his warning.

**The Houthis are working from the west and south.** Their effort this week centered on three places. Jabal Han, the mountain west of the city, is contested: the Houthis claim all of it, but Critical Threats found no holder of the summit, and a Yemeni journalist told it that neither side controls it ([Critical Threats, 20 October](https://www.criticalthreats.org/analysis/iran-update-october-20-2026)). Al-Shaqab, at the city's southern entrance, drew repeated Houthi assaults on 17 and 19–20 October that the government says it repelled. And on 22 October Critical Threats assessed that the Houthis "almost certainly" captured a segment of the Taiz–Aden road in the al-Dhabab valley, with the Hangar Checkpoint on the southwestern edge "likely" taken as well ([Critical Threats, 22 October](https://www.criticalthreats.org/analysis/iran-update-october-22-2026)). The Houthis also blew up a bridge on the Hayjat al-Abd road, the secondary route toward Lahj, around 18–19 October. Asharq Al-Awsat and the Public Works ministry report it, but nobody independent has checked the damage. Inside the city, the Old Airport neighborhood on the western edge was attacked on 17, 18 and 22 October, and the government says it turned back every attempt. That is the closest fighting to the center.

**The government's answer is in the south.** The government's main effort is the district of al-Misrakh, south of the city, because whoever holds it controls the southern supply route. On 21 October Critical Threats showed geolocated footage of government-aligned forces on ground the Houthis had held there. On the evening of 22 October the 35th Armored Brigade and Popular Resistance forces attacked Houthi positions at Jabal Manara, Kour, Wadi Kour and other sites on the al-Aqroud front. The brigade says more than 35 Houthi fighters were killed or wounded, but it did not say it captured any ground ([Anadolu, 23 October](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/yemeni-army-says-over-35-houthi-fighters-killed-or-wounded-in-taiz/4098647)). Critical Threats says government-aligned forces reached Jabal Manara. A local outlet says it was "recaptured," citing unnamed sources, so I treat the recapture as **unconfirmed**. The attack was costly: the brigade announced the death of its training chief, Col. Mahfouz al-Jaradi, along with another officer and unnamed soldiers.

## What the two sides say versus what is known

**The army's numbers do not hold together.** The army spokesman, Col. Majed al-Nuzaili, reports a new total almost every day, and the reports are only the army's own claims. Between 18 and 23 October, the number of Houthis he says were "neutralized" (killed or wounded) in 24 hours ranged from about 285 to about 800, and the number of strikes from 780 to 1,417 ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/middle-east/yemens-military-claims-1326-strikes-on-houthis-about-370-fighters-neutralized-3007034); [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/arab-world/5331653-yemeni-forces-intensify-strikes-against-houthis)). Al Jazeera said explicitly that it could not verify the Friday figure. The Houthis make their own claims, such as 110 and then 124 coalition strikes in a day, which are not verified either. Houthi officials also told AP, without giving names, that they hold about 80 percent of the countryside around Taiz and several neighborhoods inside the city. AP could not check it.

**Government commanders speak more plainly than the spokesman.** Brig. Gen. Mohamed al-Mahmoudi, the deputy interior minister, who commands forces on Jabal Sabir, told The New Arab on 21 October that the countryside positions "collapsed rapidly," that the mountain had not been prepared as a front line, and that a counteroffensive is being prepared with al-Misrakh as the key. He said that "if Taiz falls it means the fall of the Yemeni republic and the legitimate government." The foreign minister acknowledged the Houthis' tightening grip to AP on 24 October. **My inference:** the two pictures can both be partly true. The army may be inflicting losses every day without having changed who controls the roads.

**Two points where sources conflict.** *Update, 29 October:* Critical Threats again describes Mokha as Houthi-held and the coalition as bombing its port, so I treat The National's earlier report of a government capture as wrong. The Yemen Desk's earlier text called the Hayjat al-Abd damage a culvert, but the sources I opened say bridge, so the Desk's wording was wrong.

## The human cost

The UN figures are the most reliable in this story. The UN human rights office verified 32 civilians killed and 115 injured in Taiz governorate from 1 August to 19 October. Another OCHA figure, 251 civilian casualties including 57 killed, may cover a wider area, so I do not combine the two. OCHA counts almost 260,000 people displaced since August, about half of them in Taiz. On 23 October OCHA's Jens Laerke said it was "20 days and counting" since Taiz was cut off, that the cost of a water truck has tripled, and that more than 800,000 people are cut off from aid ([Al Jazeera/AFP, 23 October](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/23/time-running-out-to-reach-yemens-taiz-amid-deepening-crisis-un-says)). The WHO says 42 health facilities in Taiz have acute shortages, and hospitals need at least 156,000 liters of fuel a month. On 24 October Al Jazeera reported that the Republican Hospital's dialysis center may close within days for lack of diesel ([Al Jazeera, 24 October](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/10/24/hospital-yemen-taiz-warns-collapse-houthi-siege)), and AP reported that drinking water has reached crisis levels ([AP via NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/24/g-s1-149729/drinking-water-supplies-dwindle-in-yemeni-city-besieged-by-houthi-rebels)). Officials told [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/arab-world/5332995-yemeni-int%E2%80%99l-efforts-underway-open-route-besieged-taiz-within-days) on 24 October that a relief road through one of the city's entrances may open "in the coming days." That is a plan, not an event.

## Where this is heading

**The siege tightened this week and was not broken.** The road capture at al-Dhabab, the loss of the Hayjat al-Abd bridge, and the UN's warnings all point the same way, and named sources confirm them.

**The government moved from defense to local counterattacks at the end of the week.** The 22 October operation at al-Aqroud and Jabal Manara is the clearest sign. **Inference:** it may be the start of the counteroffensive al-Mahmoudi described, but so far it is small, about a brigade-level raid. Critical Threats also warned that breaking the encirclement will not help unless the government captures and holds large stretches of the Taiz–Aden road. So far no source reports that the road is open.

**The Houthis keep reinforcing.** Reports say hundreds of fighters arrived from Sanaa, Dhamar and Ibb on 19 October, which is unverified. AP quotes analysts who say taking the city would require a long and costly battle. **Inference:** the most likely near-term result is a long contest on the southern and western edges, with the humanitarian situation shaping events more than the front line does.

### What to watch next week (revised 30 October)
- Whether government forces turn the Mount al-Midan foothold into control of the al-Dhabab road, and who holds the Hangar Checkpoint.
- Whether the UN, WFP or the IPC respond to the governor's famine statement, and whether any evidence backs the toxic-shell claim.
- Whether the relief road opens, who escorts the convoys, and whether the Houthis allow them through.
- Whether the 35th Armored Brigade or another unit names captured ground in al-Misrakh, and whether Critical Threats or geolocated footage confirms it.
- Any report that the government has retaken the al-Dhabab road segment. That would be the strongest sign that the siege is loosening.
- Whether Jabal Han or the Old Airport neighborhood changes hands, since both are close to the city center.
- Whether the dialysis center closes, whether fuel arrives, and whether the UN reports any access.
- Whether the army's daily figures keep swinging, and whether the UN updates its verified civilian casualty count.
- Whether Houthi missile fire on Aden and Saudi Arabia keeps pace, which shows whether the Taiz fight is widening.
