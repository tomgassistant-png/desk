# Battle for Taiz: weekly update, 17–24 October 2026

Last updated Saturday, 24 October 2026, 10:50 Israel time (UTC+3), from the Yemen Desk editions of 16–24 October plus fresh web checks. Ask Scout to refresh it.

**How to read the labels:**
- **Confirmed** means a named, independent source (UN agency, wire service, analyst with geolocated footage) reports it as fact.
- **Claim** means one party to the war says it, and no independent source has verified it.
- **Unconfirmed** means reported by a local or partisan outlet only.
- **Inference** means my own reading, not a reported fact.

---

## Bottom line

Taiz is under a Houthi siege: the UN says more than 800,000 people are cut off from aid, and by most accounts the Houthis have held the main road south to Aden since early October. The government still holds the city center, and its only reported gains this week came on the southern edge, in al-Misrakh and around Jabal Manara, where no independent source has confirmed that any road has reopened. The army spokesman's daily strike and casualty figures are unverified and contradict each other, while government commanders and the foreign minister concede that the siege is tightening and that a counteroffensive is still being prepared.

## State of the front lines

### South: al-Misrakh, al-Aqroud, Jabal Manara
- **Status: contested; the government is on the offensive here, and this is its main effort.**
- Government forces announced on 11 Oct that they retook the al-Misrakh district center (Daily Beirut/Asharq; excerpt only). On 21 Oct, geolocated footage showed government-aligned forces inside previously Houthi-held ground in al-Misrakh (Critical Threats).
- On 22 Oct, forces attacked Houthi positions at Jabal Manara, Kour, Wadi Kour, al-Maqda, al-Dabaya and Kahlan. The 35th Armored Brigade claims more than 35 Houthi fighters killed or wounded. It did not claim a capture. Critical Threats says government-aligned forces reached Jabal Manara. A local outlet says it was "recaptured." **Treat the recapture as unconfirmed.**
- The brigade lost its training chief and at least one other officer at al-Aqroud, so the fighting is costly for the government side too.
- A pro-Houthi field account (Hamerintel, not opened and undated) claims "complete control" of al-Misrakh. That conflicts with the reporting above. I did not use it.

### West: Jabal Han, al-Dhabab, al-Shaqab
- **Jabal Han:** Contested, with no clear holder. The Houthis claim it (20 Oct, via Houthi-run Saba as seen in a search summary). Critical Threats found Houthi footage geolocated on the mountain on 22 Oct, but the government has repeatedly retaken parts of it. A Yemeni journalist said neither side holds the summit (20 Oct).
- **Al-Dhabab valley:** Critical Threats assesses that the Houthis "almost certainly" hold a Taiz–Aden road segment in the valley, which effectively secures the road between al-Turbah and the city's outskirts. The government regained Dhubab airport on 15 Oct (Critical Threats, 21 Oct). Fighting continued near Murad, south of the airport.
- **Al-Shaqab (southern entrance to the city):** Houthi assaults on 17 Oct and again on 19–20 Oct were reported repelled. No ground change was reported.
- **Old Airport neighborhood (inside the city):** The Houthis tried to enter it on 17, 18 and 22 Oct, per Critical Threats. Government forces report repelling every attempt. This is the closest fighting to the center.

### Jabal Habashi
- The Houthis said on or about 12 Oct that they had secured Jabal Habashi District, the Bireen triangle and the Yafrus junction (Saba, via a search summary). Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree separately claimed the highest peak. Government air strikes hit a weapons depot there on 19 Oct (Al Jazeera, 20 Oct) and again around 23 Oct (desk).
- General al-Mahmoudi said Houthi forces advanced into parts of Jabal Habashi (see below). **Houthi control is a claim backed by a senior government commander's partial admission. I found no independent map.**

### Hayjat al-Abd supply road (Taiz–Lahj)
- The Houthis blew up a bridge on this road around 18–19 Oct (Asharq Al-Awsat; Public Works ministry via Yemen TV). The desk's earlier text called it a culvert; the sources I opened say "bridge." One local outlet cited by the desk (Al-Masdar Online) said a road had reopened after an earlier blast. I could not verify that.
- Al Jazeera also reported a crossing on the Taiz–Lahj road destroyed on 22 Oct (desk, not independently opened).
- Effect: Taiz has lost its main road and this secondary route. Aid access depends on a new corridor that officials say is "within days" but has not been announced as open.

### Lahj and Bab al-Mandab (context)
- Government forces say they are clearing Houthi pockets at Bab al-Mandab, with fighting toward the Kahbub mountains and in Waziyah. The Giants Brigades said they turned back a large Houthi attack at Kahbub (Asharq, 20 Oct; the Southern Giants said so again on 23 Oct, per the desk).
- Critical Threats reports the Giants and Homeland Shield reestablished control of the al-Aradi camp near Bab al-Mandab (22 Oct).
- **Mokha:** Critical Threats (23 Oct) and AP (24 Oct) say the Houthis took Mokha and the Red Sea coast in September (CT gives 10 Sep). The National's 17 Oct article says "a government-led offensive has seized the centre of Mokha" with bombardment from surrounding hills still going on. That sentence is the newspaper's own text, and it conflicts with the other sources. **Status of Mokha is unresolved.** A local outlet reported renewed government air strikes on Houthi positions in Mokha (Almahriah, 24 Oct).

The numbers swing from 800 to 200 to 620 within five days, and Arab News itself puts quotation marks around "neutralized." Asharq (19 Oct) repeats an army estimate of more than 15,000 Houthi dead and wounded "in recent weeks," also a claim.

### Houthi claims (desk-reported, not independently opened)
- Spokesman Yahya Saree: 110 coalition strikes in 24 hours (19 Oct) and 124 Saudi strikes in 24 hours across seven provinces (23 Oct).
- Houthi media said a strike in Nihm district killed 3 and injured 9 (Xinhua, via the desk).
- Houthi officials told AP, anonymously, that they hold about 80% of the countryside around Taiz and several neighborhoods inside the city. AP could not verify it, and the AP piece also says the government holds the center.

### Figures from UN agencies (the most reliable)
- Civilian casualties, Taiz governorate, 1 Aug–19 Oct: 32 killed (including 14 children and 9 women) and 115 injured (OHCHR; Al Jazeera, 20 Oct). A later OCHA report cites 251 verified civilian casualties, 57 killed and 194 injured, with half of the dead children. I could not confirm whether that second figure covers Taiz alone or the wider conflict, so do not combine the two.
- Displacement: almost 260,000 since August, about half in Taiz (OCHA, 22 Oct). More than 174,000 displaced people reached with aid.
- Siege impact: more than 800,000 cut off from aid; at least 200,000 are women and children; 52,000+ children with acute malnutrition, about 11,500 severe (Al Jazeera/AFP, 23 Oct). WHO: 42 Taiz health facilities with acute shortages; hospitals need at least 156,000 liters of fuel a month (Al Jazeera, 24 Oct).
- Conflict-wide since August: 1,574 killed and 6,939 wounded; more than 3,800 fled to Djibouti (Al Jazeera/AFP, 23 Oct; scope as stated there).
- Government losses named this week: Col. Mahfouz al-Jaradi, Capt. Ma'moun al-Jaradi and unnamed soldiers (35th Armored Brigade). No overall government casualty figure has been published.

## What commanders admit versus what the army spokesman claims

**Admitted by government figures:**
- Brig. Gen. Mohamed al-Mahmoudi (deputy interior minister, commander on Jabal Sabir), The New Arab, 21 Oct: the countryside positions "collapsed rapidly"; the city faces a deepening crisis with about 2 million people at risk; Jabal Sabir had not been prepared as a front line; since 18 Oct forces have reorganized and reinforced the mountain; a counteroffensive is being prepared, the battle for al-Misrakh is key, and the immediate priority is consolidating the city's defenses. Quote: "If Taiz falls it means the fall of the Yemeni republic and the legitimate government." The desk also attributes to him the loss of al-Kadha, Shamir and Maqbana and Houthi advances into Jabal Habashi and parts of al-Hujariyah. I saw the interview only as a search excerpt plus the desk summary, so the latter details are desk-sourced.
- Foreign Minister Afrah al-Zouba acknowledged the Houthis' tightening grip (AP, 24 Oct).
- A government committee head (Nayef al-Bakri) told Asharq Al-Awsat on 24 Oct that a relief road is still being arranged and talks with the Red Cross are still underway. That implies the city's supply lines remain shut.
- The government also says the Houthi siege threatens more than 4 million people in the governorate (Al Jazeera, 20 Oct).

**Claimed by the army spokesman:**
- Daily totals in the hundreds of "neutralized" Houthis, "seized positions" (often without naming them), advances at al-Aqroud, and a destroyed command center at the Sofitel hotel.
- None of the spokesman's casualty figures has been independently confirmed, and Al Jazeera explicitly said it could not verify the Friday figure.

**The gap (inference):** Commanders describe a defensive, regrouping force that has not yet launched its main counterattack. The spokesman describes a force that is winning every day. Both can be partly true if the army is inflicting losses but has not changed who controls the roads.

## Assessment of the trend

1. **The siege tightened this week and was not broken.** The Houthis hold the main road south, and Critical Threats assesses they captured another segment at al-Dhabab on 22 Oct. The UN's "20 days and counting" on 23 Oct, a fuel and water shortage, and a hospital warning of collapse all point the same way. These are confirmed by named sources.
2. **The government shifted from defense to local counterattacks at the end of the week.** The 22 Oct operation at al-Aqroud and Jabal Manara is the clearest example. Critical Threats also notes that the Houthis have been pushed back from the Old Airport neighborhood three times. **Inference:** this is the opening of the counteroffensive General al-Mahmoudi described, but its scale is small so far (a brigade-level raid with 35+ claimed casualties).
3. **Critical Threats' own warning matters:** a breach of the encirclement will not help unless the government captures and holds significant segments of the Taiz–Aden road (20 Oct). So far no source reports that the road is open.
4. **Jabal Han and Mokha are open questions,** and the sources disagree on both.
5. **The Houthis keep reinforcing** (hundreds of fighters per report, from Ibb, Dhamar, Sanaa and other provinces, unverified), and AP quotes analysts saying taking the city would need a prolonged, costly battle. **Inference:** the most likely near-term outcome is a long contest on the southern and western edges, with the humanitarian situation driving events more than the front line.

**Where sources disagree on a key date:** The desk and Critical Threats (23 Oct) say the Houthis encircled Taiz on 12 Oct. Critical Threats (19 Oct) says 6 Oct. Al Jazeera says "early October" or "first week of October," and OCHA's "20 days" on 23 Oct implies about 3 Oct. Reuters said the city was already encircled on 16 Oct. The National quoted Saeed on 17 Oct saying a complete blockade had not yet formed. **Read it as "early October" until the discrepancy is resolved.**

## What to watch next week

- **Relief corridor:** whether the road Asharq describes opens "in the coming days," who escorts the convoy, and whether the Houthis allow it.
- **Al-Misrakh and Jabal Manara:** whether the 35th Armored Brigade or other units announce captured ground by name, and whether geolocated footage or Critical Threats confirms it. Compare the Houthi response; so far there is none.
- **The al-Dhabab road segment:** any report that the government has retaken it. That would be the strongest sign the siege is loosening.
- **Jabal Han and the Old Airport neighborhood:** whether either changes hands, since these are closest to the city center.
- **Hospitals and water:** dialysis center shutdown, fuel deliveries, and whether UN agencies report any access.
- **Casualty claims:** whether the army spokesman's numbers continue to swing widely, and whether the UN updates verified civilian casualties for Taiz.
- **Saudi interceptions and Houthi missile fire** at Aden and Saudi territory (Critical Threats lists interceptions over Najran, Riyadh and Khamis Mushait on 22–23 Oct). These show whether the Taiz fight is widening.
- **Mokha:** an independent statement on who controls the center.
