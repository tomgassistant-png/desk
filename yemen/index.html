Sunday, 18 October 2026

Egypt moved toward joining Saudi Arabia's defense, and the UAE is reported to be paying anti-Houthi forces again, as Houthi missiles and drones kept coming at Riyadh, the Eastern Province and the south overnight. By evening Egypt's parliament had put off its vote to Monday, Saudi Arabia confirmed the loss of a helicopter in Najran but blamed a malfunction, and Riyadh airport reopened.

NewEgypt's parliament meets today on defending Saudi Arabia

At a military academy graduation on Saturday, 17 October, President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi said Egypt would take any measure needed to protect Saudi Arabia if King Salman and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman ask, and asked parliament for an emergency session to authorize it. He said Egypt would act only in defense. Parliament's secretariat scheduled a plenary session for 1 p.m. Cairo time (1 p.m. in Israel) on Sunday, 18 October, without publishing the agenda. Reuters, Al Jazeera, AFP via Arab News, The National and Egypt's State Information Service all report it. Saudi Arabia has made no public request. Egypt was part of the 2015 coalition; its stake now is Red Sea traffic to the Suez Canal, which runs past the Bab al-Mandab strait the Houthis took in September.

The National; Reuters; Al Jazeera; Egypt State Information Service

NewUAE reported to resume paying the Giants Brigades

The Guardian reported on Saturday, citing sources in Yemen, that the UAE has agreed to resume funding southern forces, mainly the Giants Brigades it once trained and paid, in return for Saudi air support. Newsmax and Dunya News carry the report. The UAE left Yemen and stopped the payments in January after its dispute with Riyadh over the south. Neither government has confirmed the deal, so it is unconfirmed. If true, it would put money back behind the most effective ground force in the counteroffensive at a moment when the Taiz and Lahj fronts are stalled.

The Guardian via Newsmax; Dunya News

NewNew overnight barrage on Riyadh, the Eastern Province and Khamis Mushait

Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki said early on Sunday, 18 October, that the coalition intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Khamis Mushait and three drones, two over Riyadh region and one over the Eastern Province. Civil Defense sent alerts in Riyadh, Abha and Khamis Mushait before announcing the danger had passed. On Saturday the coalition had reported four drones downed over Riyadh, the Eastern Province and the south. Saudi Gazette and Anadolu report the statements; no damage or casualties were reported. The figures come from the coalition only.

Saudi Gazette; Anadolu

NewHouthis claim a Saudi Apache shot down near Najran

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said on Saturday that Houthi forces shot down a Saudi AH-64 Apache helicopter near Najran while it attacked their positions on the border. Video of a smoking aircraft is circulating on X, but it has not been verified, and Saudi officials have not commented. Anadolu reports the claim. It is unconfirmed. If true, it would be the first reported loss of a Saudi aircraft in this round of fighting and would point to cross-border ground and air combat on the Najran front.

Anadolu; Saba (Houthi)

NewTaiz: government lines hold at Jabal Han, but no breakthrough

Government forces said they repelled a Houthi assault west of Taiz city that lasted about 12 hours around Jabal Han, with fighting also at the old airport area, Ghurab and Bir Basha, and on the al-Dhabab, al-Aqrud and al-Shaqab fronts. Critical Threats, a US research project, reads the same reports as showing government forces holding the high ground west of the city that the Houthis have been trying to take since they encircled Taiz on 12 October. It also notes that the Giants Brigades and Homeland Shield Forces have made no significant gains in Waziyah and Madaribah, which protect the recently retaken Dhubab airport. The army claims 1,417 strikes and about 800 Houthis killed or wounded in 24 hours, and more than 17,000 strikes since 5 October; those figures are its own and unverified.

Asharq Al-Awsat; Critical Threats; Arab News

NewFormer prime minister warns Taiz decides the whole west

Maeen Abdelmalik Saeed, prime minister from 2018 to 2024 and now a presidential adviser, told The National that the Houthis are taking the rural outskirts and roads around Taiz before closing a full siege, and that losing the city would open the way to Aden, Marib and Lahj, home of one of Yemen's largest military camps. He said the government offensive holds the center of Mokha, but Houthi forces in the hills around it still shell it. This is his assessment, not an independent finding.

The National

NewWSJ: Iranian officers directing Houthi strikes

The Wall Street Journal reported on 16 October that Revolutionary Guard officers in Yemen helped the Houthis place missiles and radar near the Red Sea and Bab al-Mandab, gave targeting intelligence for attacks on Saudi Arabia, and that some were on the front lines directing drone and missile attacks. Iran International and Saudi outlet Ajel carry the report. Iran denies encouraging the attacks; the Journal's sourcing could not be checked independently. Yemen's foreign minister has rejected Iran's offer to mediate.

Wall Street Journal; Iran International

NewRiyadh airport: no reopening announced

The notice closing King Khalid International Airport expired at 2100 GMT on Saturday (midnight in Riyadh and in Israel). As of early Sunday, no reopening announcement from the Saudi civil aviation authority could be found in the reports checked; AGBI had said before the deadline that the notice could easily be extended, and Asiatoday reports that no date has been given. That status is unconfirmed until Saudi authorities speak. Airlines are still staying away: Air France-KLM until the end of October and IndiGo and Akasa until 24 October.

AGBI; Asiatoday; Reuters via Al-Monitor

NewEvening: Egypt's defense council pledges support to Saudi Arabia; parliament puts off its vote to Monday

Egypt's National Defence Council, chaired by President el-Sisi, pledged on Sunday 'all necessary and possible forms of support' to Saudi Arabia and said the kingdom's security is linked to Egypt's own. The statement did not mention troops, but MP Mostafa Bakry, who is close to the state, wrote that the council approved sending forces on 'a defensive mission'; that is his claim. Parliament met in an emergency closed session, where sending the army on a combat mission abroad needs a two-thirds vote, but adjourned without a vote and will meet again on Monday (Anadolu, citing Al Qahera News; AFP). The speaker of the Houthi-run parliament in Sanaa wrote to his Egyptian counterpart that joining 'the same trench as the takfiri forces and the Israeli occupation entity' would 'pose a grave danger' to Egypt's national security (The National, from Al Masirah). Saudi Arabia has still made no public request for Egyptian forces.

The National; Anadolu; AFP via LBCI and Middle East Eye

NewEvening: Saudi Arabia says the Najran helicopter crashed from a malfunction; Houthi video appears to show it hit

Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki said on Sunday that a Saudi National Guard AH-6i reconnaissance helicopter crashed inside Saudi territory in the Najran region 'due to a technical malfunction,' killing its two officers (Arab News; Al Arabiya). The Houthis had claimed on Saturday that they shot down an 'Apache' near Najran. Video they later released appears to show a small helicopter consistent with the AH-6i being struck by a loitering munition of the Iranian-designed 358 type, which they call Saqr (Defence Blog). This answers this morning's question in part: Saudi Arabia confirms the loss and the deaths but not the cause the Houthis claim. Which account is right is unconfirmed.

Arab News; Al Arabiya; Defence Blog

NewEvening: Houthi projectile hurts six in a residential area of Najran

Saudi Civil Defense said a projectile fired by the Houthis hit a residential area in Najran, injuring six Saudi citizens and damaging homes (Okaz; Sabq). It was one of several attacks on the south of the kingdom on Sunday. Saudi Arabia later lifted its warnings for the southern and southwestern regions (Anadolu).

Okaz; Sabq; Anadolu

NewEvening: Houthi media say a Saudi strike on a house in Saada killed nine

The Houthi agency Saba said a Saudi airstrike on a home in Saada province killed nine people and wounded 16, 'most of them women and children,' and that the toll was not final (AFP via Dawn). Later Houthi-linked reports gave 10 dead and 23 wounded. The figures come only from the Houthis; the coalition has not commented, and what the strike targeted is not known.

AFP via Dawn; Saba (Houthi); CGTN (Al Masirah)

NewEvening: Riyadh airport reopens, but most foreign airlines stay away

The National reports that King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh reopened on Sunday after its closure because of Houthi attacks. Many carriers have not resumed: Qatar Airways and Air Arabia gave no date, Oman Air is suspended until 22 October, Air France and Air India until 24 October, KLM until 31 October, Lufthansa until 18 November, and Cathay Pacific until 31 January. No Saudi official statement on the reopening was found, so the reopening rests on The National's report (unconfirmed by a second independent source).

The National

Trend. Saudi Arabia is gathering outside help: Egypt is preparing legal authority to defend the kingdom, the UAE is reported to be funding southern forces again, and Europe is already helping protect oil sites. On the ground the government holds its lines around Taiz but is not advancing, while the Houthis keep up daily missile and drone fire on Saudi cities.

Saturday, 17 October 202612 items

The Houthis hit Aden a second day running, this time the airport, while Washington openly floated Israeli help for Saudi Arabia. By evening Aden airport was open again, while the Houthis reported deaths from a Saudi strike in Dhamar.

Evening: Aden airport reopens; the Cairo flight diversion is confirmed

Aden International Airport resumed traffic on Saturday, 17 October. The Yemeni sites Al-Wajha and Anbaa Aden report that a Fly Aden (Aden Airways) flight from Cairo circled over Aden for more than an hour during Friday night's attack, diverted to Djibouti, and later landed in Aden. That confirms the diversion this page marked as unconfirmed this morning. No official damage assessment of the airport has been published.

Al-Wajha; Anbaa Aden

Evening: Coalition says it destroyed two missile launchers in Saada

The Saudi-led coalition said that early on Saturday it destroyed two ballistic-missile launch platforms in Saada province and stopped a sea smuggling attempt toward Hodeidah. Its spokesman, Turki al-Maliki, posted video. Asharq Al-Awsat, Anadolu and CNN Arabic report the statement. The Houthis have not commented, and the results are confirmed only by the coalition. The coalition also said King Khalid airport in Riyadh had been targeted 20 times this month, with 17 people killed and 333 injured; those are its own figures.

Asharq Al-Awsat; Anadolu; CNN Arabic

Evening: Houthis say Saudi strike in Dhamar killed three children

The Houthi-run health ministry said a Saudi airstrike on 16 October on the villages of al-Ar and Barza'a, in Dawran Anis district of Dhamar province, killed three children and wounded 15 to 17 people. The Houthi agency Saba says cluster bombs were used; that is unconfirmed. AP, reported by the Times of Israel, says Saudi Arabia did not comment, and that the Houthis say 124 people have been killed in areas they control since the fighting resumed in September. The casualty figures come from the Houthis only.

AP; Times of Israel; Saba

Evening: Riyadh airport reopening still unconfirmed

As of Saturday evening there was no Saudi announcement that King Khalid International Airport will reopen when the notice to airmen expires at 2100 GMT, or midnight in Riyadh and in Israel. Gulf Business and AGBI report the notice and the uncertainty. Airlines are extending suspensions: IndiGo and Akasa Air have cancelled Saudi flights until 24 October, and Air India until 20 October. Reports in some South Asian outlets that the airport has already reopened are contradicted by these sources and are not confirmed.

AGBI; Gulf Business

Two missiles land inside Aden airport

On Friday night, 16 October, two missiles landed inside Aden International Airport, police and government officials said, blaming the Houthis. Reports say one landed about 500 meters from a Yemenia aircraft; passengers and crew were evacuated and no casualties were reported in early accounts. A Houthi military source told the Houthi-run Saba agency that the target was a Saudi forces command headquarters and weapons depots at Badr camp, next to the airport. AP, Al Jazeera and Xinhua all report the strike. It was the second attack on the airport in ten days, after the 7 October strike, and came one day after the deadly hit on Al-Maashiq Palace. Reports on X that a second incoming flight from Cairo was diverted to Djibouti are unconfirmed.

AP; Al Jazeera; Xinhua; Saba (Houthi)

Government says it will stay in Aden

Marwan Ali Noman, political adviser to Foreign Minister Afrah Al Zouba, told The National the government will keep operating from Aden despite the palace strike, and remains committed to Operation Dawn of Yemen. The Health Ministry now puts the palace toll at five security personnel killed and 16 injured, a figure the BBC also carries from Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani. An unnamed government minister told Reuters that two of the five missiles hit a Saudi-led coalition headquarters inside the palace complex, with Yemeni and Saudi casualties; neither Riyadh nor the coalition has confirmed Saudi casualties.

The National; BBC; Reuters via The Nation (Pakistan)

Intercepts over Yanbu, the Red Sea and Riyadh

Coalition spokesman Maj Gen Turki al-Maliki said on Friday that a ballistic missile fired toward the Red Sea oil hub of Yanbu was intercepted, along with two cruise missiles over the Red Sea and three drones over the Riyadh and southern regions, plus a drone aimed at the Eastern Province. Asharq Al-Awsat, The National and AFP report the statements. On Thursday the coalition said it shot down a cruise missile over the Medina region; Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree called that claim false and denied any launch. No damage on Saudi soil was reported from Friday's attacks.

Asharq Al-Awsat; The National; AFP via Khaleej Times

Coalition strikes smuggling targets at Hodeidah

The coalition released aerial footage dated 16 October of strikes it says hit a Houthi weapons-smuggling operation at the port of Hodeidah, including boats and storage sites. Al-Maliki posted the video on X. The coalition gave no detail on the targets, the weapons or the damage, so the results are unconfirmed. Arab News reports the release.

Arab News; coalition statement

Army claims further gains in Marib; Taiz attacks repelled

The Yemeni army said on Friday it widened its breakthrough on the eastern front in Marib and took several positions and high ground, without naming them; some outlets place the push in Raghwan district near the Mas camp, which controls roads toward Sanaa. It claimed more than 430 Houthi fighters killed or disabled and over 100 vehicles destroyed in 24 hours; those figures are unverified and the Houthis have not commented. In Taiz, the 22nd Mechanized Brigade said it repelled an eight-hour Houthi assault on the Al-Shaqab front, south of the city, killing at least 16. Anadolu Agency and Arab News both carry the army statements, but there is no independent confirmation of ground taken.

Anadolu Agency; Arab News

Trump says Riyadh will accept Israeli help

Asked on Friday at the White House whether Saudi Arabia should accept Israel's offer of help against the Houthis, President Trump said, "Yes, I think so," and that "Israel has been very helpful to Saudi Arabia." He did not say what help Israel had given. He added that Saudi entry into the Abraham Accords was back on the table, "perhaps now more than ever." The Jerusalem Post, The Times of Israel and CNN Arabic report the remarks. Saudi officials have not responded, and Riyadh's stated position remains that normalization needs a credible path to a Palestinian state. The US has still announced no decision to join strikes.

The Jerusalem Post; The Times of Israel; CNN Arabic

Security Council backs Saudi right of self-defense

On 15 October the UN Security Council adopted a presidential statement (S/PRST/2026/4) strongly condemning the Houthi attacks on Saudi airports and Aramco facilities, affirming Saudi Arabia's right of self-defense, and calling for action to stop arms reaching the Houthis. The Saudi foreign ministry welcomed it on Friday and stressed resolution 2216. The UN press office and Asharq Al-Awsat both report the statement.

UN press release; Asharq Al-Awsat

Riyadh airport closure runs out tonight

King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh stayed closed on Friday. The current notice to airmen runs until midnight local time on 17 October, but aviation outlets including AGBI say reopening is in doubt and the Saudi authorities have not confirmed a date. Air India has halted flights to several Saudi cities until 20 October, according to posts on X; that is not independently confirmed.

AGBI; Air Data News

Friday, 16 October 20265 items

The Houthis struck the government's seat in Aden, widening the war beyond the Saudi home front.

Missiles hit Maashiq Palace in Aden

On 15 October the Houthis fired missiles, and according to the cabinet also drones, at Al-Maashiq Palace, the seat of Yemen's internationally recognized government in Aden. Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani said Prime Minister Shaya al-Zindani and cabinet members were unharmed and that two of the prime minister's escorts were killed. Yemen's health minister told Reuters five people were killed and 16 wounded; that higher toll is not independently confirmed. Yahya Saree said the target was a Saudi military operations headquarters in Aden. South24, The National, AP and the government Saba agency all report the strike. It was the first attack on the palace in the current round of fighting.

The National; South24; AP; Reuters

Coalition says it destroyed 127 mountain depots

Coalition spokesman Maj Gen Turki al-Maliki said the alliance had destroyed 127 Houthi mountain storage facilities and several launch platforms in recent days, in response to attacks on Saudi airports and other sites. He put the toll from 20 attacks on King Khalid airport in Riyadh at 17 dead and 333 wounded, and three dead and 28 wounded at Abha. The National and Reuters both carry the briefing. The coalition said it would keep giving air support to government units on the ground.

The National; Reuters via Al-Monitor

Aramco operations and shipping still running

Reuters sources say Aramco's Dhahran headquarters suffered extensive fire damage, including floors near the CEO's office, but that operations at the complex continued normally, with some staff working from home and some functions moved to global offices. TradeWinds separately reported that Aramco's maritime shipping kept running. No confirmed disruption to crude production or exports from the Dhahran hit has been reported. This answers yesterday's open question on oil disruption only for now; further strikes remain a risk.

Reuters; TradeWinds

France begins deploying to protect Yanbu; EU-GCC summit postponed

France said it had begun putting into action military means under an agreement to help protect Saudi oil facilities, including at the Red Sea export hub of Yanbu. The French foreign ministry did not detail the assets. Britain has already flown an RAF Voyager tanker on Saudi-linked missions; Italy has previously supplied radars and batteries. A planned EU-GCC summit in Riyadh set for 24 October has been postponed. The United States has still announced no decision on joining Saudi strikes against the Houthis.

The National; Arab News

Taiz fighting continues; five Houthi officials captured

Asharq Al-Awsat reports that Operation Dawn of Yemen entered its tenth day with heavy fighting west and south of Taiz, including at al-Dhabab, al-Aqroud and al-Shaqab, and pressure around Jabal Han. The Fifth Presidential Guard Brigade said it captured five Houthi security officials west of Taiz; Houthi prisoner-affairs official Abdulqader al-Murtada acknowledged Interior Ministry employees were held but said they had lost their way. A Yemeni military spokesman claimed about 700 Houthis "neutralized" in more than 1,250 strikes in 24 hours; that figure is unverified and the Houthis have not confirmed it. Independent confirmation of large territorial change remains thin.

Asharq Al-Awsat; Al Arabiya English

Trend. The Houthis are now hitting the Aden seat of government as well as Saudi airports and Aramco offices, while Europe offers limited defensive help at Saudi energy sites and the US still stays out of the strikes.
