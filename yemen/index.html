Friday, 16 October 2026

The Houthis struck the government's seat in Aden, widening the war beyond the Saudi home front.

NewMissiles hit Maashiq Palace in Aden

On 15 October the Houthis fired missiles, and according to the cabinet also drones, at Al-Maashiq Palace, the seat of Yemen's internationally recognized government in Aden. Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani said Prime Minister Shaya al-Zindani and cabinet members were unharmed and that two of the prime minister's escorts were killed. Yemen's health minister told Reuters five people were killed and 16 wounded; that higher toll is not independently confirmed. Yahya Saree said the target was a Saudi military operations headquarters in Aden. South24, The National, AP and the government Saba agency all report the strike. It was the first attack on the palace in the current round of fighting.

The National; South24; AP; Reuters

NewCoalition says it destroyed 127 mountain depots

Coalition spokesman Maj Gen Turki al-Maliki said the alliance had destroyed 127 Houthi mountain storage facilities and several launch platforms in recent days, in response to attacks on Saudi airports and other sites. He put the toll from 20 attacks on King Khalid airport in Riyadh at 17 dead and 333 wounded, and three dead and 28 wounded at Abha. The National and Reuters both carry the briefing. The coalition said it would keep giving air support to government units on the ground.

The National; Reuters via Al-Monitor

NewAramco operations and shipping still running

Reuters sources say Aramco's Dhahran headquarters suffered extensive fire damage, including floors near the CEO's office, but that operations at the complex continued normally, with some staff working from home and some functions moved to global offices. TradeWinds separately reported that Aramco's maritime shipping kept running. No confirmed disruption to crude production or exports from the Dhahran hit has been reported. This answers yesterday's open question on oil disruption only for now; further strikes remain a risk.

Reuters; TradeWinds

NewFrance begins deploying to protect Yanbu; EU-GCC summit postponed

France said it had begun putting into action military means under an agreement to help protect Saudi oil facilities, including at the Red Sea export hub of Yanbu. The French foreign ministry did not detail the assets. Britain has already flown an RAF Voyager tanker on Saudi-linked missions; Italy has previously supplied radars and batteries. A planned EU-GCC summit in Riyadh set for 24 October has been postponed. The United States has still announced no decision on joining Saudi strikes against the Houthis.

The National; Arab News

NewTaiz fighting continues; five Houthi officials captured

Asharq Al-Awsat reports that Operation Dawn of Yemen entered its tenth day with heavy fighting west and south of Taiz, including at al-Dhabab, al-Aqroud and al-Shaqab, and pressure around Jabal Han. The Fifth Presidential Guard Brigade said it captured five Houthi security officials west of Taiz; Houthi prisoner-affairs official Abdulqader al-Murtada acknowledged Interior Ministry employees were held but said they had lost their way. A Yemeni military spokesman claimed about 700 Houthis "neutralized" in more than 1,250 strikes in 24 hours; that figure is unverified and the Houthis have not confirmed it. Independent confirmation of large territorial change remains thin.

Asharq Al-Awsat; Al Arabiya English

Trend. The Houthis are now hitting the Aden seat of government as well as Saudi airports and Aramco offices, while Europe offers limited defensive help at Saudi energy sites and the US still stays out of the strikes.
Watch. Riyadh King Khalid airport after the 17 October closure window; whether Aramco production or exports are hit in a further strike; whether the US joins; Taiz front lines and any confirmed break of the siege.

Thursday, 15 October 20265 items

The Houthi oil threat became real: a missile hit an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran.

Aramco headquarters building hit in Dhahran

Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Energy said a missile struck an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran on Tuesday evening, causing a limited fire and no casualties. Bloomberg and a company source quoted by Reuters described the site as Aramco's executive or headquarters complex; the ministry did not name the building. The National and Al Jazeera both report the ministry confirmation. Yahya Saree had claimed a large strike on Aramco in Dhahran and a Riyadh refinery; Saudi authorities have not confirmed a Riyadh refinery hit. Oil prices rose on the news. No disruption to production or exports has been confirmed.

The National; SPA; Al Jazeera

More intercepts over Riyadh; airport still closed

The coalition says it intercepted a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh on Wednesday; Civil Defense said debris from intercepted missiles injured four people and damaged homes. Yahya Saree claimed a precise hit on King Khalid airport; Saudi authorities have not confirmed that claim. The airport remains closed through 17 October.

Al Jazeera; Xinhua; coalition statement

STC mass rally held in Aden

Tens of thousands gathered at Al-Aroud Square in Aden on 14 October for the Southern Transitional Council's mass mobilization, answering Aidarous al-Zubaidi's call. Parallel events were held in Shabwa, Hadramout, Al-Mahra and Socotra. A rally statement condemned Houthi attacks on Saudi civilians and called for recognition of an independent southern state, while limiting southern forces' role to defending southern borders. AP also reported thousands at the Aden rally.

South24; AP

Heavy fighting on several Taiz fronts

Fighting continued west and south of Taiz, at al-Dhabab, Al-Aqrud, Al-Shaqab and the Dhubab axis. The Taiz Military Axis claimed more than 200 Houthi fighters killed or wounded on Wednesday; the figure is unverified and the Houthis have not commented. Asharq Al-Awsat reports government claims of thousands of precision strikes under Dawn of Yemen and continued pressure on the Al-Waziiyah and Al-Mudaribah (Lahj) axes. Independent confirmation of territorial change beyond ongoing clashes is thin.

Anadolu Agency; Asharq Al-Awsat

Six Kings Slam postponed in Riyadh

Organizers postponed the Six Kings Slam tennis exhibition in Riyadh from late October to late December, citing organizational and logistical challenges. Multiple outlets link the move to the Houthi attacks and security concerns. The Future Investment Initiative conferences had earlier been said to be going ahead.

DW; Arab News; BBC Sport

Trend. The Houthis have moved from airport strikes and oil threats to a confirmed hit on an Aramco administrative building, while government ground fighting remains intense but hard to verify around Taiz.
Watch. Whether Aramco production or exports are disrupted; whether the US decides to join strikes; Riyadh airport status after the 17 October closure window.
Wednesday, 14 October 20264 items

The fight is widening from airports into an open exchange over oil and fuel.

Coalition strikes Houthi fuel sites; Houthis threaten Saudi oil

The Saudi-led coalition struck Houthi-held energy sites, including the Al Sabahah fuel station in Sanaa and fuel-loading piers at Hodeidah. The Houthis answered by declaring Saudi oil installations legitimate targets and telling workers to stay away.

The National, 13 Oct

Government claims new ground in Marib

Under the Dawn of Yemen operation, government forces claim the heights around Maas camp in Medghal, in the northwest, and al-Muqailat and Jabal Zajjar north of Harib. Some Yemeni outlets say Maas camp itself fell; Al Jazeera reported only the surrounding heights. Independent confirmation of the camp is thin.

Al Jazeera; Yemeni outlets

Fighting continues west of Taiz

Fighting continues on the western approaches, at al-Dhabab and nearby. Government sources claim dozens of Houthi fighters were captured at al-Dhabab. Al-Waziiya remains the choke point toward the Mocha coast.

Government sources

Three missiles intercepted; Riyadh airport still closed

The coalition says it intercepted three missiles aimed at Riyadh and Khamis Mushait. King Khalid airport in Riyadh is still closed through 17 October; streets and offices there are otherwise reported as normal.

Coalition statement

Trend. The clearest government ground claims so far are in Marib rather than on the Red Sea coast.
Watch. Whether any Saudi Aramco or pipeline site is actually hit after the Houthi oil threat.
Tuesday, 13 October 20265 items

Saudi air defenses are under strain, and the Houthis are pressing the government's supply roads on the ground.

Saudi air defenses under strain

Officials told AP that the kingdom is investigating how Saturday's barrage reached Riyadh; drones were sent ahead of cruise and ballistic missiles. The Wall Street Journal reports that interceptors are running low and batteries are being moved to protect energy sites and palaces. Riyadh airport stays closed until 17 October. The coalition says it intercepted missiles aimed at Riyadh and Khamis Mushait on Monday night; Yahya Saree claims a hit on the airport.

AP; Wall Street Journal

Saudi agencies raise readiness

The information minister said all state agencies have raised their readiness, that life continues normally, and that citizens should not share unofficial footage.

Saudi information ministry

Trump speaks with the Crown Prince; no decision

President Trump spoke with the Crown Prince on Monday and said the Houthis are "misbehaving very badly," but announced no decision. Iran offered to help start Saudi-Houthi talks; no Saudi reply is reported. Haaretz reports that the Crown Prince refused an Israeli request to join the campaign.

AP; Haaretz

Fighting reaches al-Dhabab, the entrance to Taiz

The fighting is now at al-Dhabab, the city's southwestern entrance. Government military reports say the Houthis can fire on the al-Dhabab road. Government forces say they retook several hills there. The Houthis have not commented.

Government military reports

Houthi attack in Lahj toward the coast road

Government-aligned media say the Houthis are attacking in al-Madaraba toward the coast road that supplies the Bab al-Mandab front.

Government-aligned media

Trend. The Houthis are attacking the roads that supply both Taiz and the Bab al-Mandab front, while Saudi Arabia is reported to be short of interceptors.
Watch. The Southern Transitional Council's mass rally in Aden on 14 October, called by Aidarous al-Zubaidi as a mobilization against the Houthis.
Monday, 12 October 20264 items

Riyadh airport was hit again on Sunday, and the Houthis now openly claim the attacks.

Riyadh airport struck again

A projectile struck Terminal 4 at King Khalid airport on 11 October and started a fire. The civil aviation authority says one person was killed and eleven were wounded. The airport remains closed. Saturday's death toll has risen to 13 after an Indian citizen died of his wounds.

Reuters, via Al-Monitor

Houthis claim the strikes; Saudi defenses intercept more

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed Saturday's strike, and also claimed hits on Dammam airport and two Aramco facilities. Saudi Arabia and Aramco have not confirmed the Aramco claim. The coalition reports intercepting a cruise missile near Riyadh, a drone over the Eastern Province and a ballistic missile aimed at Khamis Mushait. Civil defense alerts sounded in Abha.

Houthi and coalition statements

Riyadh conferences will go ahead

Reuters reports that organizers say the major Riyadh conferences, including the Future Investment Initiative on 26 to 29 October, will go ahead.

Reuters

New attack at al-Wazi'iyah, west of Taiz

The Giants Brigades and Nation's Shield say they opened a new attack at al-Wazi'iyah and repelled a Houthi push toward Dhubab airport. The Houthis have not commented. The government says the main roads into Taiz are open, but its own health ministry describes the siege as tightening. Government sources say aircraft are now targeting Houthi supply routes around the city.

Giants Brigades; government sources

Trend. The Houthis are doing steady damage to the Saudi home front, while government ground gains have slowed to holding the Bab al-Mandab area.
Watch. Whether President Trump decides to join the strikes. On Saturday he said, "We may."
