The daily brief on the campaign against the Houthis

Tuesday, 20 October 2026

A Houthi ballistic-missile strike killed at least 53 security recruits at a training camp in Shabwa, far from the front lines, and the war forced Saudi Arabia to put off its flagship investor conference until 2027. Egypt's parliament pushed its vote on defending Saudi Arabia back a full week, to 26 October, while Pakistan said it is already giving the kingdom whatever support it needs.

NewHouthi missiles kill at least 53 recruits at a Shabwa training camp

On Monday, 19 October, the Houthis fired three ballistic missiles at the al-Alam camp near Ataq, in Shabwa province, where recruits to the internal security forces and police were training, according to a spokesman for the government armed forces. Shabwa's local authority gave a preliminary toll of 53 killed and more than 200 injured, up from early reports of 18 dead. The deputy governor told AFP that hospitals were overwhelmed and the worst cases were moved to Hadramawt and Aden. The BBC, Al Jazeera, AP and Asharq Al-Awsat all report it. Presidential Leadership Council head Rashad al-Alimi promised a "firm response." Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said the target was a gathering of "Saudi enemy" forces run by Saudi officers; that claim is unconfirmed, and Saudi Arabia has not commented. This is one of the deadliest single attacks of the escalation, and it shows the Houthis can reach the government's rear areas, where it is building up forces for Operation Dawn of Yemen.

BBC; Al Jazeera; AP; Asharq Al-Awsat

NewSaudi Arabia puts off its flagship investor conference until 2027

The Future Investment Initiative, often called "Davos in the Desert," was due to open in Riyadh on 26 October. The FII Institute said on Monday that the tenth edition will take place in 2027 instead, with new dates to come; its notice does not mention the war. The Wall Street Journal and The National tie the decision to the Houthi missile and drone attacks on Riyadh, including King Khalid airport, and CNBC and Arab News also report it. The National adds that two other events, Intersec Saudi Arabia and the Saudi Retail Forum, were cancelled; that detail comes from The National only. FII is Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's main showcase for Vision 2030, so this is the clearest sign so far that the Houthi campaign is costing the kingdom economically, not just militarily.

FII Institute; The Wall Street Journal; The National; CNBC; Arab News

NewEgypt's vote slips a full week, to 26 October, and some MPs push back

Egypt's House of Representatives met in a second secret session on Monday and adjourned without voting on President el-Sisi's request for authority to support Saudi Arabia. Speaker Hisham Badawi set the next session for Monday, 26 October (Parlgate, Al-Qabas, and Reuters via Dawn; Al Jazeera confirms). That replaces the Tuesday vote reported here yesterday. Al-Masry Al-Youm, citing parliamentary sources, says a large number of MPs backed the authorization, but MPs Zakaria Hassan and Maha Abdel Nasser opposed it, calling it open-ended and vague. Ahmed Farghali abstained, citing the economy, the lives of soldiers and the likely damage to Suez Canal revenue. Under Article 152 of the constitution, sending troops on a combat mission abroad needs a two-thirds majority. Saudi Arabia has still made no public request for Egyptian forces.

Parlgate; Al-Masry Al-Youm; Al-Qabas; Reuters via Dawn; Al Jazeera

NewHouthis claim hits on Aramco in Yanbu, the Shaybah field and three airports

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said on Monday that over the previous 48 hours the group carried out six operations with ballistic and cruise missiles and drones. He listed Aramco facilities in Yanbu, the Shaybah oil field, King Khalid airport in Riyadh, Abha airport, radars at Jabal al-Hisn, Jabal al-Rahma and Bir Askar, Khamis Mushait air base and a coalition naval camp, and claimed fires and casualties. He called it a new rule of "blockade for blockade, escalation for escalation." Anadolu and Reuters (via Dawn) report the claims; Reuters could not verify them, and Saudi Arabia has not confirmed any damage, so all of it is unconfirmed. Al Jazeera reports that Riyadh residents again received "hostile aerial threat" alerts on Monday. If the Yanbu claim held up, it would mean the Houthis are now reaching Saudi Arabia's Red Sea export coast as well as the east.

Anadolu; Reuters via Dawn; Al Jazeera; YemenExtra (Houthi)

NewFronts: government claims gains south of Taiz as the Houthis press the city

On day 15 of Operation Dawn of Yemen, Asharq Al-Awsat, citing military sources, says government forces and the Popular Resistance advanced in the al-Sanamat and al-Sarari areas of Saber district, south of Taiz, after an offensive that began at dawn on Sunday. It says they also kept pressure on the remaining Houthi pockets around Bab al-Mandab and worked to secure the high ground between Taiz and Lahj. Alhurra reports that the Houthis are throwing large numbers of fighters at the city after cutting its southern road to Aden, and that army spokesman Col. Majed al-Nuzaili says the attacks were turned back. Regional outlets (Waradana, Beth Press) report that the Giants Brigades and Homeland Shield forces took Tabbat al-Ardi on the Dhubab axis; that is unconfirmed. Army casualty claims do not agree with each other: Asharq Al-Awsat quotes about 370 Houthis "neutralized" in 1,326 operations over 24 hours, and Al Arabiya headlines 558 in 1,164 strikes. Both are the army's own figures and unverified.

Asharq Al-Awsat; Alhurra; Waradana; Al Arabiya

NewPakistan says it is giving Saudi Arabia whatever support it needs

Pakistan's military spokesman, Lt. Gen. Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry (DG ISPR), told Independent Arabia that the security of Saudi Arabia and the Two Holy Mosques is non-negotiable and that Pakistan is already providing whatever support the kingdom needs for its defense. He described the defense pact Pakistan signed with Saudi Arabia and Turkey as purely defensive, but said a collective response could follow if diplomacy fails. He also said Pakistan knows of no formal request for military help from the Yemeni government but would evaluate one. Dawn, The News and Radio Pakistan carry the interview. He gave no details of what the support consists of.

Independent Arabia; Dawn; The News; Radio Pakistan

NewYemen's foreign minister rejects Iran as a mediator

Foreign Minister Afrah Al-Zouba, speaking on the sidelines of an Organization of Islamic Cooperation foreign ministers' meeting in Baghdad, said Iran cannot arm the Houthis and then offer to mediate, and rejected Tehran's framing of the war as a Yemeni-Saudi conflict. She cited Iran's handover of Yemen's embassy in Tehran to a Houthi envoy in 2019 and flights to Sanaa in July 2026 without government approval. This is reported by Asharq Al-Awsat only so far, so the Iranian offer and her remarks have no second source yet. It signals that Aden and Riyadh are not looking for a negotiated pause while the offensive is under way.

Asharq Al-Awsat

Trend. The Houthis are answering the government offensive by hitting far behind the lines: recruits in Shabwa, and Saudi Arabia's capital, airports and oil sites. That pressure is now costing the kingdom in prestige and investment, as the postponement of FII shows. Riyadh's allies are lining up in words, with Pakistan saying it already gives support, but Egypt's formal commitment has slipped a week and is drawing some open dissent. On the ground the government claims small gains around Taiz while the city's supply problem remains.

Egypt's vote on sending forces to Saudi Arabia slipped to Tuesday even as Egyptian transport planes were tracked into the kingdom, and the Gulf states closed ranks behind Riyadh. Egypt's vote moves to Tuesday; Egyptian transport planes tracked into Saudi Arabia

Egypt's parliament ended Sunday's emergency session without voting on President el-Sisi's request for authority to defend Saudi Arabia, and resumes today, Monday 19 October. MP Mostafa Bakry, who is close to the state, wrote that debate will run three days because so many members asked to speak, with the vote expected on Tuesday (Ahram Online); Reuters and AFP confirm the delay. The pro-Sisi parties hold more than the two-thirds needed, according to the head of the Arab affairs committee, and the Egyptian Social Democratic Party says it will abstain (CNN Arabic). Separately, flight trackers reported at least two Egyptian Air Force C-130H transports, with transponders off, landing at King Fahd Air Base in Taif on Sunday. Al Jazeera Arabic saw the aircraft in western Saudi airspace but could not confirm where they landed, so the landing and any cargo are unconfirmed. Saudi Arabia has still made no public request for Egyptian troops.

Gulf ministers back Saudi self-defense and call for the Houthis to be listed as terrorists

The GCC Ministerial Council, meeting in Manama on Sunday for its 168th session, condemned the Houthi attacks on Saudi airports and vital facilities, declared full solidarity with Saudi Arabia, and affirmed the individual and collective right of self-defense under Article 51 of the UN Charter. It said the security of member states is indivisible, called for enforcement of the UN arms embargo, and urged that the Houthis be designated a terrorist group. The GCC secretariat published the communique; Al Jazeera and Xinhua report it. The Saudi and Russian foreign ministers also discussed the Yemen escalation and the threats to Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab on the sidelines (Saudi Foreign Ministry via AFP). The statement is political cover, not a commitment of forces.

Saudi defense minister in Abu Dhabi as the UAE funding report stays unconfirmed

The official readouts from the UAE presidency and the Saudi Press Agency (via Asharq Al-Awsat) speak only of regional developments and priorities for the coming period. They do not mention Yemen funding. The visit comes a day after The Guardian reported that the UAE agreed to resume paying the Giants Brigades in return for Saudi air support. The timing fits that report, but neither government has confirmed a deal, so it remains unconfirmed.

Houthis cut the main road from Aden into Taiz

Yemen's transport ministry said the Houthis blew up a culvert on the road at Hayjat al-Abd, cutting the main route linking Taiz with Lahj and Aden. Al-Masdar Online reports the Houthis already control the area and that the road had reopened after an earlier blast, and Asharq Al-Awsat also reports the destruction. This matters for the campaign: it is the government side's main supply line into Taiz, which the Houthis have encircled since 12 October, so it makes holding the city harder. Reports that the government is building a replacement route have not been found.
