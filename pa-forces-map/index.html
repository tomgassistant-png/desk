Map of the Palestinian Authority's security forces, from Hakol Hayehudi

Saved October 26, 2026 (Israel time, UTC+2) at Tom's request. The map is the outlet's own work, reproduced here as a backgrounder. Everything on it is Hakol Hayehudi's account, not a figure we have confirmed. Items marked unconfirmed rest on that single source.
Map: Hakol Hayehudi, October 22, 2026 (1400 x 634 pixels; click the image for full size). From the article "On the Table: The Map of the Palestinian Authority's Nests" (Hebrew: Al HaShulhan: Mapat HaKenim shel HaReshut HaPalestinit) by Meir Ettinger, published October 22, 2026, 11:06 (Israel time). Original article: hakolhayehudi.co.il. Original image file: KO8TYEsRl0pez4.jpeg. The labels on the map are in Hebrew; translations are below.

What the map shows

The map shows the central and northern part of Israel and the West Bank, with ten red numbered markers. Each marker connects by a red line to a callout for one battalion of the Palestinian Authority's National Security Forces, with a satellite view of the base, a photo of the area commander, and a box with names and a strength figure. A larger boxed photo at the top right shows the National Security commander. The map's own place labels (in Hebrew) are Netanya, Tel Aviv, Jerusalem and Hebron.

The outlet's article says its figures come from "open-source intelligence" (official publications, videos and social media posts by the Palestinian forces themselves). It describes the picture as an estimate of tens of thousands of fighters across all branches, with about 8,000 in ten National Security battalions (nine regional battalions plus the mobile reserve "Battalion 101"), and more than 60 police posts that it says it will map in a second article. The article itself says there is no current public data on exact strength, and that the figures on the map come from research and estimates from about ten years ago (the map marks them "as of 2017"). The map does not show Battalion 101 or the police posts, apart from a note on the Jericho box.

What the article adds, as the outlet's claims

  • The outlet says the force is built as ten battalions, not police stations, and that its commanders include officers trained abroad, naming Algiers for most battalion commanders. This is Hakol Hayehudi's claim.
  • It says the main training base is at Na'imah camp near Jericho, and that Battalion 101 is a mobile reserve unit documented with sniper rifles, machine guns, armored vehicles and anti-tank missiles. Unconfirmed; we have not checked these points against another source.
  • It says the Salfit base is about 340 meters from the fence of Ariel, and that the Jenin base sits on the ruins of an abandoned Nahal outpost.
  • It says the Palestinian Authority presents these forces as internal police, and that the outlet assumes the IDF and the Shin Bet already know the locations.

Hakol Hayehudi is an Israeli right-wing outlet and its framing (for example, calling the force an army "under cover") is its own view. Keep that in mind when using the map; we have not verified the names, locations or numbers on it.
