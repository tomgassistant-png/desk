NewAl Arabiya: Washington to announce progress on the Lebanon framework this week

Al Arabiya's Washington correspondent, Pierre Ghanem, reported on Sunday that the State Department intends to announce progress this week on the three-way framework between the United States, Lebanon and Israel. According to his sources, Secretary of State Rubio has told his staff to finish two things by the middle of this month: a monitoring and verification mechanism for the "pilot zones" in the south, where Hezbollah is to be disarmed in exchange for Israeli withdrawal, and a framework for an economic aid conference for Lebanon. The main obstacle is the composition of the monitoring force. Israel objects to several countries, France in particular, and the United States will not send its own soldiers to the south, so Washington is looking for a way to use French troops away from the border alongside other European forces. A State Department official said any international presence must be tied to the goals of the framework agreement and not become "permanent mediation." The same sources say Hezbollah has raised at least $40 million a month this year through smuggling across the Syrian border, falsified transactions at the port of Beirut and state salaries, since Iran stopped sending money (Al Arabiya, via Infinity News). The American position, stated in public after Rubio met Prime Minister Salam two weeks ago, is that disarmament must come before any Israeli withdrawal.

NewAn-Nahar: detained men say the Dahr al-Baidar explosives belong to Hezbollah

An-Nahar reported new details on the 500 kilograms of explosives (250 of TNT and 250 of C-4) seized at Dahr al-Baidar. According to leaked investigation records, the two detained men admitted that they belong to Hezbollah and that the explosives are Hezbollah's. They said an official in the Bekaa gave them the load to deliver to the southern suburbs of Beirut, to a man known as "Dhu al-Fiqar." Judge Claude Ghanem, the government commissioner at the Military Court, sent the file back to the army's Military Police for further questioning. The paper says the idea of an assassination plot is still only a suspicion. In recent days the army has also seized 245 kilograms of TNT smuggled from Syria and another load of explosives and shells near Qasr in the Hermel district (An-Nahar). If the confessions hold up, the state will have a court case that ties Hezbollah directly to explosives moved inside Lebanon. That gives Salam something concrete to show Washington before the Tampa meeting, but it also makes a confrontation with Hezbollah harder to avoid.

NewTwo olive harvesters killed in south Lebanon; Israeli strikes on three villages

In the south, two men harvesting olives were killed on Sunday, a retired soldier near Yater by Israeli artillery and an engineer in Froun by an old munition. Froun is one of the three pilot zones announced in July. Israel carried out four airstrikes on Mansouri, Talloussa and Kfar Tibnit, shelled at least nine villages overnight and demolished houses in Haddatha and Byout al-Siyad (L'Orient Today).
