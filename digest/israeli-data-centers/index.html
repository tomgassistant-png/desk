Israeli data centers

Daily research digest section · latest edition 27 October 2026

26 October 2026

Shamir Energy takes a 10-year option to build a 100-megawatt data center at Ashdot Ya'akov in the Jordan Valley, with its own power plant or storage

A small power station or energy-storage site is planned next to the data center to supply its electricity, at a time when the Electricity Authority plans to allow only about 1,000 megawatts of new data center load against requests of about 27,000 megawatts (Calcalist). The option requires feasibility studies, permits and financing; if used, the land lease runs almost 25 years, and the industrial park may take up to 18 percent of the operating company. Calcalist says this may mark the entry of energy companies into a field so far led by real estate firms such as Azrieli, Mega Or and Melisron.

20 October 2026

Data center rules: about 1,300 new megawatts until 2030, NIS 2 million guarantees, and a separate plan to give sites land without tenders

Fuller reports on the Electricity Authority's 18 October decision explain the difference between earlier accounts. Beyond about 2,000 megawatts of connections already approved, the authority will allocate about 1,300 new megawatts until 2030: about 1,000 for large commercial data centers and about 300 for the government, bringing the total to roughly 3,000 to 3,300 megawatts, which Calcalist says equals about 20 percent of Israel's projected electricity use in 2030 (Calcalist/Ctech; Globes). Each new site is capped at 200 megawatts; developers must post guarantees of NIS 2 million per megawatt, and sites must have backup arrangements that let them be cut off from the grid at times of extreme demand. The full decision will be published in the coming days for three weeks of public comment and may change. Avi Shkouri of Psagot told Globes that only about 4 percent of open requests can be met, and that the largest planned projects, including Mega DC in Hadera (about 1,100 megawatts), Kidmat Galil and Nesher (875) and Neve Yamin (720), will have to be redesigned or split. Globes also reports that at a meeting at the Government Secretariat with Economy Minister Nir Barkat, Cabinet Secretary Yossi Fuchs and Erez Askal of the national AI directorate, officials agreed to advance recognizing data centers as productive industry in national priority areas, so they could receive state land without a tender at a set price; the Finance Ministry's budget division says it did not agree, and the Economy Ministry says the decision is not final. The land plan is reported only by Globes and is unconfirmed.

19 October 2026

Electricity Authority caps each new data center at 200 megawatts; reports differ on the national ceiling

The plenum of Israel's Electricity Authority decided on Sunday evening, 18 October, that each new data center may connect to the grid at no more than 200 megawatts, and that developers must post guarantees of NIS 2 million per megawatt, or NIS 400 million for a full 200-megawatt site, to keep out speculative applications (Globes; TheMarker). The two papers differ on the total. Globes reports a ceiling of 1,300 megawatts for the whole sector, of which 300 megawatts must serve local users and the rest may serve foreign buyers of computing power. TheMarker reports a proposed ceiling of 3 gigawatts plus 300 megawatts reserved for the defense establishment, and says half of each guarantee would be returned only after 60 percent of the requested capacity is operating. TheMarker says the details had not yet been fully published, and the authority's own text was not found online, so the overall ceiling is unconfirmed. Other criteria, including a split between the center of the country and the periphery and preferences for developers who bring their own generation and storage, go to public comment; Globes expects the first new approvals to take at least several months, probably until after a new government is formed, and notes that more than one gigawatt of data centers was already approved under the earlier first-come policy. Requests submitted to the grid operator total about 27,000 megawatts, against a record peak demand of about 17,300 megawatts in August 2025; the freeze on new applications above 8 megawatts ends in early December (Bizportal).

17 October 2026

Electricity Authority plans a 200-megawatt cap per new data center; decision due Sunday

Israel's Electricity Authority plans to limit each new data center to 200 megawatts and to publish the draft for three weeks of public comment, starting on Sunday, 18 October, Calcalist reported on 14 October. Requests to connect data centers to the grid total about 27,000 megawatts, against installed generating capacity of about 18,800 megawatts; average demand is about 9,000 megawatts and the record peak was 17,300 megawatts in August 2025. Fifty of the requested facilities are larger than 200 megawatts, among them Mega DC (1,100 megawatts, on the former Alliance tire factory site in Hadera) and SDS (875 megawatts, in Nesher). New applications were frozen for 140 days from June, and the authority estimates data centers could use about 20 percent of national electricity by 2030. According to reports in TheMarker and The State of Israel, members of the authority differ on the total capacity to allow through 2035, from several hundred megawatts to about one gigawatt, against requests of up to 2.3 gigawatts; proposals include construction deadlines and guarantees of NIS 2 million per megawatt, and only a partial decision is expected on Sunday.
