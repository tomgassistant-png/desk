20 October 2026 · today

NewLiberal MNAs sworn in, all to the people of Quebec; PQ cabinet to be sworn in on Wednesday without the lieutenant governor

The 40 Liberal members of the National Assembly were sworn in at the Red Room on 19 October; all of them took only the oath to the people of Quebec, not the optional oath to the King, and about half took it in both French and English (Canadian Press via L'actualité). Liberal leader Charles Milliard presented his party as a 'government in waiting' and said it would be a reliable partner in solving crises but 'tough and intractable' on immigration to the regions, public finances and the national question; André Fortin is the Liberal house leader. One Liberal, Affine Lwalalika (Vachon), was not sworn in because of a judicial recount. Premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon has written to Lieutenant Governor Manon Jeannotte asking her not to attend the swearing-in of his cabinet on Wednesday, 21 October; the oaths will be received by the secretary general of the government, and her office told Le Devoir that she will follow the ceremony on the Assembly's channel and sign the appointments at the same time (Le Devoir; the letter is reported by Le Devoir only and is not independently confirmed). The PQ has said it wants to abolish the lieutenant governor's post, which several experts consider very difficult under the Canadian Constitution.

17 October 2026

St-Pierre Plamondon: fix Quebec first, then talk of independence

At the swearing-in of the Parti Québécois's 59 members of the National Assembly on 15 October, Paul St-Pierre Plamondon closed with 'Vive le Québec libre' but said that before talking about independence the PQ must 'get Quebec back on the rails' and regain voters' confidence, the Montreal Gazette reported. He has asked the former premier Pauline Marois and Jean Royer, a former chief of staff to Jacques Parizeau, to train the new members. His cabinet is to be presented on 21 October.

16 October 2026

All 59 PQ MNAs sworn in; cabinet still due 21 October

On Thursday 15 October, all 59 Parti Québécois members of Quebec's National Assembly were sworn in at the Red Room in Quebec City, taking an oath of allegiance to the people of Quebec rather than to the King. Premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon spoke of the party's largest caucus since 1998 and said cabinet, of about 20 ministers, is still due on 21 October. Liberal MNAs are to be sworn in on Monday, Québec solidaire on Tuesday and Conservatives on Thursday. A court challenge to the 2022 law that dropped the oath to the King remains pending (Canadian Press via CityNews; Radio-Canada).

15 October 2026

Parti Québécois MNAs to be sworn in today; cabinet due 21 October

The 59 newly elected Parti Québécois members of Quebec's National Assembly are to be sworn in on Thursday 15 October; Liberal opposition members on 19 October; and the PQ cabinet on 21 October (Radio-Canada; CBC). Premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon says he will not swear an oath of allegiance to the King, relying on Quebec's 2022 law that made the National Assembly oath the only required oath. That law is under court challenge; an injunction hearing is expected between 19 October and 9 November. St-Pierre Plamondon has asked government lawyers whether he can keep his campaign pledges to bar the lieutenant-governor from the opening speech and to redirect the lieutenant-governor's office budget (about 1.3 million Canadian dollars for 2026–27) to homelessness work. Governor General Louise Arbour has said she is skeptical of those plans (CBC; Radio-Canada).
