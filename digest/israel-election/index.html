16 October 2026 · today

NewZman Yisrael poll: Yashar 26, Likud 20; blocs still 54–49

A Zman Yisrael poll for the Times of Israel, fielded on 14–15 October (500 respondents, margin of error 4.4 points), gives Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar 26 seats and Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud 20. The anti-Netanyahu Jewish-led bloc has 54 seats and the Netanyahu bloc 49, both short of 61, with Arab parties at 13 and the nonaligned Reservists at 4. Ofer Winter's Amcha Yisrael is at 2.8%, below the 3.25% threshold. Last week's Zman Yisrael split was 55–51 the other way on the blocs; the party gap between Yashar and Likud has widened from 24–20 to 26–20 (Times of Israel).

NewChannel 13 Madad poll: Netanyahu bloc 51, Eisenkot 50; Hendel and Winter both at 4

Channel 13's Madad poll (Shmuel Rosner and Noah Slepkov, 1,018 respondents, margin of error 3.1 points, fielded 13 October) puts Netanyahu's bloc at 51 seats and Eisenkot's at 50, with Arab parties at 15. Yashar leads with 20 seats and Likud has 18. For the first time in this Madad series, Yoaz Hendel and Yaron Zelekha's Reservists clear the threshold with 4 seats; Winter's Amcha Yisrael also has 4. That result conflicts with the same week's Zman Yisrael poll, which had Winter below the threshold. Eisenkot leads Netanyahu 45% to 38% as more suitable to be prime minister (Channel 13; Madad).

NewNetanyahu: Defense and National Security stay with Likud; Otzma fires back

At a campaign event in Acre on Thursday, Netanyahu said that if he forms the next government, Likud will keep the National Security and Education ministries, and repeated that Itamar Ben-Gvir will not be defense minister. Otzma Yehudit accused him of planning to shut the party out of the next coalition and bring in Eisenkot instead. Ben-Gvir told Walla he still expects to become defense minister if Otzma gains seats. Friday 16 October is the legal deadline for surplus-vote agreements; Otzma remains the only party in Netanyahu's bloc without one after Ben-Gvir refused a deal with Winter (Times of Israel; Ynet).

NewIsrael Hayom poll: change bloc 52, right 50; Yashar 24; Winter still below the threshold

An Israel Hayom / Kantar poll published on Thursday (551 adults online, 14–15 October, margin of error 4.2 points) puts the change bloc at 52 seats and the right at 50. Yashar rises to 24 from 22 in the prior Israel Hayom survey; Likud has 21; Bennett's Beyachad falls one seat to 12. Otzma Yehudit has 9 and Religious Zionism 6. Hendel–Zelekha's list has 4; Winter's Amcha Yisrael remains under the electoral threshold. The paper notes that neither Jewish-led camp reaches 61 without some form of Arab-party involvement, and that 87% of change-bloc voters in the survey would accept joining, external support, or abstention by an Arab party (Israel Hayom).

NewLieberman rules out a minority government and Arab or Haredi partners

On Thursday Avigdor Lieberman said Yisrael Beytenu would oppose any minority government, including a transitional one, and demanded a majority coalition of Zionist parties only, without Ra'am, the Joint List, Shas or United Torah Judaism. He also said his party would not sit with Likud while Netanyahu leads it. Benny Gantz replied that Lieberman was either lying about eventually relying on Arab parties or that the country was heading to a repeat election. The dispute matters because recent polls show neither Jewish-led bloc reaching 61 without Arab involvement (Jerusalem Post; Israel Hayom).

16 October 2026 edition · Jerusalem Post · Israel Hayom

NewIsrael Hayom reports a U.S. push for Netanyahu–Eisenkot unity; Huckabee denies it

Israel Hayom on Thursday, citing diplomatic officials, described a proposed framework in which Likud and Yashar would form a broad government, with Netanyahu serving up to two years then retiring, while advancing regional deals, especially with Saudi Arabia. Eisenkot's side would seek a timed Netanyahu exit, rollback of judicial-reform laws, and an October 7 state inquiry, according to that report. U.S. Ambassador Mike Huckabee posted on X that the U.S. government is "NOT trying to manipulate Israel's election or telling [the PM] what to do," called related claims fake news, and Channel 14 reported the same denial (Israel Hayom; Channel 14).

15 October 2026

Channel 12 poll: Eisenkot's bloc 54, Netanyahu's 49; Winter again below the threshold

A Channel 12 Midgam poll published on Monday evening (509 respondents, margin of error 4.4 points) again put Gadi Eisenkot's bloc at 54 seats and Benjamin Netanyahu's at 49. Yashar leads with 23 seats, Likud has 20, Bennett's Beyachad (Together) 13, the Democrats and Yisrael Beytenu 9 each, the Joint List, Otzma Yehudit and Shas 8 each, United Torah Judaism 7, Religious Zionism 6, Ra'am 5 and the Reservists 4. Ofer Winter's Amcha Yisrael again falls short of the 3.25 percent threshold, at the equivalent of about 1.8 seats. Parties opposed to Netanyahu would hold 67 seats if the Arab lists are counted, but parts of Eisenkot's bloc refuse to sit with them, so the Zionist opposition alone remains short of 61 (Haaretz).

Channel 14 poll: Netanyahu's bloc at 62; Bennett rises to 11 seats

A Channel 14 / Next Data poll published on Wednesday (568 adults, 14 October) gives the right-wing bloc 62 seats, the left-center bloc 44 and the Arab parties 14. Likud is the largest party with 29 seats, followed by Yashar with 20. Bennett's Beyachad rises from 7 to 11; the Democrats fall from 10 to 8; Otzma Yehudit rises to 8 and the Joint List to 9. Shas has 10, United Torah Judaism 8, Religious Zionism–Zehut 7, Yisrael Beytenu 5 and Ra'am 5. The Reservists–economic list falls to 3.1 percent, near the threshold; Winter's Amcha Yisrael is at 2.7 percent. Netanyahu leads Eisenkot 52% to 32% on who is most suitable to be prime minister, and 52% to 48% in a direct comparison (Channel 14; Ice). The deadline for surplus-vote agreements is Friday 16 October.

Ben-Gvir refuses a surplus-vote deal with Ofer Winter

National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir said on Tuesday that Otzma Yehudit would not sign a surplus-vote agreement with Ofer Winter's Amcha Yisrael. He called Winter "Bennett II" on X. Winter replied that Ben-Gvir was throwing right-wing votes away. Without a partner, Otzma Yehudit is left out of the surplus deals already signed inside Netanyahu's camp (Likud with Religious Zionism; Shas with United Torah Judaism). The opposition has deals between Yashar and the Democrats, and between Beyachad and Yisrael Beytenu. If Winter stays below the threshold, those votes are wasted for the right; Channel 12 and Channel 14 both currently place him below it (Haaretz; Jerusalem Post / Maariv).

Polymarket: Eisenkot about 48%, Netanyahu about 27%, Bennett about 14%

On Polymarket, Eisenkot is now at about 48% to be the next sworn-in prime minister, Netanyahu at about 27% and Bennett at about 14% (odds as of early Thursday Israel time). On 12 October the same market had Eisenkot at about 46%, Netanyahu at about 32% and Bennett at about 14%. The market resolves to whoever is formally sworn in after the 27 October election, not an interim caretaker (Polymarket).

12 October 2026

Kan poll puts Netanyahu's bloc at 53 seats, the opposition at 50

The Kan public broadcaster published a Kantar poll on Sunday evening (552 respondents, margin of error 4.2 points). Yashar has 22 seats, Likud 20, Bennett's Beyachad 12, Otzma Yehudit 9, and the Democrats, Yisrael Beytenu, United Torah Judaism and the Joint List 8 each. Ofer Winter's Amcha Yisrael and Yoaz Hendel's Reservists both cross the threshold with 4 seats. The Netanyahu bloc, counting Winter, rises from 51 to 53 and the anti-Netanyahu bloc falls from 52 to 50, with the Arab parties at 13. Eisenkot still leads Netanyahu 43% to 38% on who is more suitable to be prime minister, and 30% of respondents are undecided (Haaretz). Thursday's Zman Yisrael poll had the opposite result, 55 to 51 for the anti-Netanyahu bloc, so the polls agree only that neither side reaches 61. Winter's vote share matters most: when he crosses the threshold, the right gains about four seats, and when he falls below it, those votes are lost.

12 October 2026 edition · Haaretz

Bennett floats, then withdraws, a role for Netanyahu in a future government

Also on Sunday, Bennett told a conference of the religious Zionist paper HaDor that Netanyahu could join a future government, and even be a minister, if he accepted the bloc's guidelines, including a state commission of inquiry into 7 October. Eisenkot, Yair Golan and Avigdor Liberman rejected the idea at once, and Bennett withdrew it the same evening, saying it was meant to show the absurdity of the idea. Benny Gantz was the only one to support it, calling for a unity government with Likud (Times of Israel). Shas and United Torah Judaism signed their expected surplus-vote agreement. A UTJ candidate, Moshe Rosenthal, said the party would sit with whoever passes a draft-exemption law that the High Court will uphold, and that the rabbis will decide after the election (Times of Israel). That statement leaves the door open to Eisenkot, which is a real threat to Netanyahu, because without the Haredi parties his bloc has no path to a majority.
