Netanyahu presents a new security doctrine: 'The regime must fall, and I will bring it down'; Eisenkot calls it 'two hollow pages'

On Sunday Netanyahu held a two-hour security briefing for Israeli reporters at his Jerusalem office, his first such in-person briefing since 7 October 2023; several outlets boycotted it (Times of Israel). He presented an updated national security doctrine that puts preemptive offensive action ahead of containment, said bringing down Iran's regime remains the main goal ('The regime must fall, and I will bring it down'), claimed that about 90 percent of the work against Israel's enemies is done, and said Israel will act alone against Iran if necessary. He said he would put about NIS 450 billion into defense over the next decade, including more domestic weapons production (Ynet; Times of Israel). He warned that 'in two years' a U.S. president 'very hostile to Israel' could take office, and said Israel must build alliances with India and Latin America. On the next coalition he rejected a unity government with his opponents, said he wants a 'broad national government' that keeps his current partners, including Ben-Gvir's party, and said he would pass a Haredi enlistment law after the election. He called the international reaction to recent settler attacks 'a huge exaggeration' while saying the rioters cause Israel 'enormous damage.' Eisenkot answered that the doctrine was 'two hollow pages, full of slogans that have nothing whatsoever to do with a security doctrine' and an attempt to frighten voters (Ynet).

Winter's vote share matters most: when he crosses the threshold, the right gains about four seats, and when he falls below it, those votes are lost.

Bennett floats, then withdraws, a role for Netanyahu in a future government

Also on Sunday, Bennett told a conference of the religious Zionist paper HaDor that Netanyahu could join a future government, and even be a minister, if he accepted the bloc's guidelines, including a state commission of inquiry into 7 October. Eisenkot, Yair Golan and Avigdor Liberman rejected the idea at once, and Bennett withdrew it the same evening, saying it was meant to show the absurdity of the idea. Benny Gantz was the only one to support it, calling for a unity government with Likud (Times of Israel). Shas and United Torah Judaism signed their expected surplus-vote agreement. A UTJ candidate, Moshe Rosenthal, said the party would sit with whoever passes a draft-exemption law that the High Court will uphold, and that the rabbis will decide after the election (Times of Israel). That statement leaves the door open to Eisenkot, which is a real threat to Netanyahu, because without the Haredi parties his bloc has no path to a majority.
