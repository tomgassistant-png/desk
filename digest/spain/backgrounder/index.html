Spain election: Backgrounder

Last updated: 21 November 2026

← Back to the Spain election section

Dates. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez (PSOE) called a snap general election on 5 October 2026, three days after the Congress of Deputies voted down two housing decrees with the votes of the PP, Vox and Junts. Royal Decree 806/2026 (Official State Gazette, BOE, 6 October 2026) dissolved the Congress and Senate elected on 23 July 2023 and set the vote for Sunday, 29 November 2026. The Congress has 350 seats, so a majority is 176. The campaign runs from 13 to 27 November, and Saturday, 28 November, is the day of reflection, when campaigning stops. Polls may be published until Monday, 23 November; the electoral law bans publishing them in the five days before the vote. The new Congress and Senate meet on 23 December. After that the king consults the parties and proposes a candidate for prime minister; RTVE reports that this will not happen before Christmas. Without the early call, the four-year term of the 2023 parliament would have run until the summer of 2027.

Parliament and government. In the 2023 election the PP won 137 seats (33.1 percent), the PSOE 121 (31.7), Vox 33 (12.4), Sumar 31 (12.3), ERC 7, Junts 7, EH Bildu 6, the PNV 5, and the BNG, Coalición Canaria and UPN one each (Interior Ministry figures, as published by RTVE). Sánchez went on to lead a PSOE–Sumar coalition government, with Junts as an early partner after the amnesty law; Junts broke with the government about a year later (RTVE). During the term Podemos's four deputies left the Sumar group, and one seat tracker (pactómetro.es) also lists a Vox deputy, Javier Ortega Smith, leaving the Vox group in July 2026 (unconfirmed). By the end, the PP, Vox, Junts and UPN together held 178 seats in the full chamber, which is how the housing decrees fell; in the smaller standing committee that sits after dissolution, the left and the PNV hold 35 of 69 votes (Europa Press). The government never presented a new national budget in this term. Spain still runs on the 2023 budget, rolled over each year, and 2027 will start with another rollover (El País, 17 October).

Main parties and leaders. PSOE: Pedro Sánchez, prime minister since 2018, heads the Madrid list with Margarita Robles and Félix Bolaños. PP: Alberto Núñez Feijóo, with Cayetana Álvarez de Toledo and Borja Sémper behind him in Madrid. Vox: Santiago Abascal, in his sixth general election as list leader. Frente Amplio (Broad Front), the alliance of Sumar, IU, Más Madrid and the Comuns formed for this election: its candidate for prime minister is Ada Colau, former mayor of Barcelona, who heads the Barcelona list; Emilio Delgado heads the Madrid list. Yolanda Díaz, first deputy prime minister and Sumar's founder, is not a candidate but is campaigning for Colau. Podemos runs separately, led by Irene Montero, and in Catalonia it backs ERC's list headed by Gabriel Rufián. Junts: Míriam Nogueras, with Carles Puigdemont taking part in rallies by video link. PNV: lists headed by Maribel Vaquero, Idoia Sagastizabal and Gorka Urtaran; the party president is Aitor Esteban. EH Bildu: Oskar Matute. BNG: Néstor Rego. Se Acabó La Fiesta (SALF): Luis "Alvise" Pérez. Aliança Catalana is not running. Only the PSOE, PP, Vox and Frente Obrero run in all 52 districts (Antena 3, 9 November).

Possible majorities. Feijóo says the PP already governs with Vox in four regions (Extremadura, Aragón, Castilla y León and Andalusia), that Vox has set him "no uncrossable line" so far, and that he will not govern "at any price" (RTVE, 18 November). Abascal says Vox will bargain hard and wants Feijóo to say before the vote whether he would make deals with "the remains of the PSOE" (El País, 20 November). The PNV says it will not support Feijóo if Vox is part of his majority, and would demand much clearer rules before backing Sánchez again (La Vanguardia, 19 November). Junts says it will not support Feijóo, and that to back a PSOE government it would ask the Socialists to put forward a candidate other than Sánchez, which it calls the "Starmer route" (EFE, 6 November).

Polls (latest from each pollster; percent of the valid vote). Most private polls put the PP first, with the PSOE second and gaining during the campaign, and the PP and Vox together above 176 seats. 40dB. for El País and Cadena SER (daily tracking, 16 to 20 November, 2,000 online interviews): PP 31.9, PSOE 31.2, Vox 17.1; seats PP 131, PSOE 124, with the right-hand bloc at 189. Sigma Dos for El Mundo (13 to 19 November, 4,561 phone and online interviews): PP 31.2 (125 to 131 seats), PSOE 27.6 (111 to 117), Vox 18.9 (57 to 62), Frente Amplio 7.1 (11 to 14), Podemos 0.8 (no seats); PP plus Vox 182 to 193. Sondaxe for La Voz de Galicia (13 to 17 November, 1,000 interviews): PP 28.5, PSOE 27.8, Vox 19.6 (as reported by Mapa Electoral). SW Demoscopia for La Séptima (18 November) is the only private pollster with the PSOE ahead: PSOE 31.9, PP 29.1, Vox 16.4. Key Data for Público (an estimate built from published and unpublished polls, 19 November): PP 31.2 (129 seats), PSOE 26.9 (109), Vox 19.1 (66). EM-Analytics for Electomanía (published 20 November, 6,559 interviews): PP 31.0, PSOE 27.6, Vox 18.2. GAD3 for ABC (3 to 5 November, 1,502 phone interviews): PP 31.9, PSOE 25.9, Vox 18.6. Also reported by the Capitol poll list and not checked against the pollsters' own reports: NC Report for La Razón (published 12 November) PP 32.1, PSOE 26.1, Vox 19.0; SocioMétrica for El Español (published 14 November) PP 31.6, PSOE 27.8, Vox 19.1; IMOP for El Confidencial (published 13 November) PP 31.5, PSOE 26.9, Vox 19.0; DYM for Henneo (published 6 November) PP 33.1, PSOE 27.3, Vox 18.6. GESOP for El Periódico (6 to 8 October, 1,000 interviews; its latest vote estimate found): PP 28.7, PSOE 26.1, Vox 18.9, with the right at 189 to 197 seats. The state pollster CIS, in its pre-election survey (7 to 30 October, 45,074 interviews, published 12 November), has the PSOE first at 33.3, the PP at 25.5, Vox at 18.4 and Frente Amplio at 4.7. Other analysts who re-ran the CIS raw data got the PSOE at about 29.6 to 30.1 and the PP at about 27.2 to 27.7 (Capitol list). ABC leaves the CIS out of its poll average, citing what it calls the CIS's "evident bias"; ABC's average on 21 November had the PP at about 31 percent, 3.5 points ahead of the PSOE. Mapa Electoral's median of the latest poll from each of 18 pollsters (19 November): PP 31.4, PSOE 27.0, Vox 18.9.

Prediction markets. Polymarket, read on 21 November 2026 at about 09:45 Israel time: "Next prime minister of Spain": Feijóo 87.5 percent, Sánchez 11.8 percent (about $4.2 million traded). "Spain general election winner" (party with the most seats): PP 75.5 percent, PSOE 24.5 percent (about $0.8 million traded). Both markets close after the vote on 29 November. Odds are bets, not polls. No other prediction market was checked for this edition.

Regional elections. Three regions broke from the usual May cycle and voted early: Extremadura on 21 December 2025, Aragón on 8 February 2026 and Castilla y León on 15 March 2026 (dates from one election guide; Castilla y León result per RTVE: PP 33 of 82 seats, PSOE 30, Vox 14). Andalusia voted on 17 May 2026: the PP won 53 of 109 seats, two short of a majority, with the PSOE at 28, Vox 15, Adelante Andalucía 8 and Por Andalucía 5 (RTVE results; EFE). The next regular date is Sunday, 23 May 2027 (the fourth Sunday of May, as the electoral law sets), with municipal elections across Spain and regional elections in ten regions (Asturias, the Balearic Islands, the Canary Islands, Cantabria, Castilla-La Mancha, the Valencia region, La Rioja, Madrid, Murcia and Navarre) plus Ceuta and Melilla, unless any of them calls an early vote (Tu Papeleta guide). Galicia, the Basque Country and Catalonia are not due to vote in the next year.

Background to the campaign. Housing is the main issue: the eviction of a Madrid woman, Maricarmen Abascal, on 23 September set off protests, and a general strike over housing and wages took place on 11 November (RTVE). Two high-speed rail crashes on the same line, at Adamuz (Córdoba) on 18 January 2026 with 46 dead and at Urda (Toledo) in late October with 69 dead counted by 29 October, have put Transport Minister Óscar Puente under pressure; Vox filed a criminal complaint against him at the Supreme Court (RTVE, 29 October). A mass irregular entry into Ceuta from Morocco on 30 and 31 July is under investigation by a National Court judge (Público). Court cases around the PSOE: the Supreme Court upheld the prison terms of former minister José Luis Ábalos (24 years and 3 months) and his aide Koldo García (19 years and 8 months) in October (Público); Sánchez's brother David was sentenced on 14 July to nine years' disqualification from public jobs for abuse of office, which he is appealing (EFE); the prime minister's wife, Begoña Gómez, awaits trial, as Feijóo pointed out during the campaign (RTVE, 18 November); and former PSOE prime minister José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero is under investigation for tax fraud and smuggling over jewels found in his office (El País, 6 November). The PSOE calls the cases against Sánchez's family a "hunt" pushed by the far right (Patxi López, quoted by EFE); the PP says they show that "no one is above the law" (Feijóo, quoted by EFE).
