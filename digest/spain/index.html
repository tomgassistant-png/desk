Spain election

Daily research digest section · latest edition 21 November 2026

Tracker for Spain's general election of 29 November 2026 and what follows it: dates, parties and leaders, polls, prediction-market odds, coalition talks, and news that affects the race or the survival of the next government (court cases, the budget, partners such as Junts, the PNV, Sumar's successors and Podemos). 21 November 2026 · today

NewFeijóo is seen as the winner of the only head-to-head debate, but the vote barely moves; two more debates are set for 23 and 26 November

40dB. for El País and the SER (500 online interviews right after the debate) had 43 percent naming Feijóo the winner and 33.3 percent Sánchez; Sigma Dos for El Mundo had 50.5 to 29; and Ateneo del Dato for elDiario.es had 45.2 to 31.9, with 88 percent of its sample saying they would keep their vote. The roughly two-hour debate, organized by the Television Academy and watched by an average of about 10 million people, covered housing, the economy, social policy, corruption, migration and foreign affairs; El Confidencial reports that Sánchez apologized several times over the rail crashes and the corruption cases. Vox's Santiago Abascal criticized his absence from the debate (El País). RTVE holds a debate of eight party spokespeople on Monday, 23 November, at 22:00 Spain time, and the four candidates for prime minister (Sánchez, Feijóo, Abascal and Ada Colau of Frente Amplio) meet on Thursday, 26 November, at 22:00 (El Confidencial).

NewThe PSOE closes the gap in the final week of polls, but most still give the PP and Vox a majority; publication stops after 23 November

Even so, 40dB. gives the PP and Vox together 189 seats, 13 more than a majority; Vox falls to 17.1 percent and Podemos would lose its seats. Sigma Dos for El Mundo (13 to 19 November) has a wider gap, PP 31.2 and PSOE 27.6, with the PP and Vox at 182 to 193 seats. Sondaxe for La Voz de Galicia (13 to 17 November) has the PP only 0.7 points ahead, and SW Demoscopia for La Séptima is the only private pollster with the PSOE first (31.9 to 29.1), per Mapa Electoral. Key Data for Público (19 November) gives the PP and Vox 195 seats. Mapa Electoral notes that a 0.7-point lead in a 1,000-person poll is a statistical tie, and that daily tracking polls share most of their interviews from one day to the next. ABC's average (21 November) has the PP at about 31 percent and 3.5 points ahead. The electoral law bans publishing polls from Tuesday, 24 November, until the polls close on 29 November, so Monday is the last day for new figures.

NewThe state pollster CIS puts the PSOE nearly eight points ahead, unlike every private pollster except one

Asked whom they prefer as prime minister, 38.3 percent of those who answered named Sánchez, 24.3 percent Feijóo, 15.4 percent Abascal and 8.3 percent Gabriel Rufián of ERC; 49.1 percent said they decide their vote according to what convinces them at the time (CIS). Other analysts who re-ran the CIS raw data with their own models (Descifrando la Guerra, Ateneo del Dato and Opina360) got a narrower PSOE lead, about 29.6 to 30.1 percent against 27.2 to 27.7 for the PP, according to the Capitol poll list. ABC excludes the CIS from its poll average, citing what it calls the CIS's "evident bias." Polymarket on 21 November gave Feijóo an 87.5 percent chance of becoming the next prime minister and the PP a 75.5 percent chance of winning the most seats.

NewFeijóo says Vox has set him "no uncrossable line"; Abascal demands he say before the vote whether he would deal with "the remains of the PSOE"

He did not rule out a new deal with Vox after the election, said he would not become prime minister "at any price," promised "no step back" on abortion and LGBT rights, and said he could repeal all or part of the euthanasia law (RTVE). He also said he holds "fluid" talks with Abascal. On 20 November in Salamanca, Abascal welcomed that Feijóo offered Sánchez no deals in the debate, but asked him to say before 29 November whether he would make agreements with "the remains of the PSOE" or has another plan; about 200 people protested against his visit, according to police sources (El País). Abascal also rejected any approach to Carles Puigdemont, who had said Vox would get "a door slammed in its face."

NewThe PNV and Junts set their terms: no support for Feijóo with Vox, and Junts wants a PSOE candidate other than Sánchez

Esteban said the most likely outcome is a PP–Vox majority and that he is confident the PNV will keep the five seats it needs for its own group (La Vanguardia). Junts's candidate, Míriam Nogueras, said on 6 November that the PP will not get Junts's votes and that, to consider backing a PSOE-led government, Junts would ask the Socialists to replace Sánchez with another candidate, which Junts calls the "Starmer route" (EFE). Junts's campaign slogan is "Catalunya al centre," and Puigdemont is taking part in its main rallies by video link, including the central one in Castelldefels on 22 November (Europa Press).

NewSupreme Court orders that about 163,000 people naturalized under the "grandchildren's law" instruction be kept off the voter roll on 29 November

The only exceptions are 901 people who have filed a consular certificate showing descent from exiles, and anyone else who files one before election day. The court is acting on challenges by Vox and the group Iustitia Europa. It has separated people naturalized under the Democratic Memory Law itself from those naturalized under a 2022 instruction by the then director general Sofía Puente, which ABC describes as going beyond the law; it suspended their voting rights in September and October but did not extend the suspension to their children (Europa Press, ABC). The court called the government's compliance reports "hard to understand," agreeing with the prosecutor. The government's lawyers had said that checks cut the number on the roll to 113,326. The court also gave the parties two days to comment on the security checks of the roughly 61,000 phones that will read digital ID QR codes at polling stations (ABC).

NewThe caretaker parliament saves one housing decree and kills the other, a day after a general strike over housing

The PSOE, Sumar, ERC, EH Bildu, Podemos and the PNV voted for it, and the PP, Vox and Junts against (Europa Press). The decree also creates "Tu Casa," a 10 billion euro loan line through the state bank ICO for first-home buyers, with loans of up to 50,000 euros. A second decree, backed by Sumar, which would have renewed rental contracts automatically and made landlords who end one pay the tenant a year's rent, fell 34 to 35 when the PNV joined the PP, Vox and Junts; the PNV called the payment "disproportionate." These were new versions of the two decrees whose defeat in the full chamber in early October led Sánchez to call the election. The vote came one day after a general strike and large marches over housing and wages, which RTVE described as having "mixed" results.

NewCourt dates in the final week: a deputy prime minister testifies on 23 November in the Air Europa bailout case

Aagesen sat on the board of the state fund that assessed the loan when she was secretary of state for energy. Other witnesses on 23 and 24 November include Sánchez's chief economic adviser Manuel de la Rocha, the head of the postal service Pedro Saura, and former Sumar spokesman Íñigo Errejón (El Confidencial). The case began with complaints by the PP and Vox. Público reports that the judge set the dates one day after the election was called, and notes that the same judge opened a case against Irene Montero a week before a 2021 Madrid regional election that was later closed. Separately, on 6 November the National Court judge in the Plus Ultra case refused the anti-corruption prosecutor's request to also charge former prime minister José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero with embezzlement over jewels found in his office, while keeping him under investigation for tax fraud and smuggling (El País).

NewOn the left: Yolanda Díaz drops her bid to lead the ILO to campaign for Ada Colau, and Frente Amplio proposes a 10 million euro cap on inheritances

The same day Frente Amplio presented its program: a 100 percent tax on any inheritance above 10 million euros per heir (75 percent between 5 and 10 million) and lower rates below 120,000 euros; a 10 percent annual tax on the value of homes held by companies and by individuals with more than three homes worth over 800,000 euros together, unless they are rented at affordable prices; a law to expropriate homes from owners of large portfolios; and a universal child benefit of 200 euros a month per child (El Español). Polls give Frente Amplio about 5 to 7 percent, far below Sumar's 31 seats in 2023, and Podemos, which runs separately under Irene Montero, may lose all its seats (El País, 40dB.; Sigma Dos). The program details come from one report and are unconfirmed in a second source.

NewVox stalls in the polls during the campaign and courts Catalan voters while Aliança Catalana sits out

El País says Vox had started the campaign strong after Abascal's interview with the TV host David Broncano. Other pollsters have Vox steadier, at about 18 to 20 percent (Sigma Dos 18.9, Sondaxe 19.6), and every poll still has it well above its 12.4 percent of 2023. In Catalonia, where Sílvia Orriols's Aliança Catalana is not running, Abascal ended a rally in Girona with "Visca Catalunya!" followed by "Viva España!" and said Vox would defend Catalan identity, which it sees as also Spanish; he said Vox keeps its rejection of Spain's system of regional governments but that changing it would need a constitutional majority out of reach in the next term (El País, 20 November).

Backgrounder

Spain votes on Sunday, 29 November 2026, in a snap general election called by Pedro Sánchez on 5 October. Most private polls put the PP first and the PP and Vox together above the 176-seat majority; the CIS puts the PSOE first.
