22 October 2026

Trump says he is open to a three-way meeting with Xi and Putin in China next month, with nuclear weapons on the agenda

Trump told reporters on 19 October, Washington time, that he would hold a three-way meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin during his November visit to China 'if President Xi wants to have it,' to talk about 'nuclear weapons and denuclearisation' (South China Morning Post; Caixin). Caixin reports he said he had only just heard of the proposal and understood the meeting would take place in China, around the APEC summit in Shenzhen; Kremlin spokesman Peskov had said such talks would be useful. No date, format or agenda has been agreed, and China has not confirmed it.

20 October 2026

SCMP: US and Chinese negotiators push for investment deals before the November Trump–Xi meeting in Shenzhen; China adds export controls on drones and tunnel borers

The South China Morning Post reported on 20 October, citing several unnamed sources, that US and Chinese negotiators have made 'considerable progress' on setting up a joint Board of Investment ahead of Trump and Xi's planned meeting at the APEC summit in Shenzhen in November, modeled on the Board of Trade led by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and Vice Premier He Lifeng. According to the report, the sides are discussing a shortlist of possible Chinese investments in non-sensitive US sectors such as electric vehicles, battery storage and power equipment, or possibly consumer goods; one source said the remaining issues have 'major gaps.' The paper also reports that both sides are using leverage, rare earths for China and jet engine and aircraft parts for the United States, in talks on extending the trade truce, which now runs to January 2027. These points rest on anonymous sources and are not independently confirmed. Separately, on 19 October China's commerce ministry issued two announcements, effective 18 November, that require export licenses for long-range and long-endurance drones and key drone parts, and for tunnel boring machines, some excavators and high-altitude shotcrete machines bound for 'sensitive countries and regions' in disputes with China; the notices do not name those destinations (Anadolu; Peter's World, citing the ministry's announcements). No US government response had been published.

17 October 2026

China lets Visa prepare a domestic card-clearing business and books its first US soybeans since the summit

China's central bank approved on 13 October Visa's application to prepare a domestic bank-card clearing institution; Visa has one year to prepare and cannot yet issue renminbi cards. On 15 October Qiu Wenxing of the Chinese embassy in Washington called the approval 'a direct outcome of the presidential summit' between Trump and Xi in late September. This week Chinese state-owned buyers booked at least 20 cargoes of US soybeans, about 1.3 million tons, the first purchases since the summit, according to Bloomberg as cited by Quartz; most will be loaded from December to March. US soybeans still face Chinese tariffs of about 13 percent and are not on the lists of tariff cuts; China has pledged to buy 25 million tons a year through 2028. The two leaders are due to meet again at APEC in November and the G20 in December.

China proposes export controls on 40 technologies, including AI and solar

On 16 October China's Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology proposed adding 40 technologies, in fields including artificial intelligence, biomedicine and solar power, to the catalogue of technologies whose export is restricted: 14 would be banned and 26 would need a license. Public comment is open until 15 November, and exporters must register before exporting in the meantime. The proposal does not name any country; reports do not say it is aimed at the United States.
