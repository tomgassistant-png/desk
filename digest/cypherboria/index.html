Background: how Brandon Peterson counts the Bible, what he has dropped, and who else does Bible number work

Method. Peterson counts how many times a word or name appears in the standard KJV. He reports a "Raw" count (every appearance) and a "Pure" count (only appearances that refer to the person or thing in question). His examples: "Jesus" has a Pure count of 980, which is 70 times 7, after three verses about other men named Jesus are left out; and the names of the fishermen in John 21 appear 153 times, the number of fish they caught. His best-known claim, the "Elton Anomaly," is that the KJV's words and numbers total 823,543, which is 7 to the 7th power. What he has dropped. In a 2020 FAQ he says he began with Hebrew and Greek gematria, where each letter has a number value, then tried it in English. He warns that gematria "can be used to convince anybody of anything" and says he has "gradually drifted further and further away from it." In a March 2026 written reply to the critic Bryan Ross, he says he dropped equidistant letter sequence (ELS) codes, the kind used in The Bible Code, "as insignificant a while ago." He agrees that gematria is often selective, because people choose whichever cipher gives the number they want. He calls himself "very cautious" and "more and more reluctant" about it, but has not dropped it, because a few patterns in the Hebrew and Greek values of Jesus Christ still seem hard to explain to him.
