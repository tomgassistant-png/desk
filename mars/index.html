Background: Background: Scholar's Mars course — how Mars has been explored, a map set, and where each mission stands.

Summary

Where things stand on 27 October 2026. Two NASA rovers are working on the surface: Curiosity and Perseverance. Perseverance set a new distance record on 11 October (45.22 km, past the 45.16 km that NASA's Opportunity rover drove). Japan's Martian Moons eXploration probe (MMX) launched on 20 October and is on its way; arrival at Mars is August 2027 per ESA and NASASpaceFlight [JAXA's own release gives no arrival date].

Money and politics: a stopgap funding law keeps NASA at its 2026 level of $24.4 billion through 11 December 2026. The White House asked for $18.8 billion for 2027, and the House committee bill kept NASA flat at $24.4 billion. Mars Sample Return (bringing Perseverance's rock tubes to Earth) was cancelled and has no replacement plan. Two bid protests (formal challenges to a contract award) are now open at the Government Accountability Office (GAO) against NASA's Mars Telecommunications Network award to Blue Origin; the second was filed on 23 October.

Plans from companies and agencies are goals, not funded schedules, unless the entry says otherwise. They are marked [aspirational]. Facts I could not match to a second source are marked [unconfirmed]. A sol is one Martian day, about 24 hours 39 minutes.

NASA Mars 2020 team blog, "Tinga's Tale", by Margaret Deahn (Purdue University) · NASA science blog primary source

Describes a target named "Tinga" at the southern border of "Idubi", an outcrop made mostly of olivine (an igneous mineral). At Tinga the lower rock is likely a breccia (rock made of broken, angular pieces, often formed by an impact), and higher dark rocks may be part of the Idubi olivine unit or breccia that was "baked" by contact with it. The team says more study is needed. The image is from sol 2009 (14 October 2026). The post is dated 26 October on NASA's site; the author's note says it was written 19 October.

Money and politics

  • Mars Sample Return (MSR): no approved program. NASA's 2026 request said it "plans to terminate" MSR. For 2027, AAS says the request "reiterates the cancellation", while CNN's Ashley Strickland says the request does not mention the program at all; the two read the document differently. NASA's July 2026 statement: it is "exploring a range of potential return options" that are simpler and cheaper, "including possible commercial or international contributions". The 2026 spending law gave $110 million for Mars Future Missions (NASA slides); the Planetary Society says it was meant to buy down technical risk for a restructured sample return. The Planetary Society, an advocacy group for space exploration, says the House committee bill for 2027 is "silent" on MSR. · CNN · NASA MEPAG slides · Planetary Society
  • 2027 request versus Congress. White House request (3 April 2026): NASA $18.829 billion, 23% below the $24.438 billion enacted for 2026; Planetary Science $1.876 billion (down 26%); Mars Exploration $248.3 million; Perseverance operations $38.0 million, down from $87.5 million in the 2024 plan (AAS says NASA is "reassessing objectives for the mission"); Mars Future Missions $110 million (American Astronomical Society, AAS, summary of NASA's request). House Appropriations Committee bill (13 May): NASA flat at $24.4 billion, Planetary Science $2.5 billion, Mars Exploration $300 million (Planetary Society tables; the full House has not passed it as far as I read). Terms

  • Sol: one Martian day, about 24 hours 39 minutes. Rovers count their days in sols from landing.
  • MOLA: the Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter, an instrument that measured Mars's heights by laser; used for the course's maps.
  • ISRU: in-situ resource use: making water, air or fuel from what is already on Mars instead of bringing it.
  • GAO bid protest: a company challenges a federal contract award at the Government Accountability Office, which must decide within about 100 days.
  • CR: continuing resolution: a stopgap law that keeps agencies funded at last year's level while Congress finishes the new budget.
  • Mars window: the roughly 26-month period when Earth and Mars line up well for a launch.
