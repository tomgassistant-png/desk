## How to edit
Edit mars/make_data.py only. Change UPDATED to today's date and time (Israel time, "YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM") and CHECKED to today's date. The Mars link in the site nav turns bold for a visitor when UPDATED is newer than their last visit, so change UPDATED only when something on the page changed. Each morning, replace the whole `whats_new` list (see "What's new" below). An empty list means the built page has no What's new section. Do not run builds or publish: the worker only edits make_data.py (and adds picture files under mars/img/, see "Pictures"). The morning run builds and publishes once (python3 mars/make_data.py && python3 build_mars.py, then the standard build and publish command). Never edit mars/data.json or mars/index.html by hand. mars/route.json is a copy of NASA's Perseverance waypoint file; replace it only when you have re-downloaded https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/M20/Layers/json/M20_waypoints.json (points as [lon, lat, sol], 4 decimals, plus "last").

## What's new (every morning, after the checks)

The page can show a short "What's new" block under the title and the background link, above the Summary. It lists only substantive developments first recorded in this edition (the calendar date in UPDATED). The block is left out entirely when there is nothing to report: no heading and no empty box. A daily-check timestamp by itself is not news. Yesterday's highlights are not carried forward as new.

Do this on every run, after the daily checks and after you have written any real feed items:

1. Replace the whole `whats_new` list in mars/make_data.py. Do not append. Delete yesterday's entries. The list sits just above the feed.
2. Add an entry only when all of the following are true.
   - It is new in this edition: a mission event, an agency statement, a budget or Congress change, a GAO (Government Accountability Office) protest decision, or a company claim with a new date.
   - The same fact is already written, with its source link, in the feed, the narrative, the status board, or the upcoming list. The What's new line is a short restatement of that sourced text, one or two plain sentences. Do not add a fact, a number, a date, or a link that is not already in this file.
   - It changes the picture. Do not add a line for a daily-check note whose point is "nothing new" or "only checked dates change". Do not add a line only because a rover's sol count moved, a raw image arrived, or a source was opened again, unless that check changed a result a reader should know (a new drill hole, a new distance, a new sample, a mission event, a new date).
3. Each entry has:
   - `edition`: today's date as YYYY-MM-DD, the same calendar date as UPDATED.
   - `text`: the short restatement, in plain American English. Explain an unfamiliar term in a few words the way the feed does (a sol is one Martian day; the FAA is the U.S. agency that licenses launches).
   - `links`: pairs copied from links already in this file, label then url, using `L("label","url")`.
   - `detail_url` (optional): the `url` of the matching feed item. The page then adds "Full note in the feed", a link down to that note.
4. If nothing qualifies, set `whats_new = []`. Leave it empty.

`python3 mars/make_data.py` copies the list into mars/data.json. `python3 build_mars.py` draws the block from that list. The builder drops any entry whose `edition` is not the date in UPDATED, so a leftover from yesterday stays off the page once UPDATED moves to a new day. Still delete leftovers. Do not keep old sentences and only change `edition`: that would present old news as new. If every entry is dropped, or the list is empty, the page has no What's new section.

Change UPDATED when the page changed, including when you add a real What's new entry. A run that only refreshes CHECKED, with `whats_new` left empty, does not by itself call for a new UPDATED (see "Nothing new" below).

## What goes in the feed (at the top, newest first)
A dated item that changes the picture: a mission event, an agency statement, a budget, Congress, GAO or Mars Sample Return item, a protest decision, a company claim with a date, a new rover number worth reporting. Each item has: when (YYYY-MM-DD or with time), who said it (agency or a named person and role), kind (primary source, named reporter, amateur tracker, Chinese state media, interested party), where, text (what happened, plain words), evidence, url to the page you opened, and optional "other" for a conflicting source with links, and optional picture fields (see "Pictures"). Prefer primary sources: NASA/JPL, ESA, JAXA, CNSA, UAE Space Agency, ISRO, SpaceX and Blue Origin statements, Congress and NASA budget documents; then named reporters. Keep about 20 recent items; budget and Congress facts stay in the feed until a newer item replaces them, and older facts move into the status board or plans sections. Keep the feed sorted newest first (make_data.py sorts it by the "when" field). When a feed item changes a status, also update the status board row (its "d" source date and "s" text), and the matching plans or upcoming rows. Keep the dates of money events (stopgap law ends, GAO decisions, elections) in the upcoming list.

## Pictures (every run)
The page is illustrated with pictures that change with the news. Each feed item, status-board row, the Perseverance counter and the Curiosity section can carry one. In make_data.py use the helper IM(file, alt, caption, credit, source_page_url) and spread it into the dict: **IM(...). The file goes in mars/img/ (JPEG, at most 1000 px wide, quality about 80; resize with PIL). publish.sh copies mars/img/. A copyrighted photo we may not copy is a link only: photo_link=dict(url=..., label=...).
(a) New feed items: give each a fitting, freely licensed picture when one exists. A launch gets a launch photo; rover news gets a rover image; a mission event gets an agency photo or artist's concept; a budget or Congress item may use a NASA or Congress picture, or none. Sources, in order: NASA (images are generally not copyrighted; check https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/; no NASA logos, no sign of endorsement), ESA only when the item page says CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO (credit ESA), Wikimedia Commons files marked public domain or CC with the author and license read from the file page (use the Commons API: action=query&prop=imageinfo&iiprop=url|extmetadata). Do not use JAXA photos (JAXA's terms bar changes and redistribution without permission; use photo_link to JAXA Digital Archives instead), and do not use ESA items that say "No ESA licences available" or credit another agency. No press photos, no X post pictures.
(b) Latest views: when you update the Perseverance and Curiosity counters, replace their pictures with a current raw image. Perseverance: https://mars.nasa.gov/rss/api/?feed=raw_images&category=mars2020&feedtype=json&num=20&page=0&order=sol+desc (use a Mastcam-Z or Navcam frame; image_files has small, medium, large and full_res links; "link" is the raw-image page to cite). Curiosity: https://mars.nasa.gov/api/v1/raw_image_items/msl/latest/ gives latest_sol; then https://mars.nasa.gov/rss/api/?feed=raw_images&category=msl&feedtype=json&sol=SOL&num=100 (use a Mastcam or Navcam frame; the plain "sol desc" query returns nothing for Curiosity). Credit NASA/JPL-Caltech (plus ASU or MSSS as the feed says), say "raw image, not processed or checked", give the sol and the Earth date taken, and cite the raw-image page. Use the sol and date in the caption only as the feed gives them.
(c) Never use a picture you did not open. Open every downloaded file with the Read tool, and make sure it shows what the caption says (a drawing is called a drawing; a file photo of an earlier flight says so). Never invent a credit, author or license: copy them from the file page. If the license or the subject is doubtful, use no picture. When a feed item drops off, delete its file from mars/img/ unless another row still uses it.

## Nothing new
If a check finds nothing, do not pad. Add no feed item for it. Update CHECKED to today's date, and add one line in the narrative or the status board "checked" text such as "27 Oct 2026: nothing new from JAXA about MMX since 21 Oct". Set `whats_new = []` so yesterday's highlights do not stay on the page as if they were new. Do not change UPDATED when nothing changed on the page except the checked date.
