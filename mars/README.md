## How to edit
Edit mars/make_data.py only. Change UPDATED to today's date and time (Israel time, "YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM") and CHECKED to today's date. The Mars link in the site nav turns bold for a visitor when UPDATED is newer than their last visit, so change UPDATED only when something on the page changed. Do not run builds or publish: the worker only edits make_data.py (and adds picture files under mars/img/, see "Pictures"). The morning run builds and publishes once (python3 mars/make_data.py && python3 build_mars.py, then the standard build and publish command). Never edit mars/data.json or mars/index.html by hand. mars/route.json is a copy of NASA's Perseverance waypoint file; replace it only when you have re-downloaded https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/M20/Layers/json/M20_waypoints.json (points as [lon, lat, sol], 4 decimals, plus "last").

## Tom's rules (apply to every line)
Plain American English ("center", not "centre"), plain words, no slang. Explain terms like sol, MOLA, ISRU in a few words. Check any date, figure or name against at least one source besides Wikipedia, or mark it [unconfirmed] in the text. Company and agency timelines that are goals, not funded schedules, get [aspirational]. Partisan or interested sources (SpaceX, Blue Origin, Rocket Lab, the Planetary Society, Chinese state media, NASA when it describes its own budget) are full sources, labeled by who they are. Link only pages you opened this run. Do not invent numbers. Say "published", not "live". Do not touch haiti/, horses/, digest/, items.json, yemen/ or the prayer app. Do not email or message anyone.

## Daily check list (every run, in this order; note in the feed what you found, or "nothing new" with the date)
1. NASA: https://science.nasa.gov/mars/ and the Mars Exploration Program page (https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/programs/mars-exploration/future-of-mars-plan/); NASA news releases that mention Mars.
2. Perseverance blog (https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/perseverance-mars-rover/) and Curiosity blog posts (titles "Curiosity Blog, Sols ...", linked from https://science.nasa.gov/mission/msl-curiosity/). Update the perseverance counter in make_data.py: sol, date, km, mi, latitude, longitude, area, and est_sol (also refresh the two "latest view" pictures; see "Pictures"). Odometer source order: newest NASA blog number; else the last point in NASA's traverse file https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/M20/Layers/json/M20_waypoints.json (it lags days to weeks; say so). Today's sol is our own arithmetic (one sol is 1.0275 Earth days from a dated anchor); keep the "our own arithmetic" label. Update the Curiosity section the same way (blog sols and date, new drill holes, drives).
3. NASA JPL news (https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/) for Mars items. If a page returns an access error (403), say so and cite only what you could read.
4. ESA: Mars Express, ExoMars Trace Gas Orbiter (TGO), Rosalind Franklin, and the MMX pages on esa.int; ESA Science Programme news.
5. JAXA: MMX status (https://global.jaxa.jp/ press releases and the ISAS MMX page). MMX launched 20 Oct 2026; record cruise status, any trajectory maneuvers, and the arrival date. Arrival is August 2027 per ESA and NASASpaceFlight, but JAXA's launch release gives no date: keep checking JAXA and say where each date comes from. Phobos landing: ESA says 2030, NASASpaceFlight says no earlier than December 2028 [unconfirmed] until JAXA says.
6. China: CNSA / China National Space Administration, Xinhua (news.cn English) and CGTN for Tianwen-1 (orbiter), Zhurong (dormant since May 2022) and Tianwen-3 (launch about 2028, return about 2031, landing zone by end of 2026, all [aspirational]).
7. UAE: UAE Space Agency / Mohammed bin Rashid Space Centre / WAM for Hope (extended through 2028).
8. India: ISRO (isro.gov.in and @isro on X) for Mangalyaan-2 and Mangalyaan-1; Indian press only as named-reporter secondary sources. As of 27 Oct 2026 there is no official date: keep [unconfirmed] [aspirational].
9. SpaceX: spacex.com Mars and Starship pages and updates; X @SpaceX; Elon Musk on X (@elonmusk) about Starship, Mars or the Moon, with the post's date and a link. Starship flights and the next Mars windows (late 2028 to early 2029) are [aspirational].
10. Blue Origin: blueorigin.com news, @blueorigin; the Mars Telecommunications Network (MTN) contract and New Glenn flights (ESCAPADE launched on New Glenn).
11. ESCAPADE: the Berkeley mission page (https://escapade.ssl.berkeley.edu/mission-design/) and NASA's page for the Mars departure burn (November 2026) and Earth-flyby news.
12. Budget, Congress and Mars Sample Return (MSR). There is no separate money section on the page: results go into the dated feed, as items. ## What goes in the feed (at the top, newest first)
A dated item that changes the picture: a mission event, an agency statement, a budget, Congress, GAO or Mars Sample Return item, a protest decision, a company claim with a date, a new rover number worth reporting. Each item has: when (YYYY-MM-DD or with time), who said it (agency or a named person and role), kind (primary source, named reporter, amateur tracker, Chinese state media, interested party), where, text (what happened, plain words), evidence, url to the page you opened, and optional "other" for a conflicting source with links, and optional picture fields (see "Pictures"). Prefer primary sources: NASA/JPL, ESA, JAXA, CNSA, UAE Space Agency, ISRO, SpaceX and Blue Origin statements, Congress and NASA budget documents; then named reporters. Keep about 20 recent items; budget and Congress facts stay in the feed until a newer item replaces them, and older facts move into the status board or plans sections. Keep the feed sorted newest first (make_data.py sorts it by the "when" field). When a feed item changes a status, also update the status board row (its "d" source date and "s" text), and the matching plans or upcoming rows. Keep the dates of money events (stopgap law ends, GAO decisions, elections) in the upcoming list.

## Pictures (every run)
The page is illustrated with pictures that change with the news. Each feed item, status-board row, the Perseverance counter and the Curiosity section can carry one. In make_data.py use the helper IM(file, alt, caption, credit, source_page_url) and spread it into the dict: **IM(...). The file goes in mars/img/ (JPEG, at most 1000 px wide, quality about 80; resize with PIL). publish.sh copies mars/img/. A copyrighted photo we may not copy is a link only: photo_link=dict(url=..., label=...).
(a) New feed items: give each a fitting, freely licensed picture when one exists. A launch gets a launch photo; rover news gets a rover image; a mission event gets an agency photo or artist's concept; a budget or Congress item may use a NASA or Congress picture, or none. Sources, in order: NASA (images are generally not copyrighted; check https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/; no NASA logos, no sign of endorsement), ESA only when the item page says CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO (credit ESA), Wikimedia Commons files marked public domain or CC with the author and license read from the file page (use the Commons API: action=query&prop=imageinfo&iiprop=url|extmetadata). Do not use JAXA photos (JAXA's terms bar changes and redistribution without permission; use photo_link to JAXA Digital Archives instead), and do not use ESA items that say "No ESA licences available" or credit another agency. No press photos, no X post pictures.
(b) Latest views: when you update the Perseverance and Curiosity counters, replace their pictures with a current raw image. Perseverance: https://mars.nasa.gov/rss/api/?feed=raw_images&category=mars2020&feedtype=json&num=20&page=0&order=sol+desc (use a Mastcam-Z or Navcam frame; image_files has small, medium, large and full_res links; "link" is the raw-image page to cite). Curiosity: https://mars.nasa.gov/api/v1/raw_image_items/msl/latest/ gives latest_sol; then https://mars.nasa.gov/rss/api/?feed=raw_images&category=msl&feedtype=json&sol=SOL&num=100 (use a Mastcam or Navcam frame; the plain "sol desc" query returns nothing for Curiosity). Credit NASA/JPL-Caltech (plus ASU or MSSS as the feed says), say "raw image, not processed or checked", give the sol and the Earth date taken, and cite the raw-image page. Use the sol and date in the caption only as the feed gives them.
(c) Never use a picture you did not open. Open every downloaded file with the Read tool, and make sure it shows what the caption says (a drawing is called a drawing; a file photo of an earlier flight says so). Never invent a credit, author or license: copy them from the file page. If the license or the subject is doubtful, use no picture. When a feed item drops off, delete its file from mars/img/ unless another row still uses it.

## Nothing new
If a check finds nothing, do not pad. Add no feed item for it. Update CHECKED to today's date, and add one line in the narrative or the status board "checked" text such as "27 Oct 2026: nothing new from JAXA about MMX since 21 Oct". Do not change UPDATED when nothing changed on the page except the checked date.
