Abu Falah, 16 October 2026: what the evidence shows

Prepared 17 October 2026, Israel time. Items marked unconfirmed rest on a single source or on one side only. All times are Israel time (UTC+3).

Conclusions

  • Established. A group of several dozen masked men came down from the hilltop outpost above the olive groves of Khirbet Abu Falah and attacked the people in the groves with stones from slings, sticks and fire. Reporters from Reuters, AP and the Washington Post and four Israeli or American witnesses say so. The raw video shows it, and the IDF itself, as quoted by N12, said that dozens of Jewish rioters came down from an illegal outpost toward the village. The Binyamin Regional Council does not deny that Israelis were there and threw stones. It says they were answering stones and fireworks.
  • Established. Heidi Levine, 64, a photographer for the Washington Post, was knocked down and struck several times while she was on the ground by several hooded men, not by one person. Her vest was marked PRESS. Video from the Palestinian side shows four to six men standing over a person on the ground, and CNN and the Post describe the same scene in other footage. Her camera bag, cameras and phone were taken. No report says they have been recovered. The Israeli journalists' union demanded on Friday that they be returned.
  • Established. The Palestinian who was shot was hit by Israeli soldiers, not by a settler. The IDF said troops fired at a suspect who ran at them with a sharp object, and an AP photograph shows soldiers standing over the wounded man. He is Abduljaber Hassan (Obeid), 54, a member of the village council. He was shot in the thigh. The circumstances are disputed: the army says he was running at the troops with a sharp object, while village officials say he was unarmed or carrying harvest tools.
  • Not supported by the footage. JSR and the Binyamin Council say an Israeli teenager "helps her get up" and that the cry "they're killing her" was false. Their 21-second clip is edited. The first 13 seconds, with the shout, come from the raw Palestinian video at a later moment. It then cuts to a short zoomed shot in which Levine sits on the ground while three to five hooded men surround her and handle her. She does not stand up in the clip. The clip leaves out the earlier part of the same raw video, in which men stand over a person lying on the ground while a voice says "they're beating them." What the zoomed shot shows fits the account of Levine and of Jasper Nathaniel, an American journalist who reached her: an attacker pulled her up by the vest and another knocked her down again. It fits that account at least as well as it fits JSR's.
  • Disputed and not settled. The order of the first stones. Witnesses on the Palestinian side say the masked men began. The council says harvesters and activists threw stones and fireworks at the hilltop first. The footage examined here begins after both sides were throwing, and none of it shows fireworks.
  • Partly settled. The extent of Levine's injuries. Video and photographs show a bandaged hand and bleeding leg wounds. The Post, her employer, says she had lacerations, a broken rib, four broken vertebrae and a finger torn open to the bone that needs surgery. Nathaniel gives the same account. No hospital has published a statement. Police first described her injuries as light, and JSR's point that it saw only a bandaged finger is out of date.
  • Unknown. Who the attackers were: no arrests had been reported as of Saturday morning. Also unknown: whether soldiers saw the beating (the IDF says they did not), the name of the outpost, and whether any Palestinian Authority vehicles took part.

Place and land status

Khirbet Abu Falah lies northeast of Ramallah, between Turmus Ayya and al-Mughayyir. The groves where the clash took place lie on a slope below a hilltop outpost. The land is in Area B, where the Palestinian Authority has civil control and Israel has security control. Both the Binyamin Regional Council's statement ("this is Area B, where security responsibility is Israel's") and Haaretz's report (a group of settlers "coming down from an outpost in Area B toward the village lands") say so. The IDF, as quoted by N12, called the outpost "illegal." The Washington Post reports that the council named the place "Meshulash" (name unconfirmed).

The outpost is new. On 8 March 2026 settlers entered Area B near Abu Falah to set up an illegal outpost. Two villagers were shot dead and a third died after inhaling gas (Kan; AP; WAFA; the Guardian). The commander of Central Command, Avi Bluth, called the incident "unacceptable," and a criminal investigation was opened. In April, the Palestinian agency SAND reported that settlers were putting up tents and laying foundations at Ezbet Abu Zaatar ("al-Badoud") on the village's land (unconfirmed). Earlier, Arutz 7 reported on 4 January 2025, citing a security source, that Jewish rioters set fire to an empty house in the village (single source). On 6 October 2026, Elisha Yered and Elchanan Groner reported that people from Abu Falah spread poison on Israeli pastures near the Allon Road (unconfirmed).

Who farms the grove, and whether access was coordinated. The reports describe the grove only as land of Abu Falah families and give no owners' names. No report says whether the harvest was coordinated with the army. This year's new restrictions, which require army approval, cover Area C (AP; the Guardian), and this grove is in Area B. Israel Hayom printed a "settlers' version" about harvesters next to the fence of Shiloh on state land in Area C without approval. That describes a separate incident near Shiloh, which JSR reported separately, and not Abu Falah.

Who was there

  • Villagers of Abu Falah, including families picking olives and young men who threw stones back with slings (all accounts).
  • Journalists and activists: Heidi Levine (Washington Post); Jasper Nathaniel (American freelance journalist); Andrey Khrzhanovskiy ("Andrey X," an Israeli activist and video journalist); Oren Ziv (an independent Israeli journalist); Jonathan Pollak (an Israeli activist who accompanies harvesters); the photographer Avishay Mohar; and photographers for Reuters (Ammar Awad, Ali Sawafta) and AP (Leo Correa). No report names Rabbis for Human Rights, ISM, Ta'ayush or Combatants for Peace as present at Abu Falah that morning (unknown). The council refers to a "foreign activist."
  • Masked Israelis from the outpost: about 80 (Reuters), more than 50 (the Post), 50 (Levine), about 40 (the Prime Minister's Office).
  • Security forces: IDF troops and Border Police (the IDF; the council). Witnesses say jeeps passed during the first stone-throwing and later parked beside the masked men while firing tear gas at the Palestinians.
  • Palestinian Authority police vehicles: claimed by the council (unconfirmed; not seen in the footage examined).
  • Numbers on the Palestinian side: "hundreds" (the council); about 200 Arabs and far-left activists (the Prime Minister's Office).

The footage, frame by frame

Five widely shared clips were downloaded and examined at one to two frames per second, and their speech was transcribed. The links below lead to the original posts. The footage is copyrighted and is not reproduced here.

  1. Raw video from the Palestinian side, 78 seconds (Awdeh TV). It is a single phone recording with no visible cuts, although the camera turns into the trees at times. From 0:00 to 0:20 dozens of men in black hoods come down a dirt track in a body. Some swing slings, and one wears olive-colored clothing. Young Palestinians in the foreground throw stones toward them. From about 0:21 to 0:29, in the background on the upper track, a person lies on the ground while four to six hooded men stand over the person, and some of them bend down and strike. At 0:18 to 0:26 a man says in English, "It's the journalists … they're beating them." At about 0:40 the same voice says "They're kicking somebody, [s]he's down right now," while the camera shows the advancing men.
  2. JSR and Binyamin Council clip, 21 seconds (JSR_EN; JSR_HEB). The first 13 seconds are the same footage as about 0:40–0:53 of the raw video, with the shout "they're kicking somebody … they're killing [her]." At 0:13 the clip cuts to a zoomed shot, either from another camera or cropped. Levine, in a helmet, sits on the ground. Three to five hooded men surround her; one holds his hand at her head, and another reaches to her side, where her bag was. She is still sitting when the clip ends at 0:21. The clip does not show her being knocked down, and it does not show anyone helping her to her feet. It also leaves out the earlier part of the raw video described above.
  3. Compilation from the activist side, 52 seconds (Eye on Palestine; the original footage is from Khrzhanovskiy and Nathaniel). It is edited, with a caption and an inset. It shows masked men charging with slings, then Levine holding her camera, then a man in a PRESS vest running up the track to her as she sits alone on the road. He lifts her and walks her back. Voices shout "Run!" and "We need help here." This shows the end of the encounter, after the attackers had pulled back.
  4. Army clip, 11 seconds (Andrey X). Army jeeps drive along the road, and tear gas rises from the field. The clip does not show at whom the gas was aimed.
  5. JSR "additional footage," 44 seconds (JSR_EN). It is a montage of several Instagram clips and carries an Instagram watermark. It shows Palestinian youths slinging stones, smoke on the hillside and a reporter in a PRESS vest. None of the clips has a time stamp. No fireworks are visible in the frames examined. The montage cannot show who threw first.

Assessment. The raw video and the accounts of the witnesses agree. The JSR clip is a true excerpt, but its caption does not describe what it shows. The Prime Minister's Office says other footage shows Levine "falling and receiving immediate help from a person whose identity is unknown." No such footage has been published, so that claim cannot be tested.

Heidi Levine's injuries and property

  • Levine: she was pushed to the ground and kicked for about ten minutes, "probably 50 times." After that the attackers tried to stand her up in order to push her down and kick her again. They broke off her cameras, the bag holding her passport, and the phone on a cord around her neck (CNN; N12; Reuters). She says that her helmet and vest saved her.
  • Washington Post: she was treated at hospitals in Ramallah and in Israel for lacerations, a broken rib and four broken vertebrae, and needs surgery on a finger torn open to the bone. Nathaniel reports the same and adds "possible stab wounds" (stab wounds unconfirmed).
  • Images: CBS, AFP and Reuters show a bandaged left hand and bleeding wounds on the leg. CNN identifies the hospital as Ramallah Government Hospital, the Palestine Medical Complex.
  • Police: on the first day they described a foreign media worker as lightly injured (Israel Hayom; N12). No hospital statement has been published. A report on the Arabic site Wattan of fractured legs is unconfirmed, and the Post does not mention it.
  • Equipment: the cameras, phone, passport and wallet were taken (the Guardian; the Post). No report says they were returned. The Union of Journalists in Israel demanded their return (N12).

Statements by the authorities

  • IDF: forces responded to a violent confrontation, foreign media were present, and a media worker was hurt. Troops used riot-dispersal means. At another point they fired in the air and then at a suspect who ran at them with a sharp object. According to N12, the army said dozens of Jewish rioters came down from an illegal outpost and that stone-throwing between the sides began after the attack on the journalist. Lt. Col. Ariella Mazor told the Post that troops did not see the assault on Levine, that the army is examining what the soldiers did, and that improper conduct "will be dealt with."
  • Police (Judea and Samaria District): an investigation is open, and crime-scene investigators were to enter the area with an army escort. The police rejected claims of political bias and cited more than 200 arrests and more than 70 indictments in the district this year (N12). No arrests had been reported in this case by Saturday morning.
  • Border Police: no separate statement was found. The council says a Border Police force dispersed both sides.
  • Prime Minister's Office (in English, on X): it condemned violence against anyone. It cited reports that about 200 Arabs and far-left activists confronted about 40 Israeli youths and that masked people on both sides threw objects. It said the inquiry must establish who began the violence.

What is still unknown

  • The identity of the attackers, and whether any will be arrested.
  • Who threw the first stone, and whether any fireworks were fired.
  • Whether the soldiers who passed by saw the beating.
  • Whether Hassan held anything when he was shot.
  • Whether Levine's cameras and phone have been found.
  • Any medical record from the hospitals.
  • The name of the outpost.
  • Which activist groups, if any, organized the visit.
