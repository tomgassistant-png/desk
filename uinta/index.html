Uinta Mountains, Utah: easy 10-day trip plan

For Tom Goldberger. From Baltimore (BWI) or Washington Dulles (IAD). Reworked 28 October 2026 for a relaxed pace, comfortable hotels, and no strenuous activity.

Nothing is booked. No flight, car, room, tour, or permit has been reserved or held. This page is a plan only.
How to read the labels: Checked online 28 Oct means I read this on a public web page on 28 October 2026. Estimate means it is my guess or general knowledge, so please check it before you rely on it. Prices are not date-specific and will change when you search your exact dates.

Recommendation

Go in summer: 9–18 August 2027. All the roads and viewpoints are open, the days are long, and every sight in this plan is a short walk from a parking lot. Hot weather is the main cost: Vernal is hot in August Estimate.

Alternative: 19–28 November 2026. This fits between your Boston trip (ending 18 November) and your possible 1 December departure from Israel. It is a quieter trip with snow scenery and hot-spring soaks. Some places are closed for the season. Thanksgiving is 26 November, when the fossil museums are closed.

The one decision for Tom: summer 2027 (recommended) or the November 2026 version?

What changed from the first plan: Kings Peak, backpacking, and camping are removed. Every day now has at most about 3 hours of driving, a late start, and rest time. All lodging is a comfortable hotel or resort.

Day by day: summer version (9–18 August 2027)

Driving times are per day. Each day starts after breakfast, with rest in the afternoon.

  1. Mon 9 Aug. Fly to Salt Lake City (SLC), then Midway. Take a morning nonstop from BWI or IAD. Pick up the rental car. Drive to Midway, about 50–65 minutes (SLC to Heber is 50–65 minutes) Checked online 28 Oct. Check in at Zermatt Resort, use the pool, rest. Driving: about 1 hour.
  2. Tue 10 Aug. Rest day in Midway. Late breakfast. Soak at The Crater at Homestead (a warm mineral spring inside a rock dome; $30 per one-hour session, water about 96°F, life vest required and provided, book ahead) Checked online 28 Oct. Hotel pool and spa in the afternoon. Driving: about 10 minutes.
  3. Wed 11 Aug. Park City. Stroll Main Street, which is flat in its main blocks Estimate, with a lunch stop. Heber to Park City is about 25 minutes Checked online 28 Oct. Return to Midway for rest. Driving: about 1 hour total.
  4. Thu 12 Aug. Mirror Lake Highway scenic drive. Drive from Kamas on Utah 150. Stops: Provo River Falls (under 150 yards from the parking area to the overlook) Checked online 28 Oct; Mirror Lake shoreline loop (about 1.5 miles, mostly flat, with boardwalks; some sources say up to 2.1 miles) Checked online 28 Oct, which you can shorten to any length; the Bald Mountain Pass overlook at 10,715 feet Checked online 28 Oct. You can do the entire day from the car and short strolls. Take the high stops slowly because of altitude (see Safety). Fee: a recreation pass is required at fee sites Checked online 28 Oct. Driving: about 3 hours total Estimate.
  5. Fri 13 Aug. Drive to Vernal. Take US-40 through Duchesne, Roosevelt, and Fort Duchesne. About 3 hours Estimate. This road crosses the Uintah Basin, the area in the UFO section below. Stop in Roosevelt for lunch. Check in at the Dinosaur Inn & Suites or Holiday Inn Express.
  6. Sat 14 Aug. Utah Field House of Natural History, Vernal. It is a dinosaur museum with a dinosaur garden, $10 for adults, open 9 AM–7 PM daily in summer Checked online 28 Oct. It also has a free visitor center Checked online 28 Oct. Afternoon rest. Optional: the UFO Valley mesa tour (see the UFO section). Driving: under 30 minutes.
  7. Sun 15 Aug. Dinosaur National Monument. Quarry Exhibit Hall (about 1,500 dinosaur bones in a rock wall; you can touch some real fossils) Checked online 28 Oct. Swelter Shelter petroglyphs, a 400-foot round-trip walk with a few stone steps Checked online 28 Oct. Then the Tour of Tilted Rocks, a 10-mile one-way scenic drive; the first 8 miles are paved, and the last 2 are maintained gravel that is not recommended for low-clearance cars Checked online 28 Oct. The monument entrance is $25 per vehicle Checked online 28 Oct. After dinner and a rest, return after dark to the Split Mountain Campground stargazing area (see the Night sky section). Driving: about 1.5 hours total including the evening trip Estimate.
  8. Mon 16 Aug. Flaming Gorge-Uintas National Scenic Byway. Drive US-191 north from Vernal about 35 miles, then Utah 44 to the Red Canyon Overlook Checked online 28 Oct. Walk the paved overlook paths. Visit the Red Canyon Visitor Center and the dam visitor center. Optional: a scenic boat tour from Cedar Springs Marina (see Do you need a guide). Option A: stop after the dam. Option B: add the Sheep Creek Geological Loop, a 13-mile scenic drive through tilted rock layers, mostly passenger-car friendly in dry weather, 1–2 hours Checked online 28 Oct; skip the dam if you pick B. Driving: about 2.5–3 hours for Option A; about 4.5 hours for Option B Estimate.
  9. Tue 17 Aug. Drive back to Midway. About 3 hours Estimate. Second Crater soak or hotel spa.
  10. Wed 18 Aug. Fly home. Midway to SLC is 50–65 minutes Checked online 28 Oct. Take a midday or afternoon nonstop to BWI or IAD.

The UFO angle: Professor Frank B. Salisbury and the Uintah Basin

I separated this section into three kinds of statements. Documented facts come from library records, books, or institutional pages. Witness claims are what people said they saw; they are not proven. Speculation is an explanation or opinion that nobody has proved. Spelling note: the basin and county are written "Uintah"; the mountains are written "Uinta".

Documented facts
  • Who he was. Frank Boyer Salisbury was born in Utah in 1926 and earned a Ph.D. at the California Institute of Technology in 1955. He was a plant physiologist. He taught at Colorado State University from 1955 to 1966, then at Utah State University from 1966, where he led the Department of Plant Science from 1966 to 1970. He retired in 1997 after leading a project to grow wheat on the Russian space station Mir Checked online 28 Oct (Sign Oral History Project; Library of Congress authority record).
  • How he got involved. His UFO interest grew from his interest in life on other planets. He published "Martian Biology" in Science in April 1962 Checked online 28 Oct. In October 1966, after a talk on Mars to the Association of Utah Science Teachers, Joseph Junior Hicks, a junior high science teacher from Roosevelt, Utah, introduced himself and described sightings in Roosevelt and Vernal Checked online 28 Oct (Salisbury's own book excerpt; Sign Oral History Project).
  • The book. "The Utah UFO Display: A Biologist's Report" was published in 1974 by Devin-Adair, Old Greenwich, Connecticut, with data from the files of Joseph Junior Hicks and a foreword by J. Allen Hynek. It has 286 pages plus plates Checked online 28 Oct (Brigham Young University library catalog record). It was reissued around 2010 Checked online 28 Oct; a publisher page describes "over 400 sightings" Checked online 28 Oct.
  • When he visited. He arrived in the Uintah Basin on 13 June 1968 and began interviewing witnesses Checked online 28 Oct (book excerpt; his 1975 BioScience article).
  • The case file. In his 1975 article he wrote that Hicks's file held 80 cases, and he summarized 7 of them plus 1 reported directly to him Checked online 28 Oct (copy of the article hosted by a third-party archive, rr0.org; I did not see the original journal page).
  • The place. The basin is about 75 miles long, 5,000 to 6,000 feet in elevation, a dry high desert, with US-40 running east to west. A map in the book shows sightings clustered around Roosevelt and Fort Duchesne, with others around Vernal and along US-40 Checked online 28 Oct (book excerpt).
  • His papers. Utah State University's Special Collections in Logan holds the Frank Boyer Salisbury papers, including UFO material and 1974 drafts of the book Checked online 28 Oct. Logan is far from this trip, so it is not in the plan.
Witness claims, as recorded by Salisbury and Hicks (not proven)
  • On 26 September 1966, Kent Denver and friends said they watched a red ball of fire move around on the horizon for about 30 minutes Checked online 28 Oct.
  • Thyrena Daniels said a large red glowing sphere followed her car on the roughly 30-mile drive from Vernal to Roosevelt Checked online 28 Oct.
  • One red glowing object was said to be seen by at least 40 scattered witnesses across the basin, including night-shift workers at a phosphate plant Checked online 28 Oct.
  • Three brothers named Ercanbrack said they saw a large silver object in a field in daylight and approached it Checked online 28 Oct.
  • Salisbury's own opinion, from his 1975 article: the cases have "such a high strangeness index that it seems virtually impossible to think of them as misinterpreted natural phenomena or as secret weapons." He also wrote that he could not accept that the witnesses were lying or hallucinating, "although I cannot prove that they were not" Checked online 28 Oct. This is his judgment, not a proven result.
  • One secondary source says about 89 percent of the sightings in his count fell between summer 1965 and winter 1968, peaking in late 1966 and 1967. I found this in one place only, so treat it as unconfirmed.
Speculation and open questions
  • Nobody has shown what the lights were. No physical evidence from the 1960s wave is confirmed Estimate. One secondary source says a later scientific paper proposed that swarms of spruce budworm moths lit by electric fields caused some sightings; I found that in one place only, so it is unconfirmed.
  • Skinwalker Ranch is a different story. It is a private cattle ranch near Ballard and Fort Duchesne. One source says its UFO claims were first published in a Utah newspaper in 1996 Checked online 28 Oct. That is after Salisbury's 1974 book, so do not assume his cases were at the ranch. This is my reading of the dates. The ranch's claims (strange lights, animals, cattle deaths) are witness claims. One source says several funded studies produced no peer-reviewed evidence Checked online 28 Oct. One person reported that the county sheriff's office had no records of visits to the ranch for the period it keeps records Checked online 28 Oct; I could not confirm that independently.

Can you visit Skinwalker Ranch?

No. It is private property. Several independent pages say it is fenced, guarded, and closed to the public, with no public tours Checked online 28 Oct. I could not load the ranch's own website on 28 October, so I could not read its statement directly. Do not drive to the gate and do not enter the property. The ranch borders land of the Ute Indian Tribe of the Uintah and Ouray Reservation, so visit the area with respect Checked online 28 Oct.

A UFO-themed route that fits the easy plan

  • Before the trip: read Salisbury's book. A library copy can be borrowed from the Internet Archive Checked online 28 Oct, and the reissue is sold as an ebook Checked online 28 Oct.
  • Day 5 (summer) or Day 5 (November): drive US-40 through Duchesne, Roosevelt, and Fort Duchesne. These are the towns at the center of the 1960s reports. Roosevelt is where Hicks taught. I could not verify any UFO museum, marker, or festival in Roosevelt, Fort Duchesne, Hanna, or the Uintah County library, so I do not list any. A photo caption on a Utah tourism page mentions a "Phenomecon" convention in Vernal, but I could not verify its dates Estimate.
  • Vernal: ask at the free visitor center inside the Utah Field House for current local events Checked online 28 Oct. It is a natural history museum and has no verified UFO exhibit.
  • Optional paid tour: UFO Valley Campground & UTV Tours in Fort Duchesne runs a 2-hour guided tour on a 4-mile off-road trail on the mesa with elevated views of the Skinwalker Ranch area, for $180 per person Checked online 28 Oct. It is a rough off-road ride and is not required. See the next section.

The night sky

  • Best car-reachable spot: Dinosaur National Monument. It was certified as an International Dark Sky Park on 22 April 2019 Checked online 28 Oct. It can be reached 24 hours a day for stargazing Checked online 28 Oct. The monument has a designated stargazing spot at the back of the Split Mountain Campground, which you can drive to Checked online 28 Oct. From Vernal, the Quarry area is about 20 minutes Checked online 28 Oct; Split Mountain is somewhat farther Estimate.
  • Ranger night sky programs run on select nights, usually in summer, for 20–60 minutes, with telescopes when weather and staffing allow Checked online 28 Oct. Check the monument calendar for August 2027 dates. The Quarry Exhibit Hall itself is closed at night Checked online 28 Oct.
  • Tips from the park service: let your eyes adjust for 30 minutes, use a red light, avoid the full moon for faint objects Checked online 28 Oct.
  • November: the sky is dark, but expect 20–30°F at night Estimate. Bring warm layers.

Safety

  • Altitude: Mirror Lake Highway reaches 10,715 feet at Bald Mountain Pass Checked online 28 Oct. Mirror Lake is about 10,000 feet Estimate. With an easy plan the risk is lower, but some people feel headache, nausea, or dizziness above 8,000 feet. Do the mountain day on day 4 after three low-elevation days, walk slowly, drink water, and go down the road if you feel unwell. Red Canyon Overlook is 7,417 feet Checked online 28 Oct. Vernal is about 5,300 feet Estimate.
  • Heat (summer): basin temperatures can pass 90°F in August Estimate. Walk early, carry water.
  • Storms: summer afternoon thunderstorms happen in the mountains Estimate. Leave the lakeshore if you hear thunder.
  • Winter driving: check UDOT conditions each morning. Carry water and fuel. Mountain roads are not plowed past the winter gate, and there is no help or cell coverage there Checked online 28 Oct. Avoid night driving on mountain roads Estimate.
  • Petroglyphs: do not touch them; skin oils damage them Checked online 28 Oct.
  • Hot-spring soak: keep sessions short, drink water, and ask a doctor first if you have a heart condition Checked online 28 Oct.

Carry-on packing list (no mountaineering gear)

  • Layers: T-shirts, a fleece, a light jacket, a rain shell.
  • Comfortable walking shoes (wear them on the plane); wool socks.
  • Sun hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, a refillable water bottle.
  • A swimsuit for hotel pools and the Crater; a light towel.
  • Small binoculars for viewing petroglyphs from a distance and for stargazing; a red-light flashlight or headlamp.
  • November additions: warm coat, hat, gloves, waterproof walking shoes, hand warmers.
  • Photo ID, medicines, and a small daypack. Liquids over 3.4 ounces and knives do not go in a carry-on.
