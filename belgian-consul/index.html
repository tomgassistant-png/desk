The Belgian Consul General in Jerusalem

Prepared October 18, 2026. Items marked unconfirmed rest on a single source or on a source we could not open.

Key points

  • The Belgian Consul General in Jerusalem is Anick Van Calster, a career diplomat. She took up the post in August 2025 and presented her letter of appointment to Mahmoud Abbas in early October 2025 (her own LinkedIn posts; Wallonia's export agency AWEX repeated the second).
  • The consulate is not accredited to Israel. Belgium's own history page says its district is all of Jerusalem plus the West Bank and Gaza, that since Oslo it is Belgium's political representation to the Palestinian Authority, and that relations with Israel belong only to the embassy in Tel Aviv. It also handles Belgian development aid and UNRWA.
  • Taybeh, October 17, 2026: Van Calster was in the European delegation. Belgium's Foreign Ministry spokesman told Belga that she and a second Belgian diplomat were present and unhurt, and that a window of "the consul general's vehicle" was broken. Who used the damaged car is unclear: the Guardian says the French mayor Bernard Baudoux was by "a vehicle with diplomatic plates," and its photo caption calls the damaged car the mayor's. No Belgian official called the car armored (see section 6). The IDF and Foreign Minister Sa'ar said the visit was not coordinated with the army. Israel Police sources told Ynet that the delegations had not cooperated with requests for statements, footage and access to the vehicles.
  • Abu Falah, October 16: No report we found places a Belgian diplomat there.
  • Israeli criticism of Belgium centers on its development money. NGO Monitor documents Belgian state funds passed through Belgian NGOs to Palestinian groups that Israel designated as PFLP fronts (Bisan, Health Work Committees, DCI-P). Israel summoned Belgian envoys over this in 2020, and over other matters in 2017, in August 2023 over a minister's remark, and in November 2023 over Gaza. Belgium, for its part, cut off school construction funding in 2017–2018 after a Belgian-built school was renamed for the terrorist Dalal Mughrabi.

Her deputy is Nadia Benini (WAFA, October 1, 2026). On October 1, 2026, the two met PA Vice President Hussein al-Sheikh in Ramallah. According to WAFA, the official Palestinian agency, they discussed settler attacks, settlement growth, Israel's withholding of PA tax money, the war in Gaza and the coming Palestinian elections, and the consul "reaffirmed her country's continued support for the Palestinian people." WAFA calls her "Consul General to the State of Palestine." That is WAFA's wording; Belgium has announced recognition but has not formalized it (see section 5).

Her public statements are few and mild. Her LinkedIn post of October 2025 said: "We hope that we are at a turning point today, at the beginning of a road leading to peace, security and prosperity for all in the region." We did not find a consulate X account with statements by her on settlements or Gaza. A full review of the consulate's social media was not done.

2. The consulate: role and history

From Belgium's own history page (last updated August 1, 2023):

  • An honorary consulate opened in 1851. Its first purpose was to restore the monuments of Godfrey of Bouillon and Baldwin of Flanders in the Church of the Holy Sepulchre. A career consulate was set up in the early 1920s so Belgium could take part in a planned Catholic commission of consuls, and the present Consulate General opened on March 1, 1941.
  • The district follows the 1947 UN partition plan (Resolution 181), which called Jerusalem a corpus separatum under international rule. The page notes that the plan "was never applied." The West Bank was added in 1948 and Gaza in 1967. It is one of nine consulates general in Jerusalem that report directly to their capitals.
  • After Oslo (1993), it became Belgium's political representation to the PA. "Diplomatic relations with the State of Israel are the exclusive domain of the Belgian embassy in Tel Aviv." Since 2008, the consul general has also been Belgium's representative to UNRWA.

The building is at 5 Baibars Street in Sheikh Jarrah, East Jerusalem. Enabel, the Belgian development agency, has its Palestine office at the same address (Enabel). The Custody of the Holy Land notes a separate, religious side of the post: the consul attends an annual Mass for the Belgian king, a remnant of Belgium's traditional role toward the Catholic communities.

Development aid. Enabel says Belgium has spent 144 million euros on projects in Jerusalem, the West Bank and Gaza since 1997, and that the 2022–2026 program totals 70 million euros. The previous consul general, Wilfried Pfeffer, signed that program with PA Prime Minister Shtayyeh in Ramallah on March 9, 2022 (WAFA). Pfeffer's tenure began in 2021 and ran at least to mid-2024 (Palestinian media). His exact dates are unconfirmed.

4. NGO funding: what NGO Monitor documents

Belgium does not fund Palestinian or Israeli NGOs directly. It co-funds Belgian NGOs (up to 85 percent of a grant), and they pass the money to local partners (NGO Monitor). According to NGO Monitor's report (November 2020, updated July 2021), for 2017–2021:

  • About 9.5 million euros went to Belgian NGOs for "Jerusalem & Palestinian Territory." At least 3 million of that went to projects with Palestinian NGOs that NGO Monitor ties to the PFLP.
  • Via Viva Salud: Bisan Center 337,892 euros; Health Work Committees 297,925 euros; Union of Health Work Committees 192,823 euros. Via Broederlijk Delen: DCI-P 170,800 euros. Via Solidarité Socialiste: Ma'an Development Center about 987,000 euros. Via Oxfam Solidarité: a project with the Union of Agricultural Work Committees (UAWC).
  • UAWC's financial director, Samer Arbid, is accused of making and setting off the August 2019 bomb that killed Rina Shnerb. The finance director of the Health Work Committees, Walid Hanatsheh, is accused of commanding him. Israel designated these groups as terrorist organizations in 2020–2021.
  • The 2017–2021 Joint Strategic Framework for Belgian aid to the Palestinians, approved by the development minister, set the goal of "mitigating the influence of pro-Israel voices." Belgium has not published the 2022–2026 framework.
  • In 2021, Development Minister Meryame Kitir said Israel's information "contains no concrete material evidence" of fraud and refused to freeze funds or order an outside audit.

These figures are NGO Monitor's, drawn from Belgian government and NGO documents. We did not check them against the Belgian DGD database ourselves. The Belgian NGOs and the Palestinian groups reject the terror designations, and several European governments said Israel's evidence was not sufficient. Those are their claims.

5. Belgium's current policy, in brief

On September 2, 2025, the government of Bart De Wever agreed on a package (Foreign Minister Maxime Prévot's paper):

  • Recognition of Palestine announced at the UN in September 2025, but formalized by royal decree only "once the last hostage has been released and terrorist organisations such as Hamas have been removed from the administration of Palestine." An embassy would follow only after Hamas is disarmed and elections are held.
  • Ben-Gvir and Smotrich, Hamas leaders, and violent settlers on EU lists declared persona non grata.
  • A planned national ban on imports from the settlements; consular services for Belgians who live in settlements limited to emergencies; a legal study of refusing long-stay visas to settlers; no Israeli military overflights; prosecution of Belgians who commit serious violations of humanitarian law.
  • Support for suspending the trade part of the EU–Israel Association Agreement and Israel's role in Horizon Europe.
  • The same paper says Belgium will step up its protection against antisemitic threats, and calls for listing the IRGC as a terrorist group.

6. Taybeh, October 17, 2026: the Belgian role

Israeli account first. The IDF said the consular visit was not coordinated with it in advance and that no soldiers were at the spot. Foreign Minister Sa'ar condemned "Israeli rioters" but added that coordination "could have helped safeguard the visitors' security" (BBC; Ynet). The Prime Minister's Office said the delegation "entered a sensitive area with a history of confrontations without prior coordination," pointed out that Baudoux is a Communist mayor who once raised the Palestinian flag over his town hall, said the images "do not establish the complete sequence of events," and warned of "a coordinated campaign to smear Israel" during the harvest (PMO on X, quoted in full by Channel 14). The PMO gave no evidence for the campaign claim.

Israel Police sources told Ynet on October 18 that the diplomats had not cooperated. Investigators asked for detailed statements, footage, access to the vehicles and forensic samples, and as of Saturday evening, October 17, they said, the missions had not given testimony, footage, access to the vehicles or permission for forensic tests. No arrests had been reported as of Sunday morning, October 18.

The Belgian account. Prévot condemned the attack "by masked settlers" on Palestinians, "our Consul General," French and Polish diplomats and Catholic representatives. He tied it to "Israel's illegal settlement policy" and said Belgium will keep pressing for EU sanctions (RTBF/Belga; Ynet). His spokesman, Laurens Soenen, said the delegation had joined the olive harvest "as a symbolic gesture of support to the local population," that several masked settlers armed with sticks attacked, and that "a window of the consul general's vehicle was broken." Van Calster and a second Belgian diplomat were unhurt.

The damaged car (corrected October 18). Belgium's foreign ministry spokesman, Laurens Soenen, told Belga that "une vitre du véhicule de la consule générale a été brisée" (a window of the consul general's vehicle was broken; in Dutch, "werd ingeslagen") (La Libre; HLN). Minister Prévot said only that "a Belgian vehicle was damaged." Who the car belonged to is unclear: the spokesman calls it the consul general's vehicle, while The Guardian says Baudoux was by "a vehicle with diplomatic plates," and its photo caption calls the damaged car "mayor Badoux's car." No Belgian official called the car armored; only the Guardian's and HLN's own text say so. The Guardian's photo shows a silver SUV whose rear left quarter window is cracked in a spiderweb pattern around a hole about the size of a fist. A hole through the glass suggests that this window was not ballistic glass, or that it took many blows; that is our inference and is unconfirmed. The weapons reported are sticks, clubs, stones and crowbars; no firearms or explosives were reported (The Guardian; Ynet). An earlier version of this page said, wrongly, that the Guardian reported Baudoux was trying to get into an armored Belgian car when he was knocked down.

Other details. The Guardian puts the French consul general in Jerusalem and his wife in the group too, along with the archbishop of Cambrai. A diplomat told Ynet that the "past politics of the mayor are not relevant to the question of who attacked him." A Western diplomatic source told the Guardian that requiring advance notice violates diplomatic convention. That is the diplomats' position. Israel's position is that coordination is how it protects visitors in Areas B and C.

Context the wire reports leave out. Taybeh has been closed to Israelis by military order since August 9, 2026 (see the Desk's earlier editions). The visit was arranged around a twinning ceremony between Taybeh and Baudoux's town, Aulnoye-Aymeries, together with activists. That makes it a political visit, not a consular one. None of this excuses the assault, which the IDF, the police and the Foreign Minister all condemned.

What is not known

  • Whether the Belgian consulate organized the visit or only joined it.
  • Whether any Israeli authority was told of the visit in any form.
  • Van Calster's full career record before 2021, apart from the 2012 Sofia decree.
  • Whether the Belgian mission has since agreed to give police statements or its footage.
