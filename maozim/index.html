The Ma'ozim Road (Beitunia bypass) and its link to the Wallerstein Road

Prepared October 14, 2026. Items marked unconfirmed rest on a single source.

Key points

  • The "Ma'ozim axis" (Hebrew: ציר המעוזים, also "Derech HaMa'ozim," the Ma'ozim Road) is not a formal government plan with a published document. It is the name given by residents to an old bypass road around the Palestinian town of Beitunia. The road leads from the Dolev area in the Dolev–Talmonim bloc of western Binyamin south to Route 443 and on to Jerusalem (Arutz 7; HaKol HaYehudi; i24NEWS).
  • Israelis were barred from the road after the Second Intifada began in 2000. Residents began to drive on it again on their own initiative in 2026. Most of it lies in Area C, with a few hundred meters in Area B (HaKol HaYehudi; i24NEWS).
  • On October 8, 2026, Israel Ganz, head of the Binyamin Regional Council and chairman of the Yesha Council, told Olam Katan that the Wallerstein Road (Route 463) "is joining" the Ma'ozim axis and that the IDF has begun work toward opening it. He said the work is budgeted and will take "several months." No government decision, budget figure or date has been published.

1. The name

The road is named for two residents of Talmon, Maoz Fenigstein and Maoz Morel, who fell in combat in the Gaza Strip during the Swords of Iron war. Their parents unveiled a road sign at a ceremony held by residents of the Dolev–Talmonim bloc (i24NEWS). The ceremony date and the names are unconfirmed by a second source.

2. Route and history

Until the mid-1990s, residents of Dolev and Talmon reached Jerusalem on a route that passed the edges of Ramallah and Beitunia and the villages of Ein Arik and Deir Ibzi'. After the Oslo Accords placed Ramallah and Beitunia in Area A, a bypass of Beitunia was used. After 2000 Israelis were sent west to Route 446 and Shilat Junction, a journey of more than an hour (i24NEWS; +972 Magazine, August 27, 2020). i24NEWS gives the distances as 11 km before Oslo, 13 km on the bypass (35 minutes), and 37 km by Route 446 (one hour and ten minutes). These exact figures are unconfirmed.

A formal road along this corridor, Road 935, has been planned since the 1990s. According to +972 Magazine, it was to run from the "Horseshoe Junction" near Dolev and Deir Ibzi' to Route 443 near Beit Ur al-Fauqa and Beit Horon. About 150 acres were expropriated from Deir Ibzi', Ein Arik and Beitunia, and work began on a short stretch, but it stopped during the Second Intifada. A 2007 petition by residents, led by Adi Mintz of Dolev, asked the High Court to order the road completed. The state opposed it on security, archaeological and property grounds, and the court dismissed the petition in 2009. In 2012 the expropriation was cancelled in a case brought by Yesh Din. In 2020 the Transportation Ministry said Road 935 was "in the initial planning process." These court and ministry details come from +972 Magazine only and are unconfirmed. Whether today's Ma'ozim Road follows the same line as the planned Road 935 is also unconfirmed.

3. Events of 2026

  • Early 2026. Residents resumed driving on the road. HaKol HaYehudi and Hityashvut Achshav link this to new farms set up between western Binyamin and Route 443, among them Beit Karnayim farm, and to the new community of Ma'oz Tzur. The road is described as a main route for these sites.
  • Night of August 23–24. Palestinian Authority police at a checkpoint near the Beitunia junction detained Shlomo Rosenthal, a reservist in civilian clothes who was driving on the road, took his weapon, and drove him toward Ramallah before handing him to the IDF. The IDF then closed the road to Israelis and changed the lock on its gate (Arutz 7; HaKol HaYehudi; i24NEWS).
  • August 24. Border Police blocked a convoy of protesters near Dolev under a closed military zone order; some entered the road on foot (Arutz 7).
  • August 26. An IDF inquiry found that the checkpoint stood in Area C, where the PA police had no authority, and that they detained Rosenthal unlawfully. This reversed an earlier account that the police had rescued him (Arutz 7).
  • Early September. The IDF arrested the three PA policemen (Arutz 7, September 9). Rabbis of the Dolev–Talmonim bloc wrote to the IDF asking that the road be reopened (HaKol HaYehudi).
  • September 29. Hundreds of residents held a holiday prayer service, secured by the IDF, at the Beitunia traffic circle on the road, and demanded that both the Ma'ozim Road and the Wallerstein Road be reopened (Hityashvut Achshav; i24NEWS).
  • October 8. Mr. Ganz's interview (Olam Katan). October 9. Hundreds walked the Wallerstein Road from Beit El to Dolev (Hityashvut Achshav).

4. The connection to the Wallerstein Road

The Wallerstein Road (Route 463) runs east–west from Nili Junction to Route 466 near al-Bireh, linking western and eastern Binyamin. It was closed to Israelis in the Second Intifada. Together the two roads would give the Talmonim bloc short links both to Beit El in the east and to Route 443 and Jerusalem in the south. In his words to Olam Katan: "The army needs to move military weight and deploy in the area differently... bases are being moved, equipment is being moved. It is a matter of several months; it is already budgeted... The principal decision was taken; there is work on it, including legal work." Who took this decision, when, and the budget amount are not stated in any source found, and the IDF has not commented publicly.

5. Views

Supporters. Residents, the Binyamin Regional Council and the bloc's rabbis argue that the roads shorten travel greatly, that the land is Area C where Israelis may lawfully travel, and that the PA police detention showed the need for full Israeli security control. The council has called its program a "road revolution" to make up for years of neglect, and says the roads will serve Arab residents too (+972 Magazine).

Opponents. Dror Etkes of Kerem Navot said in 2020 that such roads are the infrastructure for settling hundreds of thousands more Israelis and amount to annexation in practice. Residents of Ein Arik said they would resist a road on their land. In 2009 the state itself told the High Court that the road would be hard to secure and would harm private Palestinian property disproportionately (+972 Magazine). Peace Now and Kerem Navot's 2026 report on settlement activity criticizes new bypass roads in general. No statement by Peace Now or Kerem Navot specifically on the Ma'ozim Road in 2026 was found.

6. Map

No map is included. The exact line of the road could not be located reliably from the sources, so drawing one would risk error. It runs, in general terms, from the Dolev area south past the west side of Beitunia to Route 443.
