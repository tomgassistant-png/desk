Jonathan Pollak

Prepared October 18, 2026. Items marked unconfirmed rest on a single source, on Wikipedia alone, or on a source we could not open.

Key points

  • Pollak, born about 1982 in Tel Aviv, is an anarchist and anti-Zionist activist. He co-founded Anarchists Against the Wall (2003) and was for years the media coordinator of the Palestinian Popular Struggle Coordination Committee. He was a graphic designer, later chief digital designer, at Haaretz.
  • He says openly that Israeli courts are illegitimate, refuses to defend himself, and has called on Israeli Jews to "join the children of stones and Molotov cocktails." The State Attorney indicted him for incitement over those words in July 2024.
  • He has been arrested dozens of times and convicted four times (+972 Magazine, 2023). The convictions are for illegal assembly and interfering with police, not for violence. In January 2023, he was indicted for assaulting a police officer and throwing stones at a Border Police jeep at Beita. We could not find the outcome of that case or of the 2024 incitement case.
  • He was in Abu Falah on October 16, 2026, and gave the AP its account that soldiers "stood literally shoulder-to-shoulder with settlers." He is an advocate, not a neutral witness, and he says so himself.

1. Biography and family

  • Family. He is the son of the actor Yossi Pollak (Makor Rishon/NRG, 2010, which also notes that his father came to his sentencing). Hebrew and English Wikipedia name his mother as Tami, a psychologist, his half-brothers as the actor Avshalom Pollak and the director Shai Carmeli-Pollak, and his maternal grandfather as Nimrod Eshel, leader of the seamen's strike of the early 1950s. These family details beyond his father are from Wikipedia and the sources it cites; we did not confirm them independently. Israel Hayom's indictment report names him "Jonathan Pollak Pasternak."
  • Early activism. +972 says he co-founded One Struggle, an anarchist group that linked animal rights with other causes, in the early 2000s. He is a vegan. Hebrew Wikipedia says he was active in the anti-globalization movement in Europe and was deported from the Netherlands in 2002. Unconfirmed.
  • Haaretz. The Times of Israel (2020) and HaKol HaYehudi (2021) describe him as a Haaretz graphic designer, the latter as its chief digital designer. In 2019, two men attacked him with a knife outside the Haaretz offices and cut his face (+972). By July 2024, News1 described him as a "former" chief digital designer at Haaretz, and Ynet said the 2020 column was written "while he worked" there. When and why he left Haaretz is unconfirmed. In January 2025, Haaretz published a long piece by him on Palestinian prisoners.

2. Activist history

  • 2003. Founding member of Anarchists Against the Wall at a protest camp in Mas'ha (+972; Hebrew Wikipedia). The group joined Palestinian protests against the security barrier at Budrus, Bil'in, Ni'lin and other villages for about a decade.
  • April 2005. Hit in the head by a tear gas canister at Bil'in and hospitalized (+972). The IDF said the canister hit a rock first. The IDF account rests on Wikipedia's citation of Haaretz.
  • 2009 onward. Weekly protests at Nabi Saleh against the settlers' use of the spring near Halamish. Soldiers arrested him there in January 2010 (+972 photo caption).
  • Also: Sheikh Jarrah, Jaffa, Kafr Qaddum, Burqa (2019) and, from 2021, almost every Friday at Beita, at the protests against the Evyatar outpost (+972).
  • 2018–2020. Ad Kan filed a private criminal complaint against him and two other activists, Kobi Snitz and Ilan Shalif, saying they attacked soldiers and Border Police officers. He refused to appear and was arrested in January 2020. He stayed in custody for about six weeks, until the Attorney General stayed the private case (+972). Defense Minister Bennett ordered West Bank exclusion orders against the group's members in January 2020 and accused them of "violent provocations" each week (Times of Israel).
  • September 6, 2024. At the Beita protest where the Turkish-American ISM activist Aysenur Ezgi Eygi was killed, Pollak was the AP's main witness. He said soldiers on a rooftop fired after the clashes had ended. The IDF said troops had fired at an "instigator of violent activity" (AP via PBS). The IDF later said she was probably hit by mistake. That later IDF finding is from an AP report we could not open.
  • August 2026. As a member of the "Sumud Network," he told Al Jazeera that soldiers kept activists from bringing aid to families at Qusra whom settlers had surrounded.

4. His own words

  • Haaretz, 2020 ("I'm Going to Prison Again"): "We must cross the lines and break the law. Despite the price, we must join the children of stones and Molotov cocktails. We must walk in their path." An editor removed the second and third sentences a few hours later (Ynet; Times of Israel). About 12,000 people read the piece (News1).
  • Leibowitz Prize speech, June 2021, Tzavta club, Tel Aviv: "Between the river and the sea there is one colonialist regime that is entirely illegitimate." He compared Israelis who oppose it to white South Africans who joined the ANC, "some of them even took up arms," and said what is needed is "to walk beside the children with stones and Molotov cocktails ... whether in Beita, Bab al-Amud, or a few kilometers from here in Jaffa" (+972; Arutz 7; Ynet).
  • Court, September 2023: "No voice is louder than the voice of the uprising!" He asked to be tried in a military court, as Palestinians are; the court refused (support-jonathan.com).
  • On the 2024 indictment: "political persecution in the service of right-wing organizations" (Ynet).
  • Abu Falah, October 16, 2026: "There was absolutely no motive except one simple thing: to send a message that people cannot go to their land" (AP via the Los Angeles Times).
  • He supports BDS (Times of Israel). His X posts were not reviewed for this page.

5. Abu Falah and 2026

At Abu Falah, AP reports that Pollak said settlers came down from a hilltop outpost "throwing stones, burning trees and firing gunshots," that soldiers stood with them, and that soldiers later shot a council member, Abdel Jaber Hassan. The IDF said the man was running at troops with a sharp object. The Desk's Abu Falah backgrounder lists him among the committed activists present. The Binyamin Regional Council and the Yesha Council's Situation Room said "anarchists" came with Palestinians to provoke a clash under cover of the harvest. Pollak's presence is established. That he organized or led anything that day is not shown by any source we found.

6. Israeli criticism

  • Ad Kan (Gilad Ach, 2021): Pollak "has organized and led riots throughout Judea and Samaria for over a decade, in which many soldiers were hurt," as part of organizations "funded by Europeans" whose aim is "the destruction of the Jewish state" (HaKol HaYehudi).
  • The Defense Ministry (Bennett, 2020): the anarchists stage "violent provocations" meant to hurt soldiers and cause "serious public relations damage" to Israel (Times of Israel).
  • Liran Baruch of the IDF Disabled Forum (linked to Im Tirtzu), 2023: "Let every anarchist who raises his hand against the security forces and the State of Israel know that we will settle accounts with him" (quoted by +972).
  • Israel Hayom and Arutz 7 call him a "far-left activist." Right-wing groups have published footage of him blocking roads and carrying tires for burning. +972 says that footage shows no violence by him.
