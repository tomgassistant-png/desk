Israel election

NewChannel 13: the joint investigation team told Sohlberg there is still no evidence of security offenses, and Shin Bet chief Zini is unhappy that Sohlberg backs the attorney general; a democracy group asks that Netanyahu–Zini contacts be preserved and examined

Shin Bet sources quoted by Channel 13 said Shin Bet chief David Zini was "very unhappy" that Sohlberg sided with the attorney general and complained that she still "ties his hands"; Sohlberg explained to him that there is cooperation. Unnamed officials involved told the channel that Zini "drove a whole country crazy" without evidence that would justify summoning Arab lawmakers or show foreign interference. These are anonymous accounts, not findings. Separately, The Seventh Eye (a media-watchdog site, an interested party) reported on 13 November that the Israeli Democracy Guard (משמר הדמוקרטיה הישראלית, an advocacy group and also an interested party), through lawyers Doron Barkat and Ayala Honigman, wrote on 5 November to the legal advisers of the Shin Bet and the Prime Minister's Office demanding that records of meetings and calls between Prime Minister Netanyahu and Zini, from two weeks before the election to days after it, be preserved and examined. The letter cites Zini's 27 July statement to a Knesset subcommittee about separation between him and the prime minister and a report that the information behind the election-day arrests came from the Prime Minister's Office, through Ido Norden to police chief Danny Levi; the group itself says these developments "do not in themselves show" improper interference. This is a demand letter, not a complaint to police or a court filing. Likud: no appeal filing was found; Maariv reported on 11 November that Likud plans to file on 18 November, and the deadline is not yet confirmed in an official record. Dated history: 11 November, Sohlberg lets Likud inspect materials from about 190 polling stations (access, not a finding); 12 November, Sohlberg's letter that the classified material has not yet become evidence (assessment, not a finding); 13 November, Channel 13 report on the team's update and Zini's complaints (anonymous sources); 5 November letter reported on 13 November (demand letter); no court filing so far. No change to the official results was found, and the Israel Democracy Institute has published nothing new on the election since 20 October.

Brazil

NewSupreme Court has four votes to keep the shift from a six-day to a five-day work week starting 16 December

Under the rules being upheld, the week is 42 hours during the transition and 40 hours from December 2027, with no pay cuts, and Congress has 180 days to regulate. Separately, Justice Dias Toffoli of the Superior Electoral Court gave the Lula–Alckmin campaign three days to explain inconsistencies in its accounts, including suspected irregular subcontracting and lack of proof that suppliers could do the work (g1).
