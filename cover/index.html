Israel election

NewMaariv poll: blocs tied at 51, Likud falls to 18, Arab parties reach 14

A Maariv poll by Lazar Research with Panel4All (700 respondents, 14–15 October, margin of error 3.7 points), published on 16 October, puts Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar at 21 seats and Likud at 18, down two from the previous week. The other parties: Bennett's Beyachad 13, the Democrats 9, United Torah Judaism 8, Otzma Yehudit 8, Yisrael Beytenu 8, the Joint List 8, Shas 7, Religious Zionism 6, Ra'am 6, Ofer Winter's Amcha Yisrael 4 and the Hendel–Zelekha Reservists 4. The Netanyahu bloc and the Zionist opposition have 51 seats each; the Arab parties together have 14, their highest in this poll series; the Reservists (4) are not counted in either bloc, and neither bloc would reach 61 even with them. Asked what should happen if the result is inconclusive, 55 percent prefer a unity government and 27 percent another election (18 percent do not know). As prime minister, Eisenkot leads Netanyahu 48 to 41; Netanyahu and Bennett are tied at 43; Netanyahu leads Lieberman 43 to 37. The share of undecided voters was 5.2 percent.

Lebanon

NewHezbollah admits the seized explosives are its own and demands them back; Rubio says that is 'out of the question'

On 15 October the Hezbollah member of parliament Hassan Fadlallah acknowledged that the explosives seized by the Lebanese army on 5 October at the Dahr al-Baidar checkpoint belong to Hezbollah. According to a judicial source, the shipment held 500 kilograms of explosives: 250 kilograms of TNT and 250 kilograms of C-4. Hezbollah demanded that the explosives be returned and that the two men detained with them be released, said the material was meant for use against Israel, and warned the authorities. Prime Minister Nawaf Salam promised firm action and noted that C-4 has been used in past political assassinations in Lebanon. Lebanon has seized three shipments of explosives since the start of October. In a phone call with President Joseph Aoun, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said that returning the explosives to Hezbollah is 'out of the question' and that everyone who ordered, financed and directed the shipment should be held accountable; he also criticized Lebanon's 1955 law boycotting Israel and urged direct military-to-military communication with Israel to avoid incidents. On 16 October the IDF carried out air strikes in southern Lebanon; the Home Front Command told Galilee residents they might hear explosions, and the IDF fired an interceptor at a suspicious aerial target (no sirens sounded).

Brazil

NewTwo new runoff polls: Flávio Bolsonaro barely ahead, within the margin of error

Two polls published on 16 October show the 25 October runoff almost even. CNT/MDA, its first runoff poll (2,014 face-to-face interviews, 13–15 October, margin of error 2.2 points, registered with the electoral court as BR-00652/2026), gives Flávio Bolsonaro 50.5 percent of valid votes and Lula 49.5; counting all answers, 46.8 to 45.9, with 4.9 percent blank or null and 2.4 percent undecided. Fewer voters reject Flávio than Lula: 46.6 percent say they would not vote for Flávio and 48.8 percent would not vote for Lula. Futura Inteligência (2,000 interviews, 14–15 October, margin of error 2.2 points, BR-03764/2026) gives Flávio 50.8 percent of valid votes and Lula 49.2, a narrower lead than its 51.8 to 48.2 of 13 October; its rejection figures are Lula 47.2 and Flávio 46.1. Both results are within the margin of error, as was Datafolha's 47 to 45 in the previous edition.

Colombia and Israel

NewColombian foreign minister ends Israel visit with Herzog; tourist visa exemption restored

Foreign Minister Omar Bula Escobar ended his four-day official visit to Israel on 16 October with a meeting with President Isaac Herzog. According to the Colombian Foreign Ministry, they discussed cooperation in security, defense, trade, investment, artificial intelligence, agriculture and water. The two countries restored the mutual exemption from tourist visas and have designated ambassadors; the minister also visited the Old City of Jerusalem and the Western Wall. Infobae reports that Colombia has recognized Israeli sovereignty over the Golan Heights, which it describes as a position shared only by the United States; this was not found in the Colombian ministry's statement and is unconfirmed. Infobae calls Colombia the eleventh country with an embassy in Jerusalem, while the earlier report used here said the tenth; the correct count is unconfirmed. Infobae also reports condemnations of the visit from Iran, Egypt, Kuwait, Turkey and the Arab Parliament.

Israeli data centers

NewElectricity Authority plans a 200-megawatt cap per new data center; decision due Sunday

Israel's Electricity Authority plans to limit each new data center to 200 megawatts and to publish the draft for three weeks of public comment, starting on Sunday, 18 October, Calcalist reported on 14 October. Requests to connect data centers to the grid total about 27,000 megawatts, against installed generating capacity of about 18,800 megawatts; average demand is about 9,000 megawatts and the record peak was 17,300 megawatts in August 2025. Fifty of the requested facilities are larger than 200 megawatts, among them Mega DC (1,100 megawatts, on the former Alliance tire factory site in Hadera) and SDS (875 megawatts, in Nesher). New applications were frozen for 140 days from June, and the authority estimates data centers could use about 20 percent of national electricity by 2030. According to reports in TheMarker and The State of Israel, members of the authority differ on the total capacity to allow through 2035, from several hundred megawatts to about one gigawatt, against requests of up to 2.3 gigawatts; proposals include construction deadlines and guarantees of NIS 2 million per megawatt, and only a partial decision is expected on Sunday.

Trump and Xi

NewChina lets Visa prepare a domestic card-clearing business and books its first US soybeans since the summit

China's central bank approved on 13 October Visa's application to prepare a domestic bank-card clearing institution; Visa has one year to prepare and cannot yet issue renminbi cards. On 15 October Qiu Wenxing of the Chinese embassy in Washington called the approval 'a direct outcome of the presidential summit' between Trump and Xi in late September. This week Chinese state-owned buyers booked at least 20 cargoes of US soybeans, about 1.3 million tons, the first purchases since the summit, according to Bloomberg as cited by Quartz; most will be loaded from December to March. US soybeans still face Chinese tariffs of about 13 percent and are not on the lists of tariff cuts; China has pledged to buy 25 million tons a year through 2028. The two leaders are due to meet again at APEC in November and the G20 in December.

UK breakup politics

NewBurnham rules out a second Scottish independence referendum while he is prime minister

On his first major visit to Scotland as prime minister, in Motherwell on 16 October, Andy Burnham ruled out a second independence referendum during his premiership; Downing Street sources said this applies to his whole time as leader. He offered 'deeper devolution' instead, including powers over employment support now held by the Department for Work and Pensions, and said he will meet the first ministers at the National Economic Council. The Scottish Conservative leader, Russell Findlay, criticized him. Earlier, Burnham had told MPs that a referendum would be considered if there were a clear consensus. First Minister John Swinney gives his keynote speech to the SNP conference in Aberdeen on 17 October; according to the BBC's preview, he will say that 'the stars are aligning' and that every first minister in Edinburgh, Cardiff and Belfast now supports the right to self-determination. The SNP won a fifth term in May but without a majority.

Greenland

NewGreenland's parliament votes to prepare a ban on deep-sea mining until 2029

On 15 October Greenland's parliament, Inatsisartut, unanimously adopted a resolution telling the government to draft a temporary ban on deep-sea mining and exploration until 31 December 2029, which could be extended two years at a time. The bill is expected in the spring 2027 session, so no binding ban exists yet. The vote came a day after President Trump said that 'massive oil, gas and mineral exploration' in Greenland was about to begin. Greenland's deputy minister for mining, Jørgen Hammeken-Holm, said the situation was 'very far away from massive,' and Denmark said the September agreement gives American investors no preferential access.

Quebec

NewSt-Pierre Plamondon: fix Quebec first, then talk of independence

At the swearing-in of the Parti Québécois's 59 members of the National Assembly on 15 October, Paul St-Pierre Plamondon closed with 'Vive le Québec libre' but said that before talking about independence the PQ must 'get Quebec back on the rails' and regain voters' confidence, the Montreal Gazette reported. Trenton

NewA local developer's graffiti museum plans a five-story home on East State Street

The American Graffiti Museum, a nonprofit founded in 2021 by the Trenton native Daniel R. Brenna Jr., whose firm Ajax Management Group has redeveloped more than 70 properties in the city over 25 years, plans a 100,000-square-foot museum of graffiti and street art in a five-story historic building on East State Street, with a 12,000-square-foot courtyard. The site was secured in 2022 and the building is under construction; no opening date has been set. Its 'Downtown Gets Up' events this year brought new murals by CRASH, Ron English, Wane One, Queen Andrea and the Trenton artist Leon Rainbow, and an exhibition at Hanover Creative Gallery. The founders expect 200,000 or more visitors a year; that is their own projection. Public element: the event series was paid for in part by a state grant of $210,161 from the New Jersey Economic Development Authority's A.R.T. program, funded by the federal American Rescue Plan. Funding for the building itself was not reported.

France presidential election

NewOpinionWay: Glucksmann gains after the primary, Philippe at his lowest, Le Pen at 35 to 37

Two OpinionWay polls taken on 13 and 14 October, after the left's primary (978 registered voters in a sample of 1,043), were published on 15 October. In the Présitrack poll for Les Echos and Radio Classique, Marine Le Pen has 35 percent if both Gabriel Attal and Édouard Philippe run, followed by Jean-Luc Mélenchon 14, Raphaël Glucksmann 13, Philippe 12, Attal 7 and Bruno Retailleau 7. With Attal as the only centrist, Le Pen has 37, Glucksmann 16, Mélenchon 15, Attal 11 and Retailleau 9; with Philippe as the only centrist, Le Pen has 35 and Mélenchon, Glucksmann and Philippe 15 each, with Retailleau at 8. Glucksmann gained four to five points; Philippe fell three points to his lowest level in this series. In the OpinionWay poll for CNEWS, Le Pen has 35, Mélenchon 15, Glucksmann 13 and Philippe 12 in the first round, and in the second round she beats Philippe 56 to 44, Glucksmann 56 to 44 and Mélenchon 68 to 32. Her margin over Philippe is much wider than in Elabe's August poll (52.5 to 47.5). On Polymarket there has been no material change: Le Pen about 46 percent, Philippe about 17.5, Lisnard and Mélenchon about 11.5 each, Glucksmann about 3.5.
