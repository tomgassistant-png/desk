Israel election

NewKnesset sworn in: Netanyahu attacks the Arab parties in his speech and Eisenkot cancels a planned meeting with Likud; Ohana sets the vote on replacing him for 17 November; Matan Kahana, not Eisenkot, is the bloc's choice for speaker

Correction to yesterday's item: ynet and Tal Schneider now report that the bloc's speaker-designate is Matan Kahana of Yashar!, not Gadi Eisenkot himself; ynet says Eisenkot first wanted the post but decided Kahana would hold it at least during coalition talks. Ohana said he opposes the move because, citing media reports, it aims to pass laws giving more power to the attorney general, but that he will respect the majority. At the traditional faction-leaders' photo event, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called again for a "national unity government" led by him, attacked the change bloc's contacts with Ra'am, and told the Haredi parties that even a narrow government without him must not be formed; when Netanyahu said the influence of foreign actors on the results must be investigated "to the end", Naftali Bennett (B'Yachad) shouted back, asking about a state inquiry into 7 October and "your Qatari advisers", Yair Golan (Democrats) walked out, and Gilad Kariv was removed (ynet; Hezki Baruch on X). Members of the Joint List left the plenum during the national anthem; Ra'am's members stayed (ynet). Eisenkot then canceled a negotiating meeting with Likud planned for Wednesday, 11 November, citing what Yashar! called Netanyahu's "divisive speech"; ynet reports the meeting was to be between Ronen Manelis for Yashar! and Netanyahu associate Michael Rabilo. Likud said Netanyahu "will turn every stone" for unity and will not cooperate with "supporters of terror" (ynet; Hezki Baruch on X); Likud also pointed to the Joint List's walkout. Avigdor Liberman (Yisrael Beytenu) said he will not sit in any government that includes Netanyahu, though he has "no problem" with Likud without him (N12). On the first day, Yashar! filed bills to set up a state commission of inquiry into 7 October, to limit a prime minister's time in office, and to repeal several of the outgoing government's judicial and legal changes; Yair Lapid (B'Yachad) filed bills for a Basic Law on the Declaration of Independence, civil partnerships and an eight-year term limit (ynet). Ra'am chair Mansour Abbas told ynet that Netanyahu told him at a 2021 meeting at the prime minister's residence that he and Yahya Sinwar "understand each other"; Likud called this "a total lie". This is his claim only. No government has been formed.

Lebanon, Syria and the Druze

NewSweida (Syria): the Druze National Guard closes three offices linked to the Kurdish-led women's units and sends their staff to its military court

Verify-Sy, a Syrian fact-checking platform, says local sources told it the closure process began about a month ago and ended with the offices sealed and their staff referred for investigation to the National Guard's military judiciary. An unnamed source in the YPJ military office blamed a change of policy by the SDF-run areas east of the Euphrates as they merge with the government in Damascus, and accused officials there of trying to recruit people in Sweida, promote the merger and collect daily intelligence reports; a source close to the women's office said it had in fact closed on 25 September under pressure to promote the merger. Verify-Sy says it could not confirm these accusations and the YPJ did not respond. Suwayda 24, a local activist network, reported on 10 November that local sources tied the closures to the end of SDF support for the National Guard, while other local reports spoke of recruitment, information-gathering and promotion of the merger. No new Israeli reaction to the EU Support Mission in Lebanon (EUSM) and no new contributing country were found.

France presidential election

NewÉric Zemmour officially declares for president; first Ifop poll on Teddy Riner: 22% "ready" to vote for him

Zemmour, who won 7.07% in the first round in 2022, promised a referendum on immigration policy and said he would send home foreigners unemployed for more than six months (RTL; La Nouvelle République). La Nouvelle République, citing a franceinfo count, says 37 people have now declared. An Ifop poll for LCI and Sud Radio, taken online on 9 and 10 November among 1,003 adults, found 22% would be ready to vote for judo champion Teddy Riner, who declared on 9 November, and 70% have a good opinion of him; 20 Minutes notes this measures willingness, not voting intention. On Polymarket, checked about 4:20 AM Israel time on 11 November, Marine Le Pen is at about 46%, Jean-Luc Mélenchon 13.5%, David Lisnard 13% (down from 14%), Édouard Philippe 8.5% (down from 9.5%) and Riner 2.6%.

Brazil

NewElectoral court fines Meta R$7.5 million for not obeying its orders on equal treatment of Lula and Flávio; Federal Police open an inquiry into suspected foreign money in Flávio Bolsonaro's campaign

The case was brought by President Lula's campaign, which said Meta treated the two sides unequally in the reach of political content and ads in the runoff. According to G1, the court found that Meta replaced the court's deadlines with its own, gave information piecemeal and in technical foreign-language terms, and lacked transparency; the fine is for not complying, and Bellizze said this case cannot show that Meta acted deliberately to tilt the election, which would need strong evidence. Meta's lawyer said all orders were obeyed and that only brief technical failures occurred (G1). Separately, the Federal Police opened a formal inquiry, ordered in October by Supreme Court Justice Flávio Dino, into suspected money from the United States for Flávio Bolsonaro's campaign through Meta and the US conservative group Rockbridge Network; in his October order Dino cited "intensifying signs of foreign interference" and asked about a possible link between electoral support and foreign economic interest in Brazil's rare earths and critical minerals (CNN Brasil). No findings have been published.

UK breakup politics

NewWales's Plaid Cymru government sets up a standing National Commission that will weigh independence, a federal UK and more devolution

Constitution Minister Dafydd Trystan Davies said it will cost "in the ballpark" of £500,000 a year. It will advise on devolving powers over the Crown Estate, rail, justice and policing, and on moving responsibilities to regional and local levels; independence is one of three options it will consider, with enhanced devolution and a federal UK (Welsh government; BBC). Plaid Cymru has ruled out an independence referendum this Senedd term, which runs to 2030. Rob Hughes, chair of the pro-independence group YesCymru, called it a step toward independence and said the group is looking at a referendum in 2032; Reform said the commission is a waste of time and money (BBC). It is not clear when the commission will report. A 2024 commission concluded that independence was a viable option (BBC).

Israeli data centers

NewNational AI strategy sets a goal of about 300,000 GPUs in Israel, 200,000 of them to draw data centers, needing about 2 gigawatts of power; Azrieli says Norway's freeze on new data-center grid links will not hurt current projects

He said this would need about 2 gigawatts of power for server farms, computing and chip plants, and that Israel also wants its own advanced foundation model; no budget or timetable was published (Gadgety; Ctech). Gadgety notes that in July the Electricity Authority froze, for 140 days, new grid-connection requests from server farms of 8 megavolt-amperes or more, after the queue reached about 27 gigawatts. Separately, Azrieli Group, which owns Green Mountain (four data centers in Norway, about 174 megawatts installed), reported on Tuesday that Norway's grid operator has temporarily frozen new capacity allocations for data centers while rules are drawn up by August 2027. Azrieli expects no material effect on its operating projects; Globes notes the freeze could delay an 80-megawatt campus Green Mountain agreed to build for an international tech company, which Azrieli denied. Azrieli shares are down about 7% since the start of the week (Globes).
