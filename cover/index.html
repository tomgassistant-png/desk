Lebanon

NewTampa military talks end after two days without agreement; Macron will hold a conference for the Lebanese army after the Israeli vote

The second day of US-mediated talks between the Lebanese and Israeli military delegations at Central Command in Tampa ended on 21 October without agreement or any expansion of the pilot zones (MTV; Jarayid, from Lebanese media). The dispute is still who verifies what happens in the south: Israel wants a direct joint operations room with the Lebanese army, and Lebanon refuses any arrangement that gives Israel a say over its army's movements and wants contact to go through the United States. Lebanese reports say the third day is set aside for the US side to review the results, with possible separate meetings with each delegation. In Doha, President Macron said France will organize a conference to support the Lebanese army 'in the coming weeks,' but only after the Israeli election, since holding it before would be 'useless'; he called the pressure on Lebanon 'the least best way' to deal with Hezbollah and said disarmament needs an Israeli withdrawal with confidence-building measures (Naharnet, from AFP; Times of Israel).

Brazil

NewQuaest: Flávio 45, Lula 44, still a technical tie; Lula's rejection falls to 47 percent; Datafolha due Thursday

A Quaest poll for Globo and O Globo published on 21 October (2,154 voters in 124 cities, 17–20 October, margin of error 2 points, registered as BR-08535/2026) has Flávio Bolsonaro at 45 percent and Lula at 44 in the 25 October runoff, against 46 to 44 a week earlier; blank, null or not voting 9 percent, undecided 2. In valid votes that is about 50.6 to 49.4, by UOL's calculation. In the unprompted question both have 40 percent. Rejection moved toward Lula: 52 percent say they would never vote for Flávio (54 before) and 47 percent for Lula (53 before). Approval of the Lula government rose to 46 percent, with 47 percent disapproving (g1; UOL). It is Quaest's first poll since the end of the 6x1 work schedule, a Lula campaign promise, was enacted. Datafolha publishes on Thursday, 22 October. Polymarket has Flávio at about 79 percent, Lula about 21.

France presidential election

NewUnions leave Matignon empty-handed and call a general strike for 5 November; Lecornu meets the parties on Thursday

The five main unions met Prime Minister Lecornu for two and a half hours on 21 October and came out saying he announced no measures; CGT leader Sophie Binet called for strike meetings everywhere and said 'the strike must take over' from 5 November, the date nearly all unions have set for a general strike in the private and public sectors (Nouvel Obs, from AFP; RTS; L'Humanité). The CFTC said the government had opened the door to talks on the minimum wage, and Lecornu has promised first announcements for young people early next week. On Thursday, 22 October, he receives the political parties on the 17 October violence and on securing the presidential campaign, and Macron holds a meeting with the intelligence services on the failures of that day. Curfews were renewed on Wednesday night in parts of Paris, Lille, Lyon and Marseille, and undeclared gatherings remain banned in Paris ahead of Saturday's protests (franceinfo; RFI). Polymarket is unchanged, with Le Pen at about 46.5 percent.

Trenton

NewLocust Hill Cemetery, Trenton's 1861 Black burial ground, becomes a site on the New Jersey Black Heritage Trail

The New Jersey Historical Commission marked Locust Hill Cemetery in Trenton as a site on the state's Black Heritage Trail at a ceremony on 20 October (NBC10 Philadelphia; PIX11). Mount Zion AME Church bought the land in 1861 as a burial ground for Black residents; 174 people are known to be buried there, including at least 10 Civil War veterans. The late Algernon Ward nominated the site, it was approved in 2024, and City Council approved the marker on 16 December 2025. The trail was set up in 2022. No reusable photo was found; NBC10's report has photos of the ceremony.

Trump and Xi

NewTrump says he is open to a three-way meeting with Xi and Putin in China next month, with nuclear weapons on the agenda

Trump told reporters on 19 October, Washington time, that he would hold a three-way meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin during his November visit to China 'if President Xi wants to have it,' to talk about 'nuclear weapons and denuclearisation' (South China Morning Post; Caixin). Caixin reports he said he had only just heard of the proposal and understood the meeting would take place in China, around the APEC summit in Shenzhen; Kremlin spokesman Peskov had said such talks would be useful. No date, format or agenda has been agreed, and China has not confirmed it.
