Israel election

NewBennett meets Netanyahu for over two hours and backs a unity government with Eisenkot as prime minister first; Golan and Liberman refuse, and the change-bloc leaders call for an Eisenkot-led government "as soon as possible"

Likud said the meeting was a security briefing; i24NEWS, cited by Srugim, reported it also covered politics. People close to Bennett said he will not serve under Netanyahu and that a unity government is relevant only if Eisenkot is first in a rotation. Channel 12, as reported by Srugim, described a Bennett outline: Eisenkot first in the rotation (for one or two years, in versions under discussion), then Netanyahu, with agreements on a state commission of inquiry, military service and the courts. At the change-bloc leaders' meeting that afternoon, Channel 13 reported (via Srugim) that Bennett said "we need to set up a unity government," that Yair Golan and Yisrael Beytenu chair Avigdor Liberman answered that they would not sit with Netanyahu "in any form," and that Eisenkot urged forming a government quickly, "even if it is a narrow government for now." Channel 13 also reported that Bennett has warned in private against relying on the Arab parties: "I was there, it won't work." A Maariv headline quotes Liberman as saying he would vote against "such a government" (headline only; the article text did not load). The leaders then issued a joint statement saying they all agree on the urgency of forming a national emergency government led by Eisenkot and replacing Netanyahu "as soon as possible" (text posted by Arutz Sheva reporter Hezki Baruch on X); Yesh Atid chair Yair Lapid wrote that he and Bennett are working "tirelessly" for a change government led by Eisenkot. ynet reports two readings of Bennett's moves inside his own bloc: that he wants to exhaust the unity option so he can later blame Netanyahu, or that he expects new elections. In the Knesset, under the agreement reached around the speaker vote, the plenum on Wednesday passed in preliminary readings change-bloc bills to repeal the media law (63 to 49), the law that weakened the attorney general (63 to 47) and the arrangements that changed the Judicial Selection Committee (62 to 46), and set up two special committees for them; in return, two bills by Likud faction chair Ofir Katz passed preliminary readings, one to strip rights from MKs who do not recognize Hamas as a terror group (48 to 0) and one barring interference with tefillin stands in public places (Davar; the vote counts on the media and AG bills also in Times of Israel headlines). On Wednesday evening Shas chair Aryeh Deri submitted a bill to halt arrests of Haredi draft evaders until a new government is formed, as a temporary provision; mako (Channel 12) and Kipa describe it as a test for Liberman, Bennett and Eisenkot, since Netanyahu's 53-seat bloc is expected to back it. Deri said "the state cannot keep arresting yeshiva students." No coalition agreement has been signed, and no candidate has a majority yet.

Lebanon, Syria and the Druze

NewAfter UNIFIL: Germany and Austria commit personnel to the EU Support Mission in Lebanon; France takes its plan for a new force to the UN Security Council today, and Defense Minister Katz says Israel will not leave its security zones

Austria's government approved its part on 11 November: for 2027, up to 20 Defense Ministry and 5 Interior Ministry personnel, plus support staff, for a total ceiling of 79 people who will not all be in Lebanon at once; the mission is to advise and train the Lebanese army in border security, reconnaissance, surveillance and maritime security (Austrian Foreign Ministry, Heute). Separately, Walla reports that France will present its plan for a new international force to replace UNIFIL at a closed UN Security Council session on Thursday, 19 November: thousands of European troops, mainly from France, Italy and Spain, working with the Lebanese army in the south, plus unarmed observers; the United States backs a smaller observer force instead, and Israel opposes the French plan. In Israel, Defense Minister Israel Katz said on 18 November that the IDF "controls the security zones in Lebanon, Syria and Gaza" and will not withdraw from their current lines before Hamas is dismantled, Hezbollah is disarmed "in all of Lebanon" and jihadist threats are removed from Syria, and that if fighting flares the IDF is ready to take more territory (Srugim; Jerusalem Post). No official Israeli statement on EUSM Lebanon itself was found. In Lebanon, the judiciary on 18 November charged five Hezbollah members, arrested earlier this month in northern Lebanon, with "forming an armed group, recruiting members, equipping them and supplying them with weapons" (Arab News, citing a judicial source speaking to AFP; Times of Israel headline).

Israeli data centers

NewEnvironmental Protection Ministry official warns data centers will raise air pollution and deaths; ministry puts the cost at more than NIS 3 billion a year

According to CTech, the ministry estimates about NIS 2 billion a year in damage from the extra power generation the centers need and more than NIS 1 billion from running backup generators, plus higher greenhouse-gas emissions and faster use of Israel's natural gas reserves; air pollution is already blamed for an estimated 5,500 premature deaths a year. The warning comes as the Electricity Authority plans to release another 1,000 MW of capacity; Nazar wrote that at the projected rate the gas reserves would run out in about 17 years, about three years earlier than expected. A Times of Israel headline the same day reads "Official warns pollution from data centers could prove lethal in coming years" (headline only; the site blocks automated readers). The figures are the ministry's estimates as reported; no response from the Electricity Authority was found. The Haifa Bay project of Enlight and Shemen was covered on 17 November.

Greenland

NewDanish parliament's final vote on the US–Greenland–Denmark defense agreement is scheduled for about 10 a.m. Copenhagen time today, 19 November

The agreement, signed in September by the United States, Denmark and Greenland, would expand the US military role in Greenland, including permission for a "Golden Dome" missile-defense system, while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to self-determination. Greenland's parliament ratified it this month, and it passed its first reading in the Danish parliament last week with support from all parties. It does not need a US Senate vote; it enters into force once Denmark's Foreign Ministry notifies the United States by diplomatic note. University of Copenhagen professor Mikkel Vedby Rasmussen told Reuters that "this is not an end, but rather a beginning," and that the US will be able to "demand greater influence" on security decisions and on resource extraction; Reuters adds that other experts say it does not add much US military access. The vote result will be reported in the next Greenland update; the vote time comes from one source.

Non-Latin script digitization

NewJammu: a rare-manuscripts repository and e-library opens at the Raghunath Temple's Sanskrit research institute under India's Gyan Bharatam program

It was opened by Vivek Aggarwal, secretary of India's Ministry of Culture, with Karan Singh, chairman trustee of the Dharmarth Trust. It is set up as an independent center under the ministry's Gyan Bharatam manuscript program, to preserve and digitize manuscripts and open them to scholars and the public. The institute also launched the Jammu Series of Texts and Studies for editions based on its manuscripts, and presented an 11-volume Bhagavad Gita edition with 21 commentaries and a 14-volume Tantra series. The reports do not say how many manuscripts are held or when digitized copies will be online.

Trump and Xi

NewShenzhen: Xi meets Trump at the airport, Trump meets Putin after the APEC plenary, and the first Xi–Trump–Putin meeting is due later Thursday

BBC Chinese reports that Putin, who arrived earlier that day, was met by Politburo Standing Committee member Cai Qi. China opened the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting in Shenzhen on Thursday, 19 November, with Xi as chair (Anadolu). After the plenary, Trump met Putin on the sidelines at about 12:43 p.m. Shenzhen time and told him "a lot of wonderful things can come out of this" (Nikkei Asia live page). According to the White House schedule cited by BBC Chinese, Trump then holds a bilateral meeting with Xi, his third this year, followed by the three-way meeting at the Qilin Villa, about 30 minutes from the APEC venue. China's Foreign Ministry says the three will discuss "issues concerning global peace," and Trump said "we're going to talk about denuclearization" (Anadolu). BBC Chinese, citing the Financial Times and other media, reports that the sides disagree on the agenda: Trump prefers nuclear arms control and Iran, Putin wants to discuss a Ukraine ceasefire, and China is cool to discussing Ukraine without Europe. Trump has said he may meet Kim Jong Un, which North Korea has not confirmed (Anadolu). The results of the meetings were not yet known when this edition was prepared.
