Brazil

NewLula wins a fourth term in the runoff, about 51.7 to 48.3 percent; Flávio Bolsonaro says it is "impossible to concede" and will ask the courts to annul the vote

With 97 percent of the votes counted, g1 had Lula at 51.74 percent and Flávio at 48.26. Lula received 64,220,485 votes, the most any candidate has ever won in Brazil, and his margin was about 4.38 million votes (g1). Abstention fell to 19.21 percent, from 21.08 percent in the first round (Guardian live blog). Voting was extended to 7 p.m. after Supreme Court Justice Cristiano Zanin acted on complaints that the free buses promised to carry voters were missing in nine states (Guardian live blog; g1). Flávio called the result "sacanagem" (a rude word for a dirty trick), said it was "impossible to concede" and said he would ask the courts to annul the vote; the head of the electoral court (TSE), Justice Nunes Marques, said he had "absolute security" in the result, and Prosecutor General Paulo Gonet also backed the process (g1; Guardian). In his victory speech Lula spoke of "interference" by the big technology companies. The leaders of Mexico (Sheinbaum), Paraguay (Peña), Uruguay (Orsi), Bolivia (Paz), Cuba (Díaz-Canel) and Panama (Mulino) sent congratulations (Guardian live blog). The new term begins on 5 January 2027 (g1).

Israel election

NewChannel 12: IDF chief Zamir resisted a Netanyahu order for an unspecified military action that could start a clash with Iran before Tuesday; Netanyahu calls the report "nonsense"

No details of the action were given, and the report was broadcast under military censorship. A military source told Channel 12 it "could lead to a flare-up with Iran, at a sensitive and dramatic moment", and that army leaders fear an effort "to utilize the Iranian issue for political benefit" (Times of Israel, citing Channel 12). Netanyahu told Channel 14 there was "no fight" with Zamir and that the report was "nonsense" (Times of Israel). i24NEWS and Haaretz report the same fear among senior officers. Opposition leaders backed Zamir: Eisenkot wrote "Netanyahu, I'm watching you"; Lapid said Netanyahu wants a conflict with Iran "just to postpone the election"; Lieberman called it "tantamount to treason" (Times of Israel). Earlier on Sunday, at the Palmahim air base, Netanyahu warned Iran "Don't test us" and said there were intelligence signs that Iran and its allies might attack to disrupt the vote. Trump said there would be no US strike on Iran on Sunday (Ynet); Axios reporter Barak Ravid says the White House was alarmed by Netanyahu's nuclear-blast image (next item) and that Trump's remark may have been meant to calm Tehran (Times of Israel). All of this rests on unnamed sources and cannot be checked.

Lebanon, Syria and the Druze

NewDruze: Sunday's Route 6 protest followed the stabbing of a young Druze man from Isfiya in Jaffa; his father says it was for speaking Arabic, police say it began as an argument

His father says the three were attacked because they were speaking Arabic, and Kan called it a "racist attack". Police say it began as an argument; three suspects aged 18 and 19 were arrested and two knives seized, and no racist motive was mentioned at the remand hearing (Ynet; Mako). This is the cause of Sunday afternoon's protest that closed Route 6 at the Elyakim interchange, which the 25 October edition could not yet explain: about 100 Druze protesters burned tires and blocked the road for about an hour, and police arrested four (Ynet; i24NEWS). The motive is disputed and not yet established.

NewLe Pen launches her campaign in Orléans: a "national union" government led by Bardella and France as "a nation of peace"; Jewish student activists interrupt Bardella

Colombia and Israel

NewNetanyahu names Colombia as a country Israel "brought back" with "countermeasures" against sanctions

He gave the example of Britain ("we closed their consulate in Jerusalem") and of "bringing back countries that had moved away, like Colombia or Slovenia" (Europa Press). Israeli data centers

NewShamir Energy takes a 10-year option to build a 100-megawatt data center at Ashdot Ya'akov in the Jordan Valley, with its own power plant or storage

A small power station or energy-storage site is planned next to the data center to supply its electricity, at a time when the Electricity Authority plans to allow only about 1,000 megawatts of new data center load against requests of about 27,000 megawatts (Calcalist). The option requires feasibility studies, permits and financing; if used, the land lease runs almost 25 years, and the industrial park may take up to 18 percent of the operating company. Calcalist says this may mark the entry of energy companies into a field so far led by real estate firms such as Azrieli, Mega Or and Melisron.
