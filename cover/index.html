Israel election

NewElection committee chair: Shin Bet's classified information "has not yet matured into real evidence, and may not"; he condemns leaks; a Joint List lawmaker asks for a criminal probe of a rabbi's ruling against working with Arab parties

Sohlberg wrote that the evidentiary picture is similar to the one on the eve of the committee's statement last week, but that the material still requires a vigorous investigation until it is exhausted. He condemned leaks, calling them "a grievous evil" that harms the security side of the probe, and said a classified police opinion had been published before it reached the committee, which received it only at noon on 11 November; he added that media reports are far from how the investigation is actually being run. He thanked the Shin Bet and its chief, called cooperation among the Shin Bet, the police and the attorney general good, and said he found no case in which the attorney general refused a Shin Bet request in this investigation. That partly conflicts with a report the same day in Arab48, citing Israel Hayom, that the attorney general has not approved Shin Bet requests to summon more people, that the Shin Bet has not yet given police additional material it promised, that Shin Bet chief David Zini still holds that the suspicions are serious and could lead to disqualifying votes, and that police see possible party-financing violations at most. This letter is an assessment of where the probe stands, not a finding. Separately, MK Ofer Cassif (Joint List, an interested party) asked State Attorney Amit Aisman to open a criminal investigation of Rabbi Gedalia Axelrod of Haifa, a former head of the Haifa rabbinical court, over a ruling aimed at leaders who cooperate with Arab parties that Cassif calls incitement to violence (Arab48); this is a complaint, not an investigation. Likud: no appeal filing was found; Maariv reported on 11 November that Likud plans to file on 18 November. Dated history: 11 November, Sohlberg lets Likud inspect materials from about 190 polling stations (access, not a finding); 12 November, Sohlberg's letter on the Shin Bet probe (assessment, not a finding); no court filing so far. No change to the official results was found.

France presidential election

NewIsrael bars Mélenchon and Mathilde Panot from entering, a day after France barred settler leader Yossi Dagan

Mélenchon said: "I regret that Israel's government pursues me this way." Panot wrote on X that she was still looking for the bad news. Brazil

NewSenate approves the base text of the fiscal package, 45–31, with a 10% cut in tax breaks and a 2% spending cap; prosecutor general opposes reopening Bolsonaro's conviction

The text cuts tax benefits by a flat 10%, caps real spending growth at 2%, leaves oil revenue out of the health-spending floor until 2030, gives Congress 120 days to regulate pay above the legal ceiling of R$46,300 ("supersalaries"), and takes up to R$10.5 billion in diesel subsidies out of the 2026 fiscal target. Separately, Prosecutor General Paulo Gonet told the Supreme Court he opposes Jair Bolsonaro's request to review his criminal conviction; Justice Nunes Marques is the rapporteur (Agência Brasil).
