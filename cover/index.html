Israel election

NewEisenkot reportedly plans to put his government to a Knesset vote before his mandate runs out on 6 December, even as a minority government; Likud sets a second round of unity talks for Sunday

According to the ynet report by Amir Ettinger, Eisenkot would take this step even if talks on a unity government with Likud and on a government with United Torah Judaism both fail. The Times of Israel, citing Haaretz, reported that he would hold the vote up to a week before his mandate expires on 6 December; Herzog can extend the mandate by two weeks, but his party is not counting on it. Eisenkot received the mandate on 8 November. Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas said on 17 November, in an interview with Radio Nas reported by i24NEWS in Arabic, that a minority government without the Haredi parties is a "very dangerous scenario" that could not last, though he added that every option his party discusses with the change bloc aims to replace Netanyahu's government and avoid another election. Likud answered the report by announcing that its negotiating team will meet Yashar!'s team again on Sunday, 22 November, after their first meeting on Tuesday; it said "national unity is the need of the hour" but accused Eisenkot of pursuing unity "only for appearances" (Srugim). ynet reported that people in both camps are discussing a rotation in which Netanyahu would serve two years as prime minister and then move to the President's Residence, with a change in the law so the next president could be elected before the government is sworn in; this is a report of an idea under discussion, not a proposal by either party. In the Knesset, Yisrael Beytenu faction chair Oded Forer, with the heads of the change bloc's Zionist factions, tabled a Basic Law bill that would limit a prime minister to eight years, bar a Knesset member indicted for serious crimes from being given the task of forming a government, and end a prime minister's term if his offenses are found to involve moral turpitude (Srugim). Eisenkot also announced that the change bloc will back retired Supreme Court Justice Yosef Elron for state comptroller in the repeat vote ordered after the High Court found a flaw in the June vote (Srugim). On the election-interference watch, there was no new ruling, filing or official finding. People close to Shin Bet chief David Zini said on Thursday that he did not determine that no vote-buying took place, that "significant suspicions" exist but have not become a sufficient evidentiary basis, partly because of limits placed on the investigation, and that a letter by Elections Committee chair Justice Noam Sohlberg should not be read as Zini's position; senior law-enforcement officials told N12 that the Shin Bet itself delayed passing material to the police fraud unit, and people close to Sohlberg said the letter had been coordinated with the Shin Bet (N12). Likud's deadline to file its election appeal remains Sunday, 22 November; no filing was reported by early Friday.

Lebanon, Syria and the Druze

NewIsrael strikes a Hezbollah missile site in Lebanon's Beqaa Valley, its second strike in eastern Lebanon this week; the UN Security Council meets on Lebanon behind closed doors with no decision announced on what follows UNIFIL

The IDF said it had recently seen Hezbollah operatives returning to the site, which it struck several times during the war, and that it hit the entrances "to prevent its rehabilitation"; there were no immediate reports of casualties (Times of Israel; Srugim). The Times of Israel called it the second Israeli strike in the Beqaa within days, after a strike on Monday on a weapons shipment from Syria. Lebanon's official National News Agency reported four strikes on the al-Shaara area in the heights of the eastern mountain range. The Security Council held closed consultations on Lebanon on Thursday evening, with briefings from UN peacekeeping chief Jean-Pierre Lacroix and Jean Arnault, acting head of the UN special coordinator's office, on the Secretary-General's report for 21 June to 20 October; the Council renewed its support for UNIFIL and urged all sides to protect UN personnel (An-Nahar). No decision on France's plan for a force to follow UNIFIL was reported; Lebanese papers had reported competing French and US-backed proposals before the meeting (Daily Lebanon). The National News Agency also reported a change of command in UNIFIL's eastern sector and the Spanish battalion at Ibl al-Saqi, which it called the last rotation of Spanish units. No official Israeli reaction to the EU Support Mission in Lebanon and no new contributing country were found.

Trump and Xi

NewShenzhen: Xi and Trump agree to draft an "action plan" for their relationship, and the three leaders' joint declaration says a nuclear war "cannot be won" and pledges UN reform and more refined-fuel output

The Chinese readout says Xi asked Washington to "do the right thing" on Taiwan and called for cooperation on AI safety, "ensuring that AI always remains under human control"; it quotes Trump as saying the United States does not support Taiwan independence. Lianhe Zaobao notes that this line did not appear in the US public account of the meeting. Zaobao reports the meeting lasted about an hour and was their third this year, after Beijing in May and Washington in September; Trump cited the "30 for 30" tariff framework, in which each side cuts tariffs on about $30 billion of goods, and China's Commerce Ministry said the agriculture working group of the US–China trade council will meet soon. In the separate three-way "Heads of State Declaration toward Peace," published by the White House, Trump, Xi and Vladimir Putin committed to reform the UN, said they plan to increase production and supply of refined petroleum products, said the International Criminal Court and similar bodies "must not abuse their jurisdiction," and reaffirmed that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought," with a three-way dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. CNBC noted the statement does not mention Russia's war in Ukraine or the US war with Iran.

France presidential election

NewTwo new polls put Mélenchon clearly second and Philippe further behind; Le Pen still leads every scenario; Lisnard narrowly leads a test of a right-and-center primary

In the same Ifop poll David Lisnard rises to as much as 12 percent (up 3), Bruno Retailleau falls to 4 to 5, Raphaël Glucksmann has 8 to 11.5, Éric Zemmour 3 and Teddy Riner, tested for the first time, 4; 88 percent of Le Pen's voters and 85 percent of Mélenchon's say they are sure of their choice, against fewer than half of Philippe's, Attal's, Lisnard's or Glucksmann's. A YouGov poll for Le HuffPost (12 to 17 November, 1,030 adults, 952 of them registered voters; published 18 November) has Le Pen 32, Mélenchon 19, Glucksmann 9 and Philippe 8 when the right and center run several candidates; with Philippe as the only candidate of the right and center, Le Pen 40, Mélenchon 22, Philippe 16 and Glucksmann 12. In YouGov's runoffs Le Pen beats Philippe 54 to 46, Attal 55 to 45, Glucksmann 58 to 42 and Mélenchon 63 to 37, and Philippe beats Mélenchon 61 to 39 (figures from the poll's official notice). A separate Ifop poll for Le Figaro (13 to 18 November, 2,542 registered voters, 996 of whom plan to vote in a primary) found 38 percent might take part in a right-and-center primary; with Philippe in it, Lisnard has 29 percent, Philippe 28, Attal 25 and Retailleau 18, a gap within the margin of error; without Philippe, Attal leads with 38, Lisnard 33 and Retailleau 29. An Ifop poll for the weekly Franc-Tireur estimates that 38 percent of registered voters would abstain in a Mélenchon–Le Pen runoff, against 35 percent in a Philippe–Le Pen runoff. On Polymarket at about 5:30 AM Israel time on 20 November, Le Pen was at 45.5 percent (49.4 on 18 November), Lisnard 19, Mélenchon 14.5, Philippe 5.5 and Christine Lagarde 5.3; betting odds are not polls.
