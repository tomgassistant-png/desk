Lebanon, Syria and the Druze

NewMilitary court prosecutor charges 13 over a Hezbollah truck carrying about 800 kg of explosives

Two Hezbollah members are in custody, Haitham Ahmad Amhaz and Ali Shawqi al-Kurdi. Search notices were issued for six others, among them a Russian national married to Amhaz; five are known only by aliases. The charges include undermining the authority of the state, and the file goes to the first military investigating judge. Hezbollah MP Hussein Hajj Hassan called the case politically motivated, and Justice Minister Adel Nassar has asked that it be heard in a civilian court.

Quebec

NewQuebec's PQ government plans to bar religious symbols for elementary school pupils: "non-negotiable"

Critics include the National Council of Canadian Muslims (Stephen Brown), Rabbi Lisa Grushcow of Montreal, and the Canadian Civil Liberties Association, whose Anaïs Bussières McNicoll raised the likely use of the notwithstanding clause, which lets a province override parts of the Charter of Rights (CBC). Principals' associations, through Kathleen Legault and Francis Côté, said religious symbols are not a problem in schools (Journal de Québec). The PQ governs as a minority: Québec solidaire will vote against, the Liberals under Charles Milliard have not taken a position, and Éric Duhaime's Conservatives had not answered. The Supreme Court of Canada's ruling on Quebec's earlier secularism law (Bill 21) is expected this fall.

SpaceX, xAI and Cursor

NewJudge refuses to dismiss the NAACP's Clean Air Act suit over xAI's turbine plant for Colossus 2

The court rejected xAI's argument that citizen lawsuits are unconstitutional, and held that because the plant is not a "mobile source" under federal rules, even so-called temporary turbines need air permits. The same court had recently denied a similar motion from the Justice Department. It has not yet ruled on the NAACP's request for a preliminary injunction. The plaintiffs say the turbines can emit more than 5,000 tons of nitrogen oxides a year; that figure is theirs and was not checked against an independent source.

UK breakup politics

NewBurnham meets Swinney and ap Iorwerth in Glasgow: more devolution of job support and innovation money, but no referendums

The UK government will look at further devolution of employment support for young people in Scotland and Wales, and at devolving innovation funding. Scotland's first minister, John Swinney (SNP), called it "a welcome start" and pledged to work "constructively and collaboratively," but Downing Street said its review would not include a second Scottish independence referendum. Wales's first minister, Rhun ap Iorwerth (Plaid Cymru), called the meeting positive and welcomed a "step forward" on the Welsh government's borrowing powers. Northern Ireland was represented only by officials, after the dispute over the Drumcree parade; Burnham called the absence of its first and deputy first ministers "disappointing."
