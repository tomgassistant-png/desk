Israel election

NewGantz pulls Blue and White out of the race; Sa'ar and Smotrich press Winter to follow, and he refuses

Benny Gantz announced on Monday afternoon, 19 October, that his Blue and White party will not run in the 27 October election, reversing his statement a week earlier that he would stay in the race 'no matter what' (Ynet; Israel Hayom; Jerusalem Post). Polls had put the party at about 1 percent, far below the 3.25 percent threshold. Gantz said he would 'continue fighting for unity' and for a broad Zionist government. Because the withdrawal comes so late, the party stays on the ballot, but votes for it will be wasted (Times of Israel). A source close to Gantz told Ynet the timing was set so that soldiers, who begin voting on Tuesday, 20 October, would not waste their votes; Israel Hayom's sources said he was promised nothing in return. Eisenkot, Golan and Lapid praised the decision, and Yoaz Hendel asked Gantz's voters to move to his Reservists list. On the right, Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar urged right-wing parties below or near the threshold to withdraw, citing the 1992 election, when, he said, wasted right-wing votes let the left reach 61 seats; Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called on Ofer Winter to 'take responsibility' (Jerusalem Post). Winter said Amcha Yisrael will run 'until the end,' and Noam's Avi Maoz and the Haredi Public party's Moti Leitner also said they will stay in (Channel 14; Jerusalem Post).

Lebanon

NewTampa talks open Tuesday; Lebanon says it will refuse direct army-to-army coordination with Israel

Lebanese and Israeli military delegations meet on Tuesday and Wednesday, 20 and 21 October, at US Central Command in Tampa, with US Marine Corps Major General Joseph Clearfield leading the American side, according to official Lebanese sources quoted by LBCI and Naharnet; earlier reports had said the talks would run to 22 October. L'Orient Today reports that Washington and Israel have set direct coordination between the two armies as the condition for the talks to move forward, and that political negotiations are off the table for now. Lebanese officials told The National that Beirut will reject that: 'There are Israelis and Lebanese, with the Americans in the middle. That's it and that's how it will remain.' A Lebanese military source said no decision to expand the pilot zones or set up a verification mechanism is expected, and officials said they expect little progress before Israel's election. According to LBCI, the Lebanese side will say Israel has not respected the terms of the first pilot zone, including strikes, re-entry into the area and the killing of a Lebanese civilian. Netanyahu said on Sunday that Israel will stay in its zone in south Lebanon as long as Hezbollah is not disarmed (The National). MTV's correspondent in Tampa reported that the first day will review the pilot zones and run from 4 p.m. to 11 p.m. Beirut time (5 p.m. to midnight Israel time); that schedule is from MTV only and is unconfirmed.

Israeli data centers

NewData center rules: about 1,300 new megawatts until 2030, NIS 2 million guarantees, and a separate plan to give sites land without tenders

Fuller reports on the Electricity Authority's 18 October decision explain the difference between earlier accounts. Beyond about 2,000 megawatts of connections already approved, the authority will allocate about 1,300 new megawatts until 2030: about 1,000 for large commercial data centers and about 300 for the government, bringing the total to roughly 3,000 to 3,300 megawatts, which Calcalist says equals about 20 percent of Israel's projected electricity use in 2030 (Calcalist/Ctech; Globes). Each new site is capped at 200 megawatts; developers must post guarantees of NIS 2 million per megawatt, and sites must have backup arrangements that let them be cut off from the grid at times of extreme demand. The full decision will be published in the coming days for three weeks of public comment and may change. Avi Shkouri of Psagot told Globes that only about 4 percent of open requests can be met, and that the largest planned projects, including Mega DC in Hadera (about 1,100 megawatts), Kidmat Galil and Nesher (875) and Neve Yamin (720), will have to be redesigned or split. Globes also reports that at a meeting at the Government Secretariat with Economy Minister Nir Barkat, Cabinet Secretary Yossi Fuchs and Erez Askal of the national AI directorate, officials agreed to advance recognizing data centers as productive industry in national priority areas, so they could receive state land without a tender at a set price; the Finance Ministry's budget division says it did not agree, and the Economy Ministry says the decision is not final. The land plan is reported only by Globes and is unconfirmed.

Trump and Xi

NewSCMP: US and Chinese negotiators push for investment deals before the November Trump–Xi meeting in Shenzhen; China adds export controls on drones and tunnel borers

The South China Morning Post reported on 20 October, citing several unnamed sources, that US and Chinese negotiators have made 'considerable progress' on setting up a joint Board of Investment ahead of Trump and Xi's planned meeting at the APEC summit in Shenzhen in November, modeled on the Board of Trade led by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and Vice Premier He Lifeng. According to the report, the sides are discussing a shortlist of possible Chinese investments in non-sensitive US sectors such as electric vehicles, battery storage and power equipment, or possibly consumer goods; one source said the remaining issues have 'major gaps.' The paper also reports that both sides are using leverage, rare earths for China and jet engine and aircraft parts for the United States, in talks on extending the trade truce, which now runs to January 2027. These points rest on anonymous sources and are not independently confirmed. Separately, on 19 October China's commerce ministry issued two announcements, effective 18 November, that require export licenses for long-range and long-endurance drones and key drone parts, and for tunnel boring machines, some excavators and high-altitude shotcrete machines bound for 'sensitive countries and regions' in disputes with China; the notices do not name those destinations (Anadolu; Peter's World, citing the ministry's announcements). No US government response had been published.

Greenland

NewEU presents its Arctic strategy today, with a secure undersea cable to Greenland; Nuuk's vote on the US defense agreement is set for 3 November

The European Commission is due to present a new Arctic strategy on Tuesday, 20 October, and drafts seen by Euronews and AFP say the EU will connect Greenland to Europe with a 'sovereign and secure submarine cable' and work on a longer-term cable from Europe through Greenland and Canada to Japan (Euronews also names South Korea). The drafts also propose joint procurement or pooling of icebreakers, including nuclear-powered ones, an Arctic cable hub with Norway and Iceland to monitor and repair undersea cables, and better surveillance of the Russian 'shadow fleet' (Euronews; AFP via France 24). According to AFP, the draft never names Trump, mentions the United States only as a possible participant in a regular summit of 'Nordic neighborhood' partners, and adds no new money beyond the €200 million for Greenland that Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced in September; Euronews reports that €107 million of EU funds is already earmarked for connectivity projects involving Greenland. These details come from drafts and may change in the final text. In Nuuk, the agenda of Greenland's parliament, Inatsisartut, lists the second reading of the 22 September US–Denmark–Greenland defense agreement for 3 November; the agreement also needs the Danish parliament's consent before it can take effect (Inatsisartut; 2eu.brussels).

Non-Latin script digitization

NewBDRC releases Yigdzin, a free open model for reading Tibetan texts

The Buddhist Digital Resource Center (BDRC) in Boston has released Yigdzin, an open OCR model that converts scanned Tibetan pages into searchable text, announced on 16 October and not reported here before. It reads both the dbu can and dbu med scripts in woodblock prints, manuscripts and modern books, is free to use at ocr.bdrc.io, and is published under the Apache 2.0 license on Hugging Face (BDRC; Hugging Face model card). According to BDRC's own model card, the 810-million-parameter model was built by Élie Roux and Eric Werner on Baidu's PaddleOCR-VL, has a median character error rate of about 1.4 percent on a 1,070-page test set, and is being applied to about 25 million scanned pages; these figures are BDRC's and have not been tested independently. BDRC will present the model in a public webinar on 28 October. The work was funded by the Khyentse Foundation.

France presidential election

NewNuñez offers to resign and Macron refuses; first local curfews in Paris, Lyon, Nice, the Lille area and Bordeaux

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez offered his resignation after Macron's address on the evening of 18 October, and the Élysée said on Monday morning that the president refused it because of 'the need for continuous command of the police,' while asking for 'new methods of action' against rioters (franceinfo; BFMTV; Le Parisien). Nuñez told France Inter, 'I am responsible for everything that happens,' and admitted a 'lack of anticipation' and a 'loss of control' in some cities; Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin said on France 2, 'We incontestably failed' (TF1 Info live). After a crisis meeting Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu announced three decisions: a 'massive' penal response, partial and local curfews ordered by prefects, and support for businesses, with 100 percent state-paid partial unemployment and a special fund; the government says at least 3,000 shops were damaged or looted over the weekend. He said he would not put all of Paris under curfew and that a state of emergency remains possible 'if it is useful.' The curfews on Monday night: in Paris, the central esplanade of the Place de la République and streets leading into Les Halles from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m., with demonstrations banned from 8 p.m. in République, Châtelet, Bastille, the main railway stations, the Champs-Élysées and around the Élysée, Matignon and Parliament; in Lyon, the city center from 9 p.m. to 6 a.m.; in Nice, a curfew for minors from 9 p.m. until 25 October, ordered by Mayor Éric Ciotti; in Lille, Douai, Denain and Wattignies, a curfew for unaccompanied minors from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. on Monday and Tuesday nights; and in Bordeaux, for unaccompanied minors from 10 p.m. to 7 a.m., from 20 to 26 October (Le Parisien; TF1 Info; franceinfo). Le Parisien's reporters described a calm first night in Paris, with gendarmes sealing the Forum des Halles and a police helicopter overhead; ABC of Madrid also reported calm. In Lyon, about 100 members of the ultra-right joined a rally in support of shopkeepers and chanted slogans such as 'Islam out of Europe' (Le Progrès, as quoted by Le Parisien). Paris Mayor Emmanuel Grégoire said the curfew 'must remain exceptional.'

NewThree new runoff polls still show a technical tie; Polymarket slips to about 77 percent for Flávio

Quebec

NewLiberal MNAs sworn in, all to the people of Quebec; PQ cabinet to be sworn in on Wednesday without the lieutenant governor

The 40 Liberal members of the National Assembly were sworn in at the Red Room on 19 October; all of them took only the oath to the people of Quebec, not the optional oath to the King, and about half took it in both French and English (Canadian Press via L'actualité). Liberal leader Charles Milliard presented his party as a 'government in waiting' and said it would be a reliable partner in solving crises but 'tough and intractable' on immigration to the regions, public finances and the national question; André Fortin is the Liberal house leader. One Liberal, Affine Lwalalika (Vachon), was not sworn in because of a judicial recount. Premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon has written to Lieutenant Governor Manon Jeannotte asking her not to attend the swearing-in of his cabinet on Wednesday, 21 October; the oaths will be received by the secretary general of the government, and her office told Le Devoir that she will follow the ceremony on the Assembly's channel and sign the appointments at the same time (Le Devoir; the letter is reported by Le Devoir only and is not independently confirmed). The PQ has said it wants to abolish the lieutenant governor's post, which several experts consider very difficult under the Canadian Constitution.
