Israel election

NewThe Knesset is expected to elect Matan Kahana (Yashar!) speaker on Tuesday after 67 MKs demanded the vote; Likud and Smotrich offered support on conditions about Arab deputy speakers, and Eisenkot's bloc refused

The vote was set after 67 MKs, including members of Ra'am and the Joint List, signed a demand to hold it. ynet reports that in a negotiating session Likud first proposed postponing the replacement of speaker Amir Ohana as a goodwill gesture for the unity talks, and then offered to back Kahana itself on condition that no deputy speakers come from the Arab parties; Eisenkot's bloc refused. Religious Zionism chair Bezalel Smotrich sent Kahana a letter offering his party's support, and saying that after talks with Netanyahu Likud would join, on two conditions: that Kahana publicly commit to serve only until a government is formed and resign if a new government wants another speaker, and that he not appoint deputy speakers from lists that do not define Hamas as a terror organization (Walla). A Kan headline says Kahana answered that he was surprised by a demand to ignore a public that makes up 21% of the population (headline only, not opened). Journalist Tal Schneider wrote on X that Shas may abstain or even vote for Kahana. ynet also notes that Liran Avishar Ben-Horin (Beyachad) is the first woman to chair the Finance Committee; possible conflict-of-interest claims tied to her husband's strategic-communications firm were raised, and she denied them. On coalition talks, Yedioth Ahronoth reports (single source) that Yashar! MKs Inbar Gitai and Elazar Stern presented partners a draft military-service outline it would offer the Haredi parties: a reset of status, in effect a retroactive amnesty, for draft evaders aged 18 to 26 for a limited window of a few months to half a year, in three tracks: service-deferral quotas for some aged 18 to 20, about one to two months of "stage B" reserve training for those aged 21 to 26, and a full exemption for those 26 and older; it addresses what the IDF estimates is a "pool" of about 90,000 Haredim already facing desertion or evasion proceedings. United Torah Judaism chair Yaakov Asher confirmed talks with both sides and said his party will not sit with Ra'am (Channel 14). Eisenkot visited Likud MK Elisha Madan's "unity tent" outside the Knesset and said that by unity he means mutual responsibility and shared service, not a unity government (Arutz Sheva reporter Hezki Baruch on X). The official results on the Central Elections Committee site are unchanged (turnout 77.98%; Likud 27, Yashar! 21, Beyachad 12, Joint List 11, Democrats 8, Ra'am 8, Yisrael Beytenu 7, Otzma Yehudit 7, United Torah Judaism 7, Shas 6, Religious Zionism 6).

Lebanon, Syria and the Druze

NewWaltz leaves Lebanon without an official proposal for a force after UNIFIL; Israeli outlets report a French plan for troops to patrol with the Lebanese army, which Israel opposes south of the Litani

LBCI says France is drafting a UN Security Council resolution aimed at the end of the year, that Waltz said "no country can have two armies," and that he did not meet army commander Rodolphe Haykal. i24NEWS (Amichai Stein) and the Jerusalem Post, citing three sources, report that France is advancing a plan outside the UN to send hundreds and possibly thousands of troops to patrol alongside the Lebanese army, with training bases north of the Litani; no other country has joined so far. Unnamed Israeli officials said Israel does not want such a force south of the Litani, and the reports say Jerusalem also opposes a French idea of about 500 UN monitors. This French plan is separate from the EU mission (EUSM); no new Israeli reaction to EUSM was found.

France presidential election

NewFirst big TV debate: Retailleau and Mélenchon clash, Le Pen's rivals attack her funding plans; Le Pen tells Ukraine's ambassador France could no longer help Kyiv financially

Colombia and Israel

NewFormer Colombian ministers and ambassadors criticize De la Espriella's Israel moves: the embassy in Jerusalem, recognition of the Golan and leaving the ICJ case

They say President Abelardo De la Espriella has subordinated Colombia to the Trump administration's interests, and among the changes they criticize are moving the embassy in Israel to Jerusalem, recognizing Israeli sovereignty over the Golan Heights and withdrawing Colombia's intervention in South Africa's case against Israel at the International Court of Justice. They also criticize joining the Shield of the Americas, US military exercises in Colombia, the return of aerial spraying of illicit crops and the closing of embassies in Africa and the Caribbean. Signers include former foreign minister Luis Gilberto Murillo and other officials of the Petro government (2022–2026); they announced a progressive foreign-policy observatory.

NewEnlight and Shemen plan a data center of up to 150 MW in Haifa Bay
