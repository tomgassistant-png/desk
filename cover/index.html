Israel election

NewElection committee chair lets Likud inspect materials from about 190 polling stations in Arab towns ahead of a possible appeal; Likud says it will file next week; Shin Bet letter published in part

Likud may receive, on disc and with personal details removed, the phone-system (IVR) voting-tracking forms and the notes and decisions of the polling committees, and may inspect the marking lists of who voted at the committee's logistics center, where about 15 committee staff teams will work with Likud observers on consecutive working days starting today, 12 November. Sohlberg refused access to video and audio recorded by election-integrity inspectors, which he limited to police, prosecutors and the committee's legal advisers, and refused some forms because they carry no voting times; he left any question of evidence to the court that would hear an appeal. The Joint List said no defect has been proven. The decision allows inspection; it is not a finding of wrongdoing. Maariv reported that Likud plans to file its appeal next Wednesday (18 November) in the Jerusalem District Court through its legal adviser Ilan Bombach, citing about 700 complaints to the committee, use of the "Elector" app, payments and fuel cards, double voting, paid photographing of ballots and a gap of about 6,000 votes between Ra'am and Shas; these are Likud's claims and are unverified, and no filing was found. On the separate foreign-influence track, ynet's Ronen Bergman published parts of the Shin Bet's letter to the committee, which speaks of "well-founded suspicions of widespread foreign influence" with foreign funding "in very high sums" and asks that such subversion be handled like terrorism; enforcement sources told him Shin Bet investigators found no fuel-card bribery, and people familiar with the material put the money at a few hundred thousand shekels at most, while the Shin Bet says its chief never cited 100 million shekels. Haaretz (paywalled; summarized by Arab48) reported, citing a security official, that Shin Bet chief David Zini approved special technological tools and an operational unit for the probe, while police see at most party-financing violations. From the other side, FakeReporter (an interested party) and the Brandeis Center wrote to social platforms citing a 428% jump in anti-Arab posts on X within two days of the vote and "stolen election" claims spread by officials. Tal Schneider noted the working assumption that Gideon Sa'ar was made interior minister so he would be a respondent in Likud's appeals; a transitional government can appoint ministers without a Knesset vote. Haaretz (paywalled) also profiled three Bedouin Ra'am activists arrested on election day who call it political persecution. No change to the official results was found.

France presidential election

NewFirst poll testing Christine Lagarde puts her at 14%, ahead of Édouard Philippe; Retailleau proposes a right-and-center primary on 31 January and 7 February

In the Lagarde scenario Marine Le Pen has 30.5% and Jean-Luc Mélenchon 20.5%, his first score above 20% in this series; in a wide field Le Pen has 27%, Mélenchon 19% and judoka Teddy Riner 2%. Lagarde's ECB term ends 7 February and her memoir, "Lady First," is due in February; she has not declared. Bruno Retailleau wrote on 10 November to Philippe, Gabriel Attal and Lisnard proposing a primary of the right and center on 31 January and 7 February; franceinfo reports only Philippe opposes it. Separately, 110 constitutional law scholars wrote in Le Monde that Le Pen's plan to change the constitution by referendum under Article 11 is irregular. On Polymarket at about 4:20 AM Israel time today: Le Pen 46.4%, Lisnard 15.4% (up from 13), Mélenchon 13.5%, Philippe 7.5% (down from 8.5) and Lagarde 7.1%; betting odds are not polls.

Israeli data centers

NewKKR sells its unfinished Petah Tikva data center to Nofar for about $85 million; Sonol, Afcon and Pentelight plan a gas plant and up to 200 MW of data centers in Haifa Bay

KKR had invested about $70 million to $75 million of an original budget of $150 million to $180 million; the Electricity Authority has stopped new power allocations until the end of the year. Separately, Sonol, Afcon and Pentelight (Quadro) signed a memorandum of understanding on 10 November for a gas-fired plant of about 900 MW, a data center of up to 200 MW and storage on about 48 dunams of Sonol land in Haifa Bay next to an Israel Electric Corporation plant (map, approximate: https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Haifa+Bay). The plant alone is estimated at about 4 billion shekels; shares are Sonol 45%, Afcon 27.5% and Pentelight 27.5%. It is at an early stage and needs approvals.
