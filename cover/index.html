Israel election

NewThree Sunday polls: Kan has Yashar 23 and Likud 21; i24NEWS has Likud 25; Channel 14 has Netanyahu's bloc at exactly 61

Three seat polls published on Sunday evening, 18 October, nine days before the vote, point in different directions. Kan News (Kantar, Dudu Hasid; 554 respondents, margin of error 4.2 points) gives Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar 23 seats and Likud 21, each up one from last week; Bennett's Beyachad 12; the Democrats 9, their highest since early September; Otzma Yehudit, Shas, Yisrael Beytenu and the Joint List (Hadash–Ta'al–Balad) 8 each; Religious Zionism 7, up from 5; United Torah Judaism 7; Ra'am 5; and the Hendel–Zelekha Reservists 4. Ofer Winter's Amcha Yisrael falls below the threshold at 3.1 percent. The Eisenkot bloc has 52 seats and the Netanyahu bloc 51, with the Arab parties at 13. Eisenkot leads Netanyahu 43 to 35 percent as more suitable for prime minister (Netanyahu had 38 a week earlier); Netanyahu leads Bennett 38 to 34. Thirty percent of respondents said they had not yet decided how to vote. An i24NEWS poll by Direct Polls (Tzuriel Sharon; 525 respondents, margin of error 4.2 points) has Likud at 25, down two; Yashar 21; Beyachad 13; Otzma Yehudit 10, its first double-digit result in this series; the Democrats 9; United Torah Judaism, Shas and the Joint List 8 each; and Yisrael Beytenu, Religious Zionism and Ra'am 6 each. The coalition parties total 57, the Zionist opposition 49 and the Arab parties 14; Winter (2.9 percent) and the Reservists (2.4 percent) are out. A Channel 14 poll by Next Data (559 respondents, fielded 18 October) keeps Likud at 29 and raises Yashar from 20 to 22; Shas and Beyachad have 10 each; the Democrats and United Torah Judaism 8 each; Religious Zionism, Otzma Yehudit and the Joint List 7 each; Yisrael Beytenu and Ra'am 6 each. It puts the right-wing bloc at exactly 61, down one from Wednesday, and narrows Netanyahu's head-to-head lead over Eisenkot to 51–49. On Polymarket, Eisenkot stood at about 46 percent early on Monday, Israel time, Netanyahu at about 29.5 and Bennett at about 12.5 (about $48.5 million traded), against about 47, 32 and 14 on Sunday evening.

Lebanon

NewBefore the Tampa talks: Lebanon's demands, Netanyahu's verdict on the army's pilot zones, and a Hezbollah defense of arms transfers

The US–Lebanon–Israel military meeting at US Central Command in Tampa is set for Tuesday, 20 October. According to Arab media cited by Israel Hayom, Lebanon's delegation has been told to seek a halt to Israeli air strikes and bulldozer work in border villages, a timetable for Israeli withdrawal, wider Lebanese army deployment along the border, procedures for withdrawal and handover, and a mechanism for handling violations; Beirut also wants its army to be the only body that carries out security checks and disarmament. Netanyahu told reporters on Sunday that Israel had let the Lebanese army show it could clear Hezbollah infrastructure in two pilot areas, but that it had 'not demonstrated sufficient capabilities,' and that Israel would do the job itself if the army cannot (Times of Israel; not found in a second report). Hezbollah MP Hassan Fadlallah, speaking in Tibnin on Sunday, defended the group's right to move weapons to the front: 'How will it fight this enemy? Are we supposed to fight with bags of potatoes?' (L'Orient Today; not independently confirmed). After Army Radio reported on Sunday that the IDF allows olive picking inside its security strip, residents of the Arqoub villages refused to go to their groves near Kfar Shouba on the IDF's terms, which, according to Lebanese reports, included handing over phones and ID cards, a limit of 100 people and 25 vehicles, and no Lebanese army or UNIFIL escort; the head of the Kfar Shouba municipality said an Israeli patrol then ordered farmers in another area to leave (L'Orient Today; Lebanon Times). No IDF account of these conditions was found.

Israeli data centers

NewElectricity Authority caps each new data center at 200 megawatts; reports differ on the national ceiling

The plenum of Israel's Electricity Authority decided on Sunday evening, 18 October, that each new data center may connect to the grid at no more than 200 megawatts, and that developers must post guarantees of NIS 2 million per megawatt, or NIS 400 million for a full 200-megawatt site, to keep out speculative applications (Globes; TheMarker). The two papers differ on the total. Globes reports a ceiling of 1,300 megawatts for the whole sector, of which 300 megawatts must serve local users and the rest may serve foreign buyers of computing power. TheMarker reports a proposed ceiling of 3 gigawatts plus 300 megawatts reserved for the defense establishment, and says half of each guarantee would be returned only after 60 percent of the requested capacity is operating. TheMarker says the details had not yet been fully published, and the authority's own text was not found online, so the overall ceiling is unconfirmed. Other criteria, including a split between the center of the country and the periphery and preferences for developers who bring their own generation and storage, go to public comment; Globes expects the first new approvals to take at least several months, probably until after a new government is formed, and notes that more than one gigawatt of data centers was already approved under the earlier first-come policy. Requests submitted to the grid operator total about 27,000 megawatts, against a record peak demand of about 17,300 megawatts in August 2025; the freeze on new applications above 8 megawatts ends in early December (Bizportal).

France presidential election

NewMacron speaks of "anger and shame," promises the looters will pay, but declares no state of emergency and offers no new measures

President Macron addressed the nation for about nine minutes at 8 p.m. Paris time on 18 October (9 p.m. in Israel), saying, "We all feel anger and shame tonight." He blamed the violence of 17 October on "agitators from ultra-left groups, joined by many delinquents" and said "nothing explains it, nothing excuses it"; those who destroyed "must pay," those who looted "must repay," and the state will be "intractable." He said prefects, together with mayors, may go as far as banning some gatherings, ordered "transparency" on the policing strategy and told Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez to "draw all the lessons" on intelligence and anticipation. A crisis cell will help shopkeepers and local governments from Monday. He asked parties and unions to condemn the violence "without ambiguity" and said, "The country needs order... not escalation" and "Our country deserves a fine presidential election in six months." He said he understood high school pupils and students and that a youth consultation platform has received more than 100,000 contributions, but he announced no new cost-of-living measures; the prime minister is to meet parties, unions and local officials. He did not declare a state of emergency, which the right had demanded, and he keeps his trip to four Gulf countries from Monday to Wednesday. France 24, citing AFP, noted that he said nothing about the protesters who were badly injured.

Brazil

NewFirst runoff debate: Lula and Flávio trade corruption charges; Flávio admits his father's government fell short on health

Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro traded accusations in their first runoff debate, on Band on the evening of 18 October (it began about 15 minutes late and ended about 10:15 p.m. Brasília time, 4:15 a.m. Monday in Israel). Flávio raised the INSS pension fraud and the names of Roberta Luchsinger and Lula's son Fábio Luís ("Lulinha"); Lula raised the Banco Master case and money from its owner, Daniel Vorcaro, for a film about Jair Bolsonaro, and told the Bolsonaros, over Eduardo Bolsonaro's campaign in the United States, "You are betraying the homeland" (Exame). In one exchange Flávio asked whether "60 percent of prisoners voted for you"; Lula answered, "I want 100 percent, including your father." Flávio admitted that health "was not the best result" of his father's government and said, "I make my mea culpa here" (O Globo); he promised a public health service "of Einstein standard," a "war on the PCC as narco-terrorists," a Ministry of Prosperity and a plan to buy household and business debts from the banks. O Globo columnist Míriam Leitão wrote that Lula had the advantage in the second block and that Flávio's debt plan recycles the government's own Desenrola program. In the third block Lula challenged Flávio to open his bank and phone records to the heads of the Supreme Court and the Electoral Court "so that the Brazilian people can find out who the thief is"; Flávio replied, "Your money is always off the books" (Band; CartaCapital). Political scientists quoted by Valor expect little effect on votes, since Quaest found 91 percent and Datafolha 93 percent of voters say their choice is final. On Polymarket (about $188 million traded) Flávio was at about 78.5 percent early on 19 October, down from about 80 on Sunday evening, and Lula at 20.5. New polls registered with the electoral court for release from 19 October include AtlasIntel (13 to 18 October, 5,000 interviews) and PoderData; Quaest is due on 21 October and Datafolha on 22 October. The runoff is on 25 October.

Trenton

NewHealth-tech startup MyMonitor.AI moves its headquarters from Philadelphia to downtown Trenton

MyMonitor.AI, a health-care artificial intelligence startup, has moved its headquarters from Philadelphia to Suite 202 at 1 West State Street in downtown Trenton; Mayor Reed Gusciora joined founder and chief executive Charles Mbata for a ribbon cutting on 13 October (Trenton Journal, from a city release; Quadratín). The company was one of 13 in the first Trenton group of the Novella Center for Entrepreneurship's Conscious Venture Lab, a free 14-week accelerator that began in October 2025. Government role: the accelerator is paid for by a City of Trenton grant from its federal Coronavirus Local Fiscal Recovery Fund money (Community News, May 2025); no public subsidy to the company itself has been reported, and no job numbers were given. The ribbon cutting took place before the last edition but had not been reported here.

SpaceX, xAI and Cursor

NewGrok Bot update: company says computer tasks finish three times faster; The Grid Fin reports X Chat access and new finance connectors

The Grok Bot account announced on X on 16 October an update to "quality, polish, and speed": it says computer tasks now finish three times sooner on average and other replies arrive in less than half the time, chat cards have a cleaner design, voice calls are faster and better at remembering past conversations, email supports plus aliases for routines, tagging @bot in an X post starts a task, and notifications go only to the device in use. The Grid Fin reports that the same update lets Grok Bot read a user's X Chat messages once X is connected, announced by Paul Vann, and adds connectors including Robinhood, Coinbase, Trello, PostHog, beehiiv and eToro; those additions are not independently confirmed here. The company has not said how it measured the speed gain, and the official changelog still lists version 0.70.2 of 11 October as the latest release.
