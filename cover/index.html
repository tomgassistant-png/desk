Israel election

NewEisenkot says he will bring a government he leads to a Knesset vote and form a "national emergency government" if talks on a broad coalition fail; Netanyahu answers that "responsibility is unity," and Likud's reported terms include Netanyahu as prime minister for the whole term

"This is not a threat for negotiating purposes," Eisenkot wrote; if his efforts to form a broad government fail, he intends to form a "national emergency government" and to widen it before or after it is sworn in, and every Knesset member will have to decide between a new government and another election "while Netanyahu is still caretaker prime minister" (ynet; Srugim). He said talks with the Haredi parties seek a military-service outline based on Yashar!'s "service for all" plan, and that with Likud there is "a great distance between the rhetoric and the deeds," though he will give those talks another chance; the party leaders also agreed to bring retired Justice Yosef Elron's appointment as state comptroller to a vote in the coming days (Srugim). ynet says the emergency option would be a minority government of the change bloc that counts on Ra'am's support from outside without a coalition deal; the Joint List has said it will not oppose replacing Netanyahu's government, and Yisrael Beytenu chair Avigdor Liberman has said he will vote against such a government and not even abstain. New Yisrael Beytenu MK Sharon Sharabi wrote on X that he will not be part of a coalition that relies on "terror supporters," "from inside or outside" (Srugim). Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the same day that he had briefed Eisenkot, Naftali Bennett and Liberman on "the challenging security situation," that "we are standing at a historic crossroads; there is no spin here and no exaggeration," and that "what is required now is responsibility, and responsibility is unity"; he again called the Arab parties' Knesset members "terror supporters" and said the effort to form a narrow government relying on them must stop at once (ynet). ynet notes that Liberman's briefing was given by Netanyahu's military secretary, Maj. Gen. Guy Markizano, not by Netanyahu. In a Saturday analysis, ynet reports, citing people in Eisenkot's bloc, that Likud's offer for a unity government has five main conditions: Netanyahu stays prime minister for the government's whole term, no state commission of inquiry, a draft law along the lines of Boaz Bismuth's bill, Itamar Ben Gvir's party in the government, and the judicial-overhaul laws passed by the last Knesset stay in place. A Likud source involved in the talks denied part of that list but did not deny that Netanyahu wants to serve the whole term; a Times of Israel headline reports the same two main demands (article text not read). The Likud and Yashar! negotiating teams are due to meet again on Sunday, 22 November. ynet adds that even a deal with United Torah Judaism would not reach 61 seats without Shas. No coalition agreement has been signed and no government has been formed. On the election-interference watch, there was no new court ruling, filing or official finding: the Jerusalem District Court has not ruled on Otzma Yehudit's appeal, and no Likud appeal had been reported filed by early Saturday, with Likud's deadline on Sunday, 22 November.

Lebanon, Syria and the Druze

NewOn the eve of Lebanon's Independence Day, Trump urges President Aoun to "seize this opportunity" and Prime Minister Salam says only the state may hold weapons; Israeli strikes hit Kfar Tebnit and Haddatha in the south

Prime Minister Nawaf Salam said independence "is not complete without recovering all of our land" and requires that the state alone decide on war and peace, "with no weapons outside it." Aoun is to address the Lebanese at 6 p.m. on Saturday, 21 November, at a flag-raising at Nahr al-Kalb (National News Agency on X; An-Nahar). An-Nahar reports no clear outcome yet from US–French talks on what follows UNIFIL when it leaves at the end of the year, but says Lebanese hopes have grown since France advanced a formula for a continued international presence; US Ambassador Michel Issa discussed the post-UNIFIL options with Defense Minister Michel Menassa, who said the framework agreement must be applied, starting with an end to daily Israeli attacks and then withdrawal. On the five Hezbollah members charged this week, An-Nahar's sources say the cell in the north was doing field reconnaissance and was not the only such group, and an official source said documents seized from the five gave enough evidence to charge them; Hezbollah MP Hussein Hajj Hassan called the arrests arbitrary and the charges baseless. On Friday, 20 November, Israeli aircraft struck Kfar Tebnit in the Nabatieh district twice, artillery hit Mansouri, Majdal Zoun and Bani Hayyan, and drones flew over Beirut (Arab48, citing the National News Agency); the agency later reported a third strike on Haddatha. No IDF statement on these strikes was found. Arab48 cites the Lebanese Health Ministry's count of at least 4,390 killed since 2 March (ministry figure, not checked separately). No official Israeli reaction to the EU Support Mission in Lebanon and no new contributing country were found.

Trump and Xi

NewAfter Shenzhen: Trump says he and Xi agreed on shared "guardrails" for superintelligence, and Bessent says they agreed humans must always take part in military AI decisions

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC the same day that the two leaders agreed that "humans should always be involved in any military decisions" made with superintelligence and that it "should not be allowed to be autonomous for major military actions," calling this one of the main results of the trip (Lianhe Zaobao; Taiwan's Central News Agency via United Daily News, both citing Reuters and CNBC). No written text of this understanding has been published, and the US account comes from Bessent; China's Foreign Ministry readout of the 19 November meeting said the two countries should work together to ensure that AI "always remains under human control." Bessent also said Nvidia's H200 is still the most advanced chip China can buy under license and that Chinese orders for it are small.

Spain election

NewFeijóo is seen as the winner of the only head-to-head debate, but the vote barely moves; two more debates are set for 23 and 26 November

40dB. for El País and the SER (500 online interviews right after the debate) had 43 percent naming Feijóo the winner and 33.3 percent Sánchez; Sigma Dos for El Mundo had 50.5 to 29; and Ateneo del Dato for elDiario.es had 45.2 to 31.9, with 88 percent of its sample saying they would keep their vote. The roughly two-hour debate, organized by the Television Academy and watched by an average of about 10 million people, covered housing, the economy, social policy, corruption, migration and foreign affairs; El Confidencial reports that Sánchez apologized several times over the rail crashes and the corruption cases. Vox's Santiago Abascal criticized his absence from the debate (El País). RTVE holds a debate of eight party spokespeople on Monday, 23 November, at 22:00 Spain time, and the four candidates for prime minister (Sánchez, Feijóo, Abascal and Ada Colau of Frente Amplio) meet on Thursday, 26 November, at 22:00 (El Confidencial).
