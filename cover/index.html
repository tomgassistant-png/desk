Israel election

NewCount near 90 percent: Netanyahu bloc 53, Eisenkot bloc 48, Arab parties 19; Hendel and Winter fall below the threshold, and about 450,000 double-envelope ballots are now being counted

Seats on that count: Likud 26, Yashar! 21, B'Yachad (Bennett–Lapid) 12, Joint List 12, the Democrats 8, Otzma Yehudit 7, Shas 7, United Torah Judaism 7, Ra'am 7, Yisrael Beytenu 7 and Religious Zionism 6. Yoaz Hendel's list (2.63 percent) and Ofer Winter's list (1.61 percent) are below the 3.25 percent threshold. Counting of the 430,000 to 480,000 double-envelope ballots (soldiers, diplomats, hospital patients, prisoners and others who vote away from their home station) began at about 1 a.m. on Thursday. Ynet says these ballots are worth 10 to 15 seats and that turnout among soldiers was 79.9 percent; a Haaretz headline at about 1:30 a.m. said the early double-envelope count had not changed the split between the blocs (headline only; article not opened). Official results are due on 4 November. On Polymarket early on 29 October, Netanyahu was at 45.5 percent to be the next prime minister (down 7.5 points from the morning before) and Eisenkot at 25.5 percent. Seat totals can still shift by a seat or two when surplus-vote agreements are applied.

Lebanon, Syria and the Druze

NewHezbollah's Qassem: "we do not care who governs Israel"; no talks on disarmament while Israel stays in the south, and the June framework is "null and void"; Israel warns the north of another wave of attacks

Qassem said there will be no discussion of Hezbollah's weapons while what he called the occupation continues, declared the June framework "null and void", and demanded an Israeli withdrawal under the 27 November 2024 ceasefire and UN Security Council Resolution 1701 (Arab News, from AFP; Al-Manar, Hezbollah's own channel). In Israel, the Ma'ale Yosef regional council passed on a Home Front Command warning of another wave of attacks, with explosions possible in the Western Galilee, the Ramim Ridge, the central Upper Galilee and the northern Golan (Maariv). At 10:39 p.m. the army said an interceptor had been launched at a suspicious aerial target over south Lebanon (Maariv).

Brazil

NewFlávio Bolsonaro posts, then deletes, a letter calling himself the "true president-elect"; Trump says he will not get involved, and the election court head defends the count

He alleges fraud without presenting evidence and attacks Justice Cristiano Zanin's order that kept polls open until 7 p.m. President Trump said he is "not going to get involved" while calling it "a very strange election," and confirmed that Flávio has permission to enter the United States (Agência Brasil). Observers from the Organization of American States and the Carter Center found the vote normal (BBC Brasil). The president of the Superior Electoral Court (TSE), Nunes Marques, defended the result and said challenges will be examined. The court's figures: 34,393 votes were cast after 5 p.m. across 497,333 polling sections (32,399 between 5 and 6 p.m., 1,981 between 6 and 7 p.m. and 13 after 7 p.m.), far too few to change the result (O Globo).

France presidential election

NewAttal calls for a "great primary of the democrats" from the center to the moderate right; Philippe says no, and Retailleau's camp calls it "far-fetched and ridiculous"

Édouard Philippe refuses to take part, and people close to Bruno Retailleau called the idea "fantaisiste et ridicule" (far-fetched and ridiculous) (franceinfo). A new finding from the Cluster17 poll for Politico, reported by Le Parisien: 43 percent of French people most want to stop La France insoumise (Mélenchon's party) from taking power, against 30 percent who most want to stop the National Rally and 14 percent the central bloc; among Renaissance voters, 56 percent put stopping LFI first. On Polymarket on 29 October, Le Pen was at about 43 percent to win, little changed.

Quebec

NewSt-Pierre Plamondon formally stays away from the Canada–EU summit in Montreal and asks for his own meeting with EU leaders; Quebec sends Pascal Paradis instead

He warns that the talks put Quebec's health care, education and natural resources at risk, cites the Gérin-Lajoie doctrine (Quebec's claim to speak for itself abroad in areas it controls at home), and asks for a separate meeting with EU leaders. Prime Minister Carney had offered him a seat at the dinner with Ursula von der Leyen and António Costa, and four consultation sessions were held (CBC). Quebec's goods trade with the EU runs a deficit of 16.5 billion dollars (30.3 billion in imports, 13.8 billion in exports). Reports that a request for associate-member status was dropped after German objections are unconfirmed. Conservative leader Éric Duhaime said, "You never win by leaving an empty chair" (La Voix de l'Est).

Trenton

NewMeridian Furniture breaks ground on its 220,000-square-foot Trenton building, with nearly 200 jobs expected

The building ([map, approximate](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=40.2407,-74.7357)) is expected to take about a year and to bring nearly 200 jobs. The map point is Enterprise Avenue from OpenStreetMap, not the exact site.
