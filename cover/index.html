Lebanon

NewCabinet puts off sending Hezbollah's Dahr al-Baidar explosives case to the Judicial Council; Lebanese Forces ministers walk out

At its session on Thursday, 22 October, the cabinet postponed referring the case of the explosives seized at the Dahr al-Baidar checkpoint to the Judicial Council, Lebanon's special court for crimes against state security, until the investigations are finished, although Hezbollah has acknowledged that the explosives belong to it (Naharnet; This Is Beirut). The Lebanese Forces ministers walked out of the session in protest (LBCI). Naharnet reports that Speaker Nabih Berri advised against the referral (one report; unconfirmed). On 23 October Israeli airstrikes hit near the Ali al-Taher hill by Nabatieh al-Fawqa, artillery shelled near Zawtar al-Sharqiya, and drones flew low over Beirut and Tyre, according to Lebanon's National News Agency as reported by Anadolu (single source; unconfirmed).

France presidential election

NewToluna Harris Interactive: Le Pen 33 to 35, Mélenchon qualified in every scenario, Glucksmann passes Attal for the first time

Toluna Harris Interactive's eighth wave for M6 and RTL (19–21 October, 2,094 registered voters, published 23 October) has Le Pen at 33 to 35 percent, down about one point, and Mélenchon second in every scenario. With Attal as the only centrist: Mélenchon 19, Glucksmann 13 and Attal 11, the first time Glucksmann is ahead of Attal in this series. With Philippe as the only centrist: Mélenchon 17, Philippe 16, Glucksmann 13. With both: Mélenchon 17, Philippe 13, Glucksmann 12, Attal 5. Purchasing power (56 percent) and immigration (38 percent) are the issues voters cite most. RTL's headline is that the violence of 17 October has not reshuffled the race (RTL; Toluna report). Polymarket is steady: Le Pen about 46 percent, Philippe about 17, Lisnard about 14, Mélenchon about 11.

Quebec

NewSt-Pierre Plamondon's first measure as premier: $28 million more for homelessness, for 1,000 new shelter and warming-center places

On 23 October Premier Paul St-Pierre Plamondon, who also holds the homelessness file, announced $28 million in emergency funding on top of $18 million already allocated, for a total of $46 million and about 1,000 new places in shelters and warming centers this winter (Canadian Press via the Globe and Mail; Radio-Canada). The reports differ on Montreal's share: about $18 million in the Canadian Press account and $16.3 million in Radio-Canada's. He also promised a summit on homelessness in early 2027 and repeated the Parti Québécois pledge of 20,000 social housing units.

Greenland

NewDanish parliament schedules the Greenland–US defense agreement: debate on 12 November, final vote on 19 November

Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen presented the new Denmark–Greenland–US security and defense agreement to the Folketing on Thursday, 22 October, as a resolution; the first reading is on 12 November and the final vote on 19 November. The agreement, signed in New York on 22 September by Trump, Frederiksen and Jens-Frederik Nielsen, amends the 1951 defense agreement and would let the United States set up two more bases besides Pituffik. It takes effect only after both the Folketing and Greenland's parliament, Inatsisartut, approve it; Inatsisartut held its first reading on 10 October, when Naleraq proposed a referendum. Frederiksen's government needs other parties' votes for a majority in the Folketing (Ritzau via the Copenhagen Post). The Danish dates come from the Ritzau report only and are unconfirmed by a second source; Inatsisartut's vote is still expected on 3 November.

Trump and Xi

NewSCMP: US and Chinese teams work on an AI incident framework as a possible result of the Shenzhen meeting

The South China Morning Post reported on 23 October, citing three unnamed sources, that US and Chinese technical teams are in regular talks to give substance to the plan, announced at Xi's September state visit, for a bilateral channel on AI-related incidents and a US–China AI dialogue. They aim to have a basic framework for handling AI crises ready by the Trump–Xi meeting at APEC in Shenzhen in November, with details later, and one source said the meeting could also set up a standing body for AI cooperation with officials, researchers and industry. The Chinese embassy in Washington said on 22 October that Beijing 'remains open to maintaining communication' through the AI dialogue. The report rests on anonymous sources in one outlet and is unconfirmed.
