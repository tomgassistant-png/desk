Israel election

NewIsrael votes with 77.7 percent turnout, the highest since 1999; exit polls show no 61-seat majority, and overnight updates moved toward Netanyahu

At 10 p.m., Channel 12, Channel 13 and Kan all had the Netanyahu bloc at 50 seats and the Eisenkot-led "change" bloc at 52, with Likud the largest party (Channel 12: Likud 24, Yashar! 22) and the Arab parties at about 17. The right-leaning channels differed: i24NEWS had 55 to 49 and Channel 14 had 60 to 43. Overnight updates moved toward Netanyahu: Kan revised to a 51–51 tie, and Channel 12 revised to Netanyahu bloc 52, Eisenkot bloc 49 and Arab parties 19, with Likud up to 27 and Yashar! down to 21. With about 21.4 percent of votes counted (1.2 million) at 4:18 a.m. Israel time, Ynet reported Likud at 23.4 percent, Yashar! 15.7 percent and B'Yachad (Bennett–Lapid) 8.5 percent, with Ra'am at 3.2 percent, just under the 3.25 percent threshold. Early counts are not representative of the final result. Arutz Sheva projected 65 seats for the current coalition from an 11.1 percent count; this is a partisan outlet's projection and is unconfirmed. On Polymarket at about 4:30 a.m. Israel time, Netanyahu was at 53.5 percent to be the next prime minister (up 26 points in a day) and Eisenkot at 22 percent (down 33 points). Official results are due by 4 November.

Israeli data centers

NewElectricity Authority's full proposal for data centers: 1,000 MW until 2035, 200 MW per site, and guarantees of up to NIS 400 million

The proposal caps each site at 200 MW and forbids splitting a site into smaller ones to get around the cap. It sets 1,000 MW in total for new data centers until 2035, plus a 300 MW reserve for data centers serving local customers. Developers would post a guarantee of NIS 0.5 million per MW when they apply (up to NIS 100 million), rising to NIS 2 million per MW (up to NIS 400 million) once approvals are in. Quotas would go first-come, first-served to those meeting the legal thresholds; plans taking more than seven years get an automatic no, and all applications are due by 1 June 2028. On-site renewable power with storage is allowed but gives no priority, and priority rules (periphery, flexibility at peak hours, efficiency) are still undecided. The authority froze new data-center connection requests in July after requests totaling 27 GW, three times Israel's entire generating capacity, came in. Public comments are open until 17 November, so the final decision will likely fall to the next government. Mega Or, which had planned more than 1,000 MW in Hadera, filed a stock-exchange report on 26 October about the hearing (seen only as an Investing.com headline).

Non-Latin script digitization

NewIndia and the Bibliothèque nationale de France agree to digitize Indian manuscripts held in Paris and share OCR tools for Indian scripts

It was signed by Vivek Aggarwal for the ministry and Gilles Pécout for the BnF. It covers five areas: digitization, sharing on India's National Digital Repository, technology exchange, conservation, and research. It includes digitizing manuscripts of Indian origin held by the BnF and exchanging "OCR/HTR tools for Indian scripts" (software that reads printed and handwritten text).
