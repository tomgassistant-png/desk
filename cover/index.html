Israel election

NewFirst Likud–Yashar! unity talks end with no progress and a promise to keep talking; United Torah Judaism denies a breakthrough with Eisenkot's bloc; Kahana's election as speaker is set for Tuesday

Yashar! sent MK Chili Tropper and Yoav Horowitz, Eisenkot's senior adviser and a former chief of staff to Netanyahu; Likud sent faction chair Ofir Katz and lawyer Michael Rabello. Naftali Bennett's party, Beyachad, sent Shalom Shlomo, cabinet secretary in the 2021–2022 government. A short joint statement said the meeting was "businesslike" and that "the dialogue will continue." According to ynet, Likud's team said it rules out no one in the change bloc, including Yair Golan's Democrats, while Golan wrote on X that the road to change does not pass through unity talks with Netanyahu. Netanyahu visited the "unity tent" that Likud MK Elisha Madan set up outside the Knesset. United Torah Judaism chair Yaakov Asher said reports of "dramatic" progress and exchanges of drafts with his party are baseless (ynet; Arutz Sheva reporter Hezki Baruch on X). An Israel Hayom headline says people around Netanyahu told Eisenkot they are ready to drop Itamar Ben Gvir for unity and that Likud denies it (headline only; the article could not be opened). ynet's analysis says the change bloc has put its bill for a state commission of inquiry into 7 October on hold, plans to start on Wednesday on bills to repeal the judicial-overhaul laws, and will set up fewer temporary committees than planned after the Knesset's legal adviser limited them. The Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee under Avigdor Liberman and the Finance Committee under Liran Avishar Ben-Horin (Beyachad) are due to meet for the first time on Monday, 16 November, and the vote making Matan Kahana (Yashar!) speaker is set for Tuesday, 17 November; Likud will not field a candidate, ynet reported. No change to the official results and no Likud appeal filing was reported.

Lebanon, Syria and the Druze

NewUS envoy Waltz begins a three-day visit to Beirut with options for a force to follow UNIFIL; Lebanon wants UNIFIL extended for at least six months

LBCI reported that Waltz is expected to present several options, including a model like the Multinational Force and Observers in Sinai, which works outside the UN, and a civilian monitoring arrangement; Lebanon will listen without committing in advance. LBCI says Lebanon backs a French proposal to keep UNIFIL and, if that fails, is interested in a French–Italian proposal for a European force with UN approval rather than a UN mandate, while Washington treats extending UNIFIL as off the table. Ministerial sources quoted by Asharq Al-Awsat (via Al-Sadara News) say President Aoun, Speaker Berri and Prime Minister Salam have agreed on a common approach built on Resolution 1701, would prefer a UNIFIL extension of at least six months, and are cool to a civilian or Sinai-style mission (unnamed sources). No new Israeli reaction to the EU mission (EUSM) and no new contributing country was found.

France presidential election

NewOdoxa: Teddy Riner's popularity falls 11 points to 78% after his candidacy, and 61% say it is bad for French politics; Philippe accepts Larcher's invitation but still refuses a primary

In the same poll, 82% said his candidacy surprised them, 59% said it is not good for Riner, 61% said it is not good for French political life, and 57% oppose top athletes running for office. Édouard Philippe accepted Senate President Gérard Larcher's invitation to a discussion among the candidates of the right and center but still refuses a primary (Le Monde headline and summary; Le Parisien quotes him: "If it is to talk about a primary again, I don't see the point"). Le Monde also published its annual "Fractures françaises" survey by Ipsos BVA with the Cevipof, the Fondation Jean Jaurès and the Institut Montaigne, which describes an angrier country, a sharp return of the left–right divide and a presidential election that is no longer seen as a solution (summary only; the article could not be opened). Green leader Marine Tondelier said on franceinfo that "all those who want to bring us down support Marine Le Pen," citing support for her from Donald Trump and Elon Musk. On Polymarket at about 4:25 AM Israel time on 16 November, Le Pen was at 46.5% (47.35% the day before), Lisnard 16%, Mélenchon 13.5%, Christine Lagarde 7.7%, Philippe 6.5% and Riner about 2%; betting odds are not polls.

Quebec

NewFirst Carney–St-Pierre Plamondon meeting in Phnom Penh is friendly: federal support on homelessness, backing for a 2027 Francophonie economic summit in Quebec, and immigration and the constitution left for later

The meeting, planned for 30 minutes, lasted about 45 (Radio-Canada). St-Pierre Plamondon called it "very agreeable, very funny" and said it produced "immediate results": federal support for Quebec's fight against homelessness, support for a Francophonie economic summit in Quebec in 2027, and a channel for more frequent talks on tariff disputes with the United States; the two exchanged personal phone numbers (Radio-Canada, CBC). Immigration, the Quebec–Toronto high-speed rail line and Quebec's constitutional future were put off to later meetings. Asked whether he regrets his confrontational tone toward Ottawa, the premier said he will "never regret defending Quebec's interests." The vote for the Francophonie's secretary-general, in which Quebec said it may vote apart from Canada, is due on Monday, 16 November.

UK breakup politics

NewNorthern Ireland reacts to Harris's Irish unity letter: unionists reject it as "political suicide" and "fairy-tale politics," Alliance will engage "without prejudice," the SDLP welcomes it

DUP leader Gavin Robinson said Harris should instead answer the Independent Commission for Reconciliation and Information Recovery's questions about the Irish state's role in Troubles deaths, and the TUV's Jim Allister told Dublin to "butt out." SDLP leader Claire Hanna welcomed the effort, and Alliance leader Naomi Long told the BBC her party will take part "without prejudice to how we might decide the constitutional question." Fine Gael's plan proposes a junior minister for North–South relations and a sovereign wealth fund; a paper by an adviser to Harris puts the cost to the Irish exchequer at up to €25 billion (the BBC gives €3 billion to €25 billion for the first year). In Wales, Welsh Labour leader Ken Skates told the BBC the party still wants Westminster to hand over policing and the Crown Estate, a day after saying it had focused too much on new powers.
