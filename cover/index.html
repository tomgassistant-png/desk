Lebanon

NewTampa talks end their first day without agreement; Israel wants a joint operations room to verify pilot zones, Lebanon objects

The Lebanese and Israeli military delegations met for about six hours at US Central Command in Tampa on 20 October without reaching agreement, and resume on Wednesday, 21 October (Anadolu; Annahar). Lebanon sent three officers instead of the six first planned. According to Annahar and Anadolu, it asked for an end to demolitions and firing, an organized Israeli withdrawal, new 'model' areas cleared of weapons, the release of prisoners, the return of residents and access for the olive harvest. Israel wants a direct operations room with the Lebanese army to verify that the pilot zones are clear, which Lebanon rejects. The villages discussed for the pilot are Froun, Sarifa and Zawtar al-Sharqiya, possibly extended to near Tallat Ali Taher. A US official told the Times of Israel the meeting is at working level, without the two ambassadors.

Quebec

NewSt-Pierre Plamondon's cabinet is sworn in today: about 20 ministers, a minister for the independence process, and Paradis as chief negotiator

The Parti Québécois cabinet is sworn in on Wednesday, 21 October, at 1:30 p.m. Quebec time (8:30 p.m. Israel time), without the lieutenant governor, Manon Jeannotte, who was not invited and will sign the appointment acts in her office (Le Devoir; Radio-Canada). The cabinet is a reduced one of about 20 ministers, against 28 under Fréchette, and includes a 'minister responsible for the independence process'; Pascal Paradis is named Quebec's chief negotiator. The government's 100-day priorities are a special budget for homelessness, a lower gas tax, a secretariat for government efficiency and regulatory relief, and lower immigration levels. PQ members meet former premier Pauline Marois and Jean Royer on Thursday, and the National Assembly session opens in late November.

Greenland

NewEU adopts its Arctic strategy: a direct cable from Greenland to Europe, €200 million for Greenland, and shared icebreakers

The European Commission and High Representative Kaja Kallas adopted 'Europe's Northern Neighbourhood – A New Strategy for the Arctic' on 20 October, with four priorities: security and resilience, climate, prosperity, and people (European Commission; EEAS). Its measures include an Arctic Cable Hub, possibly a first step toward a maritime security hub; wider Copernicus and Galileo satellite coverage; joint purchase and pooling of icebreakers; military mobility; €260 million from the European Defence Fund; a Pan-Arctic Cable that links Greenland directly to Europe; Arctic coverage for the IRIS² satellite network; €200 million for Greenland; and the Rail Nordica project. Asked why there is no new budget, Kallas named the satellite upgrades as the concrete step within 12 months and said the EU is open to working with the United States 'if they so wish.' Greenland's broadcaster KNR notes that the EU has already put €107 million into the Greenland cable, and Commission President von der Leyen presented the strategy as a response to Russia. Greenland's reaction was not found in a second source.

SpaceX, xAI and Cursor

NewSpaceX's third quarter: revenue up 161 percent to $14.6 billion and a $4.4 billion profit, driven by AI; Cursor added $608 million

SpaceX reported third-quarter revenue of $14.575 billion on 20 October, up 161 percent from $5.576 billion a year earlier and from $7.814 billion in the second quarter, well above the LSEG analyst estimate of $12.72 billion (SEC filing; Reuters). Net income was $4.404 billion, against a loss of $1.054 billion a year earlier; operating income was $2.791 billion and adjusted EBITDA $8.809 billion. Diluted GAAP earnings were 35 cents a share; Reuters, using adjusted figures, reports 45 cents against an expected 13 cents. By segment: AI (the 'SI' segment, which includes xAI and Cursor) brought in $7.560 billion, up 195 percent from the second quarter and 830 percent from a year earlier, with operating income of $431 million after $1.2 billion of share-based pay tied to the Cursor purchase; Connectivity (Starlink) $5.248 billion; Space $1.767 billion. Capital spending was $19.593 billion, of which $16.444 billion went to AI. Starlink has 13.6 million consumer subscribers paying an average of $69 a month, and the company has 1.7 gigawatts of nameplate computing capacity as Colossus II is built. The Cursor deal closed on 14 August at an implied equity value of $60 billion (GAAP consideration $42.5 billion), and Cursor contributed $608 million of revenue through 30 September. The release also names Starship Flight 14 as the first revenue-earning orbital flight (26 Starlink V3 satellites), a planned Starbase in Louisiana with a first launch targeted for 2029, and an 800 MHz spectrum deal signed on 8 October, and it mentions Grok Bot. Yahoo Finance reports a backlog of $63 billion, $42 billion of it expected within a year.

Trenton

NewCity breaks ground on the restoration of the Carver Center, the 1922 hall where Ella Fitzgerald and Duke Ellington played

The City of Trenton broke ground on 20 October on the first phase of restoring the Carver Center at 40 Fowler Street: a contract of about $2.53 million for the roof and exterior, part of a restoration expected to cost about $8 million (Trenton Journal, from a city release; InsiderNJ). The building was put up in 1922 by the Sunlight Elks Lodge and renamed for George Washington Carver by the YMCA in 1944; Ella Fitzgerald, Count Basie and Duke Ellington performed there. The city bought it in 2021. Government role: this is a public project, and the first phase is paid for with federal Community Development Block Grant money.
