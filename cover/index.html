Israel election

NewFinal polls before Tuesday's vote: Yashar 23, Likud 21 in both Israel Hayom and the Times of Israel; neither bloc near 61

The final polls before the 27 October vote put Eisenkot's Yashar first in both. Israel Hayom's final Kantar poll (21–22 October, 1,110 respondents, margin of error 3 points): Yashar 23 seats, Likud 21, Bennett's B'Yachad 14 (up), Yisrael Beytenu 7 (down) and the Reservists (Hendel and Zelekha) 4, with Winter's party below the threshold; the Netanyahu bloc has 50 seats. The final Times of Israel/Zman Yisrael poll, published on 22 October, has the same top two: Yashar 23 (three fewer than a week earlier), Likud 21, B'Yachad 11, Yisrael Beytenu 9, the Joint List 9, Shas, United Torah Judaism, the Democrats and Otzma Yehudit 8 each, Religious Zionism 6, Ra'am 5 and the Reservists 4; each Jewish-led bloc gets 51. Its fieldwork dates are given as 14–15 October (500 respondents, margin of error 4.4 points), which is earlier than its publication suggests. In that poll almost 60 percent said Netanyahu should leave politics if he fails to form a government, against 29 percent, and 87 percent said their vote is final. The Israel Hayom figures are confirmed by the Israel 2026 poll tracker and JFeed.

Brazil

NewDatafolha three days before the runoff: Flávio 47, Lula 46, a 50–50 tie in valid votes; Lula's approval edges ahead

Datafolha's runoff poll, published on 22 October (2,154 voters interviewed 20–22 October, margin of error 2 points, registered as BR-09709/2026), has Flávio Bolsonaro at 47 percent and Lula at 46, against 47 to 45 a week earlier; in valid votes they are tied at 50 each (g1; Reuters). Flávio's rejection rose to 46 percent from 44, and Lula's fell to 45. Approval of Lula's government is 49 percent against 48 percent disapproval, while 53 percent of voters expect Flávio to win (g1; Exame). Polymarket has Flávio at about 76.5 percent, down from about 79. Datafolha and Quaest publish their final polls on Saturday, 24 October, the day before the vote.

France presidential election

NewLecornu's meeting with the parties leaves the left angry; only union marches allowed on Saturday, with Lyon and Marseille marches banned

Prime Minister Lecornu received representatives of 28 parties at Matignon on 22 October to discuss public order. The left came out angry: the Greens' Marine Tondelier spoke of 'a lot of anger,' LFI's Manuel Bompard of lost time, the Communist Fabien Roussel said the causes, not the symptoms, must be treated, and Mélenchon dismissed the meeting as 'chatter' (Le Monde; France 24). Bruno Retailleau (LR) left early and called for a state of emergency and for postponing the election dates; Lecornu ruled out a state of emergency for now. The RN's Louis Aliot said he was 'reassured.' After a meeting with the intelligence services at the Élysée, the government decided that only marches organized by unions may go ahead on Saturday, 24 October: marches are banned in Lyon and Marseille, and Paris has a single route from Bastille to Nation. Police numbers will be 20 percent above last week, with armored vehicles and Sentinelle soldiers; Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin said 217 people have been jailed out of 2,700 taken into custody. The RN holds its congress in Orléans this weekend, where 20,000 to 30,000 counter-protesters are expected on Saturday and about 3,000 police will be deployed; Sunday's march there is canceled. The Orléans figures come from French press reports and are unconfirmed by the prefecture.

NewGrok Bot changelog jumps to version 0.75.2: editable Pages, video creation, Shop Pay and X Money payments, and more connectors

Trenton

NewSignal 22, the volunteer canteen that feeds Trenton firefighters and police at emergencies, marks 85 years

The Signal 22 Association, a volunteer group that brings food and drinks to firefighters and police at fires and other long emergencies, marked its 85th anniversary on 17 October. It was founded in 1941, according to its program, and is based at 1005 South Clinton Avenue. 'We are small, but we are mighty!' said its president, Peg Beyrouty. The details come from a single local report (Peterson's Breaking News of Trenton) and are unconfirmed by a second source. No reusable photo was found; the report has photos of the event.
