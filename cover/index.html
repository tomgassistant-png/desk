Israel election

NewBank of Israel rejects Netanyahu's idea of using cryptocurrency to get around sanctions; Netanyahu does not back down

In an interview with Israel's Economy Channel, Netanyahu said Israel must prepare a financial system that can work around possible international banking sanctions. Asked whether blockchain was the planned alternative, he said it was "one of them, certainly," and that he had already asked for a plan. On Saturday evening the Bank of Israel answered that "cryptocurrencies cannot serve as a means to circumvent sanctions" and that the right course is to keep economic ties with Israel's main trading partners and prevent sanctions, which it called the responsibility of the political leadership (Globes; Ynet). On Channel 14 the same evening, Netanyahu did not withdraw the idea: crypto is "not the sole method, and it is not certain that it will actually be used," and "I thank the Bank of Israel for their advice, but I do what I think is right" (Globes; Ynet). Ynet reports that officials in the economic ministries, and senior figures in the Prime Minister's Office, said they knew of no team working on such a plan (single source, unconfirmed). Former bank supervisor Yair Avidan and former Finance Ministry director-general Yarom Ariav criticized the idea (Ynet). Eisenkot called it a threat to national security (Times of Israel headline).

France presidential election

NewTrappes and La Verrière: a gunman kills one man and wounds five, including a 12-year-old girl in a headscarf; Macron calls it a "racist terrorist attack"

The attack lasted about 15 minutes. A 26-year-old man was killed and five people were wounded, among them a 12-year-old girl wearing a headscarf, whose condition has stabilized (franceinfo; France 24). The suspect, Kévin T., 39, of Plaisir, was arrested at Coignières within minutes; a submachine gun was found in his car, and his apartment had been booby-trapped with gas bottles (franceinfo; France 24). AFP reports police found Hitler's Mein Kampf in his home and that his detention was extended Saturday (Times of Israel, via AFP). Macron called it a "racist terrorist attack" (Times of Israel; BBC). The attack is now part of the campaign: at Saturday's anti-RN march in Orléans, Trappes mayor Ali Rabeh said "there is the camp of the Republic and the camp of the racists," and Socialist leader Olivier Faure said the RN bears no direct responsibility but is responsible for a "harmful climate" (RFI).

Lebanon

NewUS cuts embassy staff in Beirut and three other capitals over Iranian and Houthi threats; envoy Barrack says a US strike on Iran "this weekend" is possible

The cuts follow a memo from Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to Secretary of State Rubio saying Iran has "substantially increased efforts to target U.S. personnel"; US officials deny the drawdowns are tied to any new US or Israeli strikes on Iran (Naharnet, citing AP; Times of Israel headline). Tom Barrack, the US ambassador to Turkey, told podcaster Mario Nawfal that a strike "this weekend" was "absolutely" possible, but that the aim would not be regime change; Trump said he knew nothing about it (Naharnet, citing AFP; Times of Israel headline). Quebec

NewSt-Pierre Plamondon will skip Monday's first Council of the Federation meeting with Indigenous chiefs in 21 years

The meeting of the premiers, Prime Minister Mark Carney, Assembly of First Nations National Chief Cindy Woodhouse Nepinak and other chiefs opens Monday in Ottawa, after a traditional ceremony on Sunday. St-Pierre Plamondon told The Canadian Press that "our idea of nation-to-nation relations is that the Quebec government meets the Indigenous nations on its territory," that he will first meet Verreault-Paul of the Assembly of First Nations Quebec-Labrador and several nations before Christmas, and that this "does not rule out our participation in the future." Alberta's Danielle Smith will also be absent (Le Devoir; Le Quotidien, both from The Canadian Press).

SpaceX, xAI and Cursor

NewCursor launches Computer Use: agents can click and type in any desktop app, including the iOS Simulator

Cursor said agents can now click and type across any app on your computer, not just the browser, for example the iOS Simulator; they use Computer Use on their own when a task needs it, or you can ask for it by mentioning Computer Use in a prompt, and you get it by updating Cursor (Cursor's X posts, as reported by AgentRiot). Cursor's changelog page still shows its newest entry as 13 October, so the feature is not yet in the release notes, and Cursor has not said which operating systems the desktop version supports, what permissions it asks for, whether the agent asks before taking control, or what it costs (AgentRiot, single source for the gaps). Cursor's documentation already describes computer use for self-hosted workers on macOS and Linux: it is an explicit opt-in (agent worker --computer-use start), macOS needs Accessibility and Screen Recording permission for a helper app, and enterprise admins can switch it off (Cursor docs).
