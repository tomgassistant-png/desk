France presidential election

NewElabe: Philippe, Glucksmann and Mélenchon level at about 15 to 16 percent behind Le Pen

An Elabe poll for BFMTV and La Tribune Dimanche, taken online from 14 to 16 October among 1,701 people (1,533 registered voters) and published on 17 October, puts Marine Le Pen at 31 to 33.5 percent in the first round, down 1 to 1.5 points since Elabe's August poll. Behind her three candidates are almost level: Édouard Philippe at 15.5 to 16.5 percent when he is the only centrist, Raphaël Glucksmann at 15 to 16.5 (up 2.5 to 4 points) and Jean-Luc Mélenchon at 15 to 16 (up 1 to 2). Gabriel Attal is at 9.5 to 10.5, Bruno Retailleau at 6.5 to 9.5 and David Lisnard at 5.5 to 6.5; if both Philippe and Attal run, Philippe falls to 13.5 and Attal to 4.5. Natacha Polony, tested for the first time in one scenario, has 1.5 percent. Elabe says 46 percent of those who name a candidate could still change their minds. In the second round Le Pen beats Philippe 52.5 to 47.5 (unchanged since August), Glucksmann 53.5 to 46.5 (57 to 43 in August) and Mélenchon 69 to 31. The margin of error is 1.2 to 2.8 points. The fieldwork ended the day before the violence of 17 October, so the poll does not measure its effect. On Polymarket (about $152 million traded) Le Pen was at about 47 percent on the morning of 18 October, Philippe 17.5, Lisnard 11.5, Mélenchon 10.5 and Glucksmann about 4; this is no material change.

Israel election

NewNetanyahu: "We're tied"; Bennett and Liberman name their first laws; Eisenkot gives an 18-month Gaza pledge

Ten days before the 27 October election, Prime Minister Netanyahu told i24NEWS on 17 October that the race is "tied" and urged his voters to turn out, warning that Iran, Hezbollah and Hamas would try to interfere, including by "creating security incidents." Naftali Bennett said a new government would first vote to set up a state commission of inquiry into 7 October, then pass an eight-year term limit for prime ministers and a draft law. Avigdor Liberman wrote that the first steps should be to replace Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana and pass, in three readings, a law barring a defendant from forming a government. Yair Lapid said on Channel 12 that he does not trust Netanyahu on security and that no intelligence justifies preventing the election. Gadi Eisenkot pledged on X that Hamas will not rule Gaza within 18 months of his taking office. No new seat poll was published on Saturday; the Globes average of 17 October gives Likud 22, Yashar 21, Beyachad 12 and the Democrats 9, with the coalition at 54, the opposition parties at 49 and the Arab parties at 13. On Polymarket (about $47.5 million traded) Eisenkot rose to about 54 percent on the morning of 18 October, from about 50 a day earlier; Netanyahu was at about 27.5 and Bennett at 13.5.

Lebanon

NewLebanon will press for a halt to Israeli strikes at the Tampa talks on 20 October; Qatar pledges a solar plant

The Lebanese presidency said on 17 October that its military delegation will demand a halt to Israeli attacks at the US–Lebanon–Israel military meeting at US Central Command in Tampa on 20 October. The meeting is to cover ceasefire arrangements, the deployment of the Lebanese army in the south and verification. Al-Mayadeen, quoted by Ynet, said the talks would run from 20 to 22 October without political officials and that no date has been set for the eighth round of negotiations. On 16 October Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, visited Beirut and announced a solar power plant to be built by QatarEnergy and TotalEnergies; Prime Minister Nawaf Salam said it would cost the Lebanese state nothing. The Qatari prime minister said Qatar is working with the United States to prepare reconstruction in the south.

Brazil

NewPoderData ties the runoff at 50–50; Gerp has Flávio 52 to 48; Polymarket back near 80 percent

Two more runoff polls were published on 16 October: PoderData/Aya (3,000 telephone interviews, 13–15 October, margin 1.8 points) has Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro tied at 50 percent of valid votes. Gerp (2,400 interviews, 14–16 October, margin 2 points) gives Flávio 52 percent of valid votes to Lula's 48, and 50 to 45 in total votes. Poder360's count on 17 October found Flávio numerically ahead in 11 of the 13 latest polls, with Lula ahead only in Vox Brasil; most results are within the margin of error. On Polymarket (about $186 million traded) Flávio rose to about 80 percent on the morning of 18 October, from about 78 a day earlier. The televised runoff debate is on Sunday evening, 18 October (2 a.m. Monday Israel time). The runoff is on 25 October.

UK breakup politics

NewSwinney: "The referendum campaign on Scottish independence has already begun"

In his speech to the SNP conference in Aberdeen on 17 October, First Minister John Swinney said the independence referendum campaign "has already begun" and launched a "Choose Scotland" campaign; his spokesman said it starts with £100,000. The National says the campaign plans to survey 1.5 million Scots. Swinney cited a Survation poll for the Foundation for Reform (FoRE) with 54 percent for independence; other polls this week were mixed, with YouGov and More in Common showing a majority for staying in the United Kingdom. Referring to Manchester, he said, "Some think we should just be like Manchester ... Scotland is no city." He promised public control of buses before 2031, extension of the £2 bus fare cap to Aberdeen from April, a misogyny bill by June 2027 and a £30 million "Scotland Together" fund. Labour's Jackie Baillie called the speech "thin and threadbare"; Conservative leader Russell Findlay also criticized it. The SNP has about 51,000 members.

Trenton

NewCouncil moves to buy the empty Lafayette Yard hotel with a $4 million state grant and hand it to a private redeveloper for $1

Trenton City Council voted on 6 October to apply for a $4 million New Jersey Economic Development Authority grant to buy the former Lafayette Yard Marriott at 1 West Lafayette Street, the city's only downtown hotel, empty since the state closed it for safety violations in 2017. Mayor Reed Gusciora told the Trentonian that the city would buy the building from its current owner with the grant and sell it for $1 to the designated redeveloper, Tetrabilt of South Carolina, which he said has committed to spend $20 million to reopen it, with long-term housing on the top two floors and a hotel below. The grant was first announced in November 2024; the mayor blamed the delay on procedural requirements, and the Trenton Journal has reported a lawsuit by a former owner's partners who still claim the property. Central Jersey Media reported on 14 October that the plan is moving forward; final state approval is still needed. The money for the purchase is public; the renovation and operation would be private.

Colombia and Israel

NewIsrael's ambassador: Colombia is considering joining the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance

Israel's ambassador to Colombia, Vivian Aisen, told The Media Line that Foreign Ministers Gideon Sa'ar and Omar Bula discussed a possible Colombian application to the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance during Bula's visit to Jerusalem. No formal application has been announced; the claim rests on the ambassador's account. Colombia adopted the alliance's working definition of antisemitism in 2022 but is not a member or observer. The report, published by the Jerusalem Post on 17 October, also says the two governments completed an exchange of diplomatic notes on 13 October restoring their 2020 free-trade agreement, and that, according to Israel's Foreign Ministry, Colombia is the tenth country with an embassy in Jerusalem and the fourth in Latin America. Bula also announced a national initiative against antisemitism, to be prepared with Colombia's Jewish communities and advice from the US State Department's antisemitism envoy.
