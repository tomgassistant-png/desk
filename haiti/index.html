Crushing the Serpent Under the Heel

Updated 2026-10-30 04:40 Israel time. Each item says who said it, where, when, and what comes with it. Nothing here is graded. Where sources differ, the item says "another source says" and the judgment is yours.

Summary

Twelve days after four Salvadoran aviation technicians went missing in Santo, El Salvador has put at least 2,000 troops in Haiti and expected about 2,500 by the end of Thursday, more than the entire Gang Suppression Force, according to the Miami Herald. On 29 Oct President Bukele spoke to Haitians in Creole: "This is not an operation against the Haitian people, nor an invasion." Jake Johnston of the Center for Economic and Policy Research says Bukele says El Salvador pays 90 percent of the cost; charters from Honduras and Ecuador, Colombian airlifts and at least one US military flight have carried the troops.

Who commands them is still not said. The Herald counts five security structures now at work: the Haitian police, the Haitian army, the force, a drone task force using contractors linked to Erik Prince, and the Salvadorans, most of whom have not had the force's human rights course, with no status-of-forces agreement signed. Romain Le Cour of GI-TOC calls the rules of engagement and command "opaque." The EU backed the force's extended mandate without naming El Salvador, and Kirikounews says, with no source named, that Chadian troops quarreled with the Salvadoran defense minister.

The push to crush the gangs is widening. Videos show the combined forces clearing barricades in Port-au-Prince and five Salvadoran helicopters at Jimaní on the Dominican border and over the Cul-de-Sac plain. In Santo, an anonymous resident says Chen Mechan is forcing youths to join and charging people to leave while drones strike. In the lower Artibonite, police and the force reportedly killed more than ten suspected Gran Grif members, a brigade member was arrested for ties to the gang, and a man presented as its leader taunted the Salvadorans on video.

Civilians are paying. IOM says 13,912 people fled Petite Rivière and Verrettes from 19 to 26 Oct as Gran Grif pressed its offensive; Doctors Without Borders has suspended mobile clinics; the Norwegian Refugee Council mourns two Haitian staff killed. Le Cour notes that no one has called for a safe corridor out of Santo.

Politics moves on beside the fighting. The electoral council set elections for 18 Jul and 4 Oct 2027, and sanctioned former Chamber president Garry Bodeau filed to run. Vectus has said nothing new. In Santo Domingo, Vinicio Castillo called Haiti's summons of the Dominican ambassador "a hostile act"; we found no word on how the meeting went. Jimmy Chérizier has stayed silent.

Gran Grif, Kokorat San Ras and Canaan

Gran Grif (led by Luckson Élan) has pressed an offensive on Petite Rivière since 19 Oct (Miami Herald); IOM counts 13,912 people displaced there and in Verrettes. On 29 Oct a video showed a man presented as its leader challenging the Salvadorans and the force in Gonaïves (@vargavila). From March 2026 they attacked dozens of localities in the lower Artibonite; Gran Grif was designated a terrorist group by the US with Viv Ansanm (UN Panel of Experts report, hosted on HaitiDocs, and State Department). source
