Crushing the Serpent Under the Heel

Updated 2026-10-26 05:00 Israel time. Each item says who said it, where, when, and what comes with it. Nothing here is graded. Where sources differ, the item says "another source says" and the judgment is yours.

Summary

Eight days after four Salvadoran aviation technicians went missing on 18 October in Santo, Croix-des-Bouquets, nobody has said for certain whether the burned remains handed over on Saturday are theirs. On Sunday the Gang Suppression Force said it received the remains through a humanitarian group but "is not in a position to confirm" who they are. President Nayib Bukele still says: "They are not our men." The New York Times and the Haitian Times say the remains were recovered by Hero Client Rescue, a US-funded medical evacuation group led by former State Department security official Scott Reece. The Miami Herald says they came with two military badges and a shirt.

A side quarrel grew over the Chadian ID card Bukele posted as found among the remains. The force says the card was lost during the search and its owner is with his unit; AyiboPost says he was wounded but is alive. Jake Johnston of the Center for Economic and Policy Research (CEPR) wrote: "I can't imagine the GSF is thrilled about this." Bukele told an account that had said he called the soldier dead: "A little reading comprehension next time wouldn't hurt."

The biggest new item is from the gangs themselves. Wilson Joseph, known as Lanmò San Jou, who leads 400 Mawozo, posted a TikTok video Sunday evening that Haitian, Dominican and Salvadoran accounts read the same way: the four died in Chen Mechan's area, "by mistake," and he would have taken them alive and negotiated with Bukele. In a longer cut he holds a bottle he dedicates to Dessalines and, in one Spanish translation, calls for shooting foreigners. Journalist Thériel Thélus says Chen Mechan has a video of its own saying the Salvadorans fired first. Radio Télé Confiance says Lanmò San Jou has moved to Saut-d'Eau and the Chen Mechan leader is dug in at Marin 12; Thélus says he is in Lizon.

On the ground, the effort to crush the gangs went on. Radio Télé Confiance reports Task Force drones over fighting in Santo, kamikaze drone strikes in Bel-Air on Sunday night followed by clashes with Haitian police special units, and police moving into gang-held parts of Mirebalais. Gangs have blocked bridges at Shada and Croix-des-Missions and, the station says, are holding civilians as human shields.

Next door, Dominican President Luis Abinader says he has sent two more battalions and artillery to the southern border and will speak on Haiti today at 3 p.m. Dominican time. Listín Diario keeps asking how Dominican army and police ammunition reached 400 Mawozo. Kenya, which led the previous mission, says it stands "in solidarity with the GSF."

El Salvador's operation

  • Remains, 25 Oct: the Gang Suppression Force says it received, through a humanitarian group, remains "suspected" to be the four, but "is not in a position to confirm" it. Bukele says they are not his men. The remains, recovered by the US-funded group Hero Client Rescue (Scott Reece), came with two military badges and a shirt and were badly burned (Miami Herald, Haitian Times). Vant Bèf Info says identification continues using dental records.
  • The Chadian ID: Bukele posted a Chadian GSF soldier's ID as found among the remains. The GSF says the card was lost during the search and the soldier is with his unit; AyiboPost and Vant Bèf Info name him as Sossal Kamis Alhadj and say he was wounded but is alive. After Haití en Español wrote that Bukele had called the soldier dead and then corrected itself, Bukele answered: "A little reading comprehension next time wouldn't hurt." Jake Johnston: "I can't imagine the GSF is thrilled about this."
  • Lanmò San Jou's 25 Oct TikTok video says the four died in Chen Mechan's area "by mistake" and that he would have taken them alive and negotiated with Bukele (Rolfy Martínez, Le Patrimoine 509).   • Four Salvadoran aviation technicians serving with the Gang Suppression Force went missing on 18 Oct 2026 after their armored vehicle was attacked in Santo, Croix-des-Bouquets (Haitian Times; Salvadoran military via AP). Bukele says they are medical-evacuation personnel, not combatants.

The Gang Suppression Force (UN-authorized)

  • 25 Oct statement: received remains through "a third-party humanitarian organization"; cannot yet say they are the missing Salvadorans; the Chadian ID card belongs to a living GSF soldier; coordination with Haitian security forces "remains essential." Special representative Jack Christofides sent condolences to the families and to El Salvador (Haitian Times, EFE).
  • Size: over 2,000 troops from six countries (Miami Herald); another source (EFE) says more than 1,000, from El Salvador, Guatemala, Jamaica, Mongolia, Sri Lanka and Chad.
  • Kenya's foreign affairs principal secretary, Korir Sing'oei, says he is "deeply concerned" at the danger to GSF personnel: "We stand in solidarity with the GSF."
  • The Gang Suppression Force (GSF) is the UN-authorized force under Security Council resolution 2793 (2025). Its mandate was renewed in September 2026 for six months, through March 2027 (Haitian Times).
  • Its commander is Mongolian Maj. Gen. Erdenebat Batsuuri (Haitian Times). Contributing countries named by Haïti Liberté: Chad, El Salvador, Guatemala, Jamaica, Mongolia, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh. The UN calls it severely understrength; EFE says it has about 2,000 of a planned 5,500 from six countries.
  • In July a GSF patrol in Tabarre came under fire near an illegal checkpoint in Kraze Baryè territory; the GSF said three armed men were killed (Haitian Times, 19 Oct).
