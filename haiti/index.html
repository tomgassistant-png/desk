Crushing the Serpent Under the Heel

Updated 2026-10-25 05:00 Israel time. Each item says who said it, where, when, and what comes with it. Nothing here is graded. Where sources differ, the item says "another source says" and the judgment is yours.

Summary

On Saturday 24 October, burned human remains were handed over that some reporters linked to the four Salvadoran aviation technicians missing since 18 October in Santo, Croix-des-Bouquets. Jacqueline Charles of the Miami Herald reports that a group called HERO collected them at a drop site in Chen Mechan territory and gave them to the Gang Suppression Force, while saying it was "in no way claiming it is them." Frances Robles of the New York Times reported bodies "believed to be" the four. President Nayib Bukele answered within hours: "They are not our men." He says the remains are those of a child, a much taller man and someone wearing part of another army's uniform, found with identity cards and uniforms from other countries. Jake Johnston of the Center for Economic and Policy Research (CEPR) notes that nothing is official and that burned remains need an autopsy.

On the ground, Franklin Vargas reports the first clash between Salvadoran troops and gangs in the Cul-de-Sac plain, with at least one Salvadoran wounded; Latinar says four. El Demócrata says fighting is under way against 400 Mawozo in Croix-des-Bouquets, with Haitian police and the international force in Butte Boyer and Cité Doudoune. Premium Media posted photos of Salvadoran armored vehicles being loaded onto a US C-130J and video of Gang Suppression Force vehicles at Santo Domingo's airport that it says are bound for Port-au-Prince. Wilson Joseph, known as Lanmò San Jou, who leads 400 Mawozo, posted new video from his pickup with ammunition: "We have to fight just to stay alive."

Dominican President Luis Abinader says he spoke with Bukele about logistical and intelligence cooperation against the gangs, and that he has reinforced the border with soldiers, armored vehicles, drones and aircraft; he says the gangs have not come near it. Listín Diario's reporter in Jimaní saw more troops arrive while the Mal Paso crossing stayed open.

Johnston says the US military is giving the Salvadorans logistical support, and calls the mission "not a 'rescue' operation" but the US "again outsourcing to El Salvador." He says that on paper the troops serve under the Gang Suppression Force, but on the ground they join the Prince "task force," which he says is "staffed mainly with Salvadorans." That task force is the Haitian government's armed drone and helicopter unit, which AP and Human Rights Watch say is supported by private contractors from Erik Prince's company, Vectus Global.

Not everyone expects the effort to crush the gangs to work. Retired Col. Himmler Rébu, a former Haitian special forces commander, told EFE the Salvadorans "will not get results" because they do not know Haiti's ground.

El Salvador's operation

  • Four Salvadoran aviation technicians serving with the Gang Suppression Force went missing on 18 Oct 2026 after their armored vehicle was attacked in Santo, Croix-des-Bouquets (Haitian Times; Salvadoran military via AP). Bukele says they are medical-evacuation personnel, not combatants.
    Map: Croix-des-Bouquets (18.578°N, 72.230°W)
  • On 21 Oct Bukele announced a military operation to rescue them and warned the gangs in Haitian Creole that he could "finish" them as he did in El Salvador.
  • Remains: on 24 Oct a group called HERO handed burned remains, collected at a drop site in Chen Mechan territory, to the Gang Suppression Force, while saying it was "in no way claiming it is them" (Jacqueline Charles, Miami Herald). The New York Times (Frances Robles) reported bodies "believed to be" the four. Bukele: "They are not our men"; he says the remains are a child, a much taller man and someone in part of another army's uniform, with other countries' ID cards and uniforms. Jake Johnston notes there is no official confirmation and the remains need an autopsy.
  • First combat: Franklin Vargas reports the first clash in the Cul-de-Sac plain with at least one Salvadoran wounded; Latinar says four were wounded. El Demócrata reports fighting against 400 Mawozo in Croix-des-Bouquets.
    Map: Croix-des-Bouquets (18.578°N, 72.230°W)
  • Troop numbers differ by source; see the reference table at the bottom of the page. Johnston, from flight listings, counted charter flights from Washington and from El Salvador into Haiti on 23 October, one likely a US Air Force plane.
  • Law: El Salvador's Special Transitory Law for the participation of its armed forces in the Gang Suppression Force was approved 29 Sep, published 30 Sep, in force 2 Oct 2026, and authorizes participation until Oct 2027 (Diario El Mundo, El Salvador).
  • Equipment: Armapedia's and Premium Media's photos show Yagu armored vehicles loaded onto a US C-130J at Comalapa; Menoni's and Premium Media's videos show armored vehicles at Las Américas airport in the Dominican Republic, which Premium Media says are bound for Port-au-Prince. Rolfy Martínez posted video he says shows "P50 tanks" in Port-au-Prince (not yet reported by others).
    Map: Port-au-Prince (18.547°N, 72.340°W) (city center)
  • Dominican Republic: President Luis Abinader says he spoke with Bukele about logistical and intelligence cooperation against the gangs, and that he has reinforced the border with soldiers, armored vehicles, drones and aircraft. Listín Diario's reporter in Jimaní saw an army contingent arrive while the Mal Paso crossing stayed open.

The Gang Suppression Force (UN-authorized)

  • The Gang Suppression Force (GSF) is the UN-authorized force under Security Council resolution 2793 (2025). Its mandate was renewed in September 2026 for six months, through March 2027 (Haitian Times).
  • Its commander is Mongolian Maj. Gen. Erdenebat Batsuuri (Haitian Times). Contributing countries named by Haïti Liberté: Chad, El Salvador, Guatemala, Jamaica, Mongolia, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh. The UN calls it severely understrength; EFE says it has about 2,000 of a planned 5,500 from six countries.
  • In July a GSF patrol in Tabarre came under fire near an illegal checkpoint in Kraze Baryè territory; the GSF said three armed men were killed (Haitian Times, 19 Oct).
    Map: Tabarre (18.562°N, 72.251°W)

Haitian police and army

  • The Haitian National Police say an offensive began 18 Oct in the Cul-de-Sac plain, in Croix-des-Missions, Santo, Carrefour Marassa and the Dyo and Las Vegas corridors, with many gang members killed and no count given (EFE via Diario El Mundo).
    Map: Carrefour (18.535°N, 72.409°W)
  • El Demócrata (Dominican outlet) reports Haitian police and the international force operating in Butte Boyer and Cité Doudoune on 24 Oct.
  • Retired Col. Himmler Rébu, a former Haitian army special forces commander, told EFE the Salvadorans "will not get results" because they do not know Haiti's ground, history and politics.
  • Haiti's government has called the soldiers' release a priority and says it is coordinating with the GSF (Haitian Times).
  • Haiti's government postponed the elections planned for December indefinitely in early October (EFE via France 24).

United States

  • Ambassador Mike Waltz briefed the UN Security Council on 19 Oct and the US mission publicly told gang bosses the GSF "is coming for you."
  • Pimentel says Bukele was in Washington on 22 to 23 Oct to meet Trump and is asking for satellite intelligence, drones, armed helicopters and UN permission for offensive force. We have not found an official readout; the White House gallery page for a Trump-Bukele meeting that we opened is dated July 2025.
  • Jake Johnston (24 Oct) says the US military is giving logistical support to the Salvadoran deployment, and calls it "not a 'rescue' operation" but the US "again outsourcing to El Salvador… This is an end around." Premium Media's photos show Salvadoran armored vehicles loaded on a US C-130J.
  • The US designated Viv Ansanm and Gran Grif as terrorist organizations on 2 May 2025 (State Department).

Special forces and private contractors hired by Haiti's government

  • Haiti's government created a Task Force that uses armed drones and helicopters against gangs and is supported by private contractors from Vectus Global, the company of Erik Prince (AP and Human Rights Watch).
  • Human Rights Watch (10 Mar 2026): Haitian security forces and private contractors working with them carried out drone strikes; at least 1,243 people were killed in 141 operations between 1 Mar 2025 and 21 Jan 2026, including at least 43 adults reportedly not gang members and 17 children; 738 were injured.
  • Vectus Global's contract was reportedly extended by one year at an additional $52 million, with operations expanded into Bas-Artibonite (Le Placentin, 10 Oct 2026, and other reports; we have not seen the contract). Reuters reported in Aug 2025 that Prince planned to keep personnel in Haiti for 10 years; OPB reported nearly 200 personnel.
    Map: Artibonite (19.337°N, 72.492°W) (department, approximate center)
  • Jake Johnston (CEPR) wrote on 23 Oct: "Bukele and Erik Prince seem to be in charge of the ongoing military operations." That is his judgment; he gave no source for it.
  • Jake Johnston wrote on 24 Oct that on paper the Salvadoran troops are under the Gang Suppression Force, but on the ground they join the Prince "task force," which is "staffed mainly with Salvadorans." Not yet reported by others.
  • Haïti Liberté (Kim Ives) uses the word "mercenaries" for GSF members; that is the author's word, not ours.

The gangs: who they are, where they operate

Viv Ansanm ("Living Together")

The coalition of the main Port-au-Prince gangs, formed in 2024 per El País (one source dates it September 2023). Jimmy "Barbecue" Chérizier is a leading figure and its spokesman. Designated a terrorist organization by the US on 2 May 2025. The Miami Herald's analyst says it had been fragmenting, and that this incident may restore its cohesion. source

Map: Port-au-Prince (18.547°N, 72.340°W) (city center)
400 Mawozo

Led by Wilson Joseph, "Lanmò San Jou," operating in Croix-des-Bouquets and part of Viv Ansanm. He posted a video of ammunition and challenged Bukele (Anadolu). On 24 Oct new video showed him in a pickup with ammunition saying "We have to fight just to stay alive" (via HaitiInfoProj). El Demócrata reports fighting against the gang in Croix-des-Bouquets. source

Map: Croix-des-Bouquets (18.578°N, 72.230°W)
Chen Mechan ("Vicious Dog")

Part of Viv Ansanm. Haïti Liberté, relaying Chérizier, says its fighters ambushed the GSF members in Santo. Salazar names Claude Celestin as its head. Martínez says its base was destroyed (not yet reported by others). The burned remains handed over on 24 Oct were collected at a drop site in its territory (Miami Herald). source

Kraze Baryè

Controls territory in Tabarre where a GSF patrol came under fire in July (Haitian Times). source

Map: Tabarre (18.562°N, 72.251°W)
Gran Grif, Kokorat San Ras and Canaan

From March 2026 they attacked dozens of localities in the lower Artibonite; Gran Grif was designated a terrorist group by the US with Viv Ansanm (UN Panel of Experts report, hosted on HaitiDocs, and State Department). source

Map: Artibonite (19.337°N, 72.492°W) (department, approximate center)
Krisla

Named by the Miami Herald's analyst as one of two leaders who spoke publicly this week. source
