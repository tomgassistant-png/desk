The Skinwalker Ranch Controversy

A short explainer on the Utah ranch at the center of modern UFO research, why it matters, and why many people doubt it. Names link to the UFO Researcher Network map.

Last updated Oct 30, 2026 (Jerusalem time). Facts marked unconfirmed were not checked against a second source.

1. The Shermans and Bigelow (1994–2004)

Terry and Gwen Sherman bought a 480-acre ranch in Utah's Uintah Basin in 1994. In June 1996 the Deseret News reported their story: UFOs, strange lights, crop-circle-like marks, and cattle that vanished or were found mutilated. A large wolf that bullets could not stop is part of the lore.

Within about three months, Las Vegas businessman Robert Bigelow bought the ranch for about $200,000 and put his National Institute for Discovery Science (NIDS) there, with round-the-clock watch. Biochemist Colm Kelleher led the work. NIDS closed in 2004. Kelleher and reporter George Knapp wrote Hunt for the Skinwalker (2005).

2. The Pentagon contract (2008–2010)

Defense Intelligence Agency official James Lacatski read the book, visited the ranch in 2007, and said he saw something there. With help from Sen. Harry Reid, the DIA in 2008 gave Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS) a contract of about $22 million for the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP). The ranch became a study site. Funding ended around 2010. The program became public after The New York Times story in December 2017.

In 2021 Lacatski, Kelleher and Knapp told the story in Skinwalkers at the Pentagon.

3. Fugal and the TV show (2016–now)

Bigelow sold the ranch in 2016 to Adamantium Real Estate, owned by Utah real estate investor Brandon Fugal, who kept his name secret until 2020. The price is reported as about $4.5 million by one source and about $500,000 by others unconfirmed. In November 2020 the History Channel launched The Secret of Skinwalker Ranch, which follows Fugal's team with sensors, drones and radar. A spinoff, Beyond Skinwalker Ranch, began in 2023.

4. The criticisms

  • No peer-reviewed data. After decades of watching, no physical evidence has been published in a scientific journal. Even History's own article says NIDS found no meaningful physical proof.
  • Bigelow's two roles. Bigelow owned the ranch and his company got the Pentagon money to study it, so the government was paying him to study his own property.
  • A circle that vouches for itself. The main accounts come from the same few people (Bigelow, Kelleher, Knapp, Lacatski), who cite and back each other. See the Bigelow cluster on the network map.
  • Money and entertainment. Books, tours, merchandise and a long-running cable show all depend on the mystery staying alive. TV editing favors cliffhangers over clear results.
  • Using Navajo folklore. The "skinwalker" is a figure from Navajo belief, and many Navajo people consider it a subject not to be spoken of lightly. Critics say the name was borrowed for branding, even though the land is near the Ute reservation, not Navajo land.

5. Key people

  • Robert Bigelow – owner 1996–2016, funded NIDS and BAASS.
  • Colm Kelleher – led NIDS fieldwork, coauthor of both books.
  • George Knapp – Las Vegas reporter, coauthor of both books.
  • James Lacatski – DIA official behind AAWSAP.
  • Brandon Fugal – owner since 2016, the face of the TV show.
  • Terry and Gwen Sherman – owners 1994–1996, first reported the events.
  • Harry Reid – senator who pushed the funding.
